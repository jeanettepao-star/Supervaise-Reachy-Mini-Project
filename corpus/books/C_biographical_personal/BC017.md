# Justice and Faith -- Ch. 10: Offering to the Father

Chapter 10 of 'Justice and Faith' is CJP's Holy Friday reflection on Luke 23:46 ('Father, into your hands, I commend my spirit'), the last words of Jesus on the cross, framed as a paradigm of perfect obedience that every disciple is invited to imitate in daily work and decisions. He opens with a vignette of his six-year-old grandson Miggy, who told him he earned a preschool outstanding-pupil award at Southridge School by studying hard, watching no TV except during holidays, waking early, and doing his homework every day -- then placed the certificate at the home altar, a child's complete sacrifice-and-offering. CJP then catalogues the plural Christian imitations of Christ -- contemplative hermits, priests and nuns, missionaries who 'sail the seven seas,' even flagellants -- before turning to the most ordinary form, citing Mother Teresa: 'We are all called to be Christ to others so that others may become Christ to still many others.' He develops this with a litany of ordinary vocations: a student offering his grades, a policeman patrolling honestly, a housewife preparing meals lovingly, a husband working faithfully, a businessman selling quality at reasonable prices, a government functionary discharging duties in integrity -- each lifts work to the Lord as Christ did on the cross. Augustine's 'our hearts are restless until they rest in God' anchors a walking-wounded image of humanity; CJP supplies his own confession -- a restless young man chasing fame, fortune, power, position and possession that produced no peace and no fulfillment ('Who am I? Why was I born? Where am I headed for?'). Only when he committed to the Father, acknowledged the Holy Spirit, and proclaimed Jesus as 'Lord and Savior, Healer and Friend' did the dual-citizenship orientation arrive: a resident of earth, truly a citizen of heaven. He closes with a dedicatory pledge in oath form -- 'I hold my present office -- as indeed my life and my time -- by God's grace' -- and a closing prayer: 'O, Heavenly Father, into Your hands we commend our work, our minds, our lives and our spirits. Amen.'

Six-year-old grandson Miggy at Southridge School -- 'I studied hard, did not watch television except during holidays, woke up early during school days, and did my homework every day' -- then places the certificate and award at the home altar; sacrifice and offering as a single childlike act

### Anecdote 2

Catalogue of imitations of Christ -- hermits in permanent isolation; men and women of the cloth leaving family; missionaries 'sail the seven seas'; flagellants 'beat their backs with glass-imbedded whips or even get themselves physically nailed to the cross during Holy Week'

### Anecdote 3

Mother Teresa's 'be Christ to others' as the lay-spirituality formula

### Anecdote 4

Ordinary-vocations litany -- student, policeman, housewife, husband, businessman, government functionary -- each named as a Christ-bearing role

Conversion turning point -- committing to the Father, acknowledging the Holy Spirit, proclaiming Jesus as 'Lord and Savior, Healer and Friend' -- only then 'I found the meaning and purpose of life'
