# A Centenary of Justice -- Appendix D: MPGR (a tribute to Justice Minerva P. Gonzaga-Reyes)

Appendix D of 'A Centenary of Justice' reproduces CJP's tribute 'MPGR,' delivered September 24, 2001 at the Manila Hotel retirement program for Justice Minerva Perez Gonzaga-Reyes, his colleague of thirty-two months in the Court's Third Division (joined by her husband 'Johnny' and children Alda, Edwin Romualdo and Johann Michael). He distills his impression of her into four words. MODESTY -- not timidity but humility: she declined the Court's customary grand Session-Hall eulogy, where robed justices individually eulogize a retiree, for a modest family gathering, and even asked CJP not to praise her -- a caveat he gently overrode as a jurist bound to the truth, since 'her legacy' is a docket with 'zero backlog,' every ripe controversy decided and every case left current for her successor and 'the expectant and oftentimes demanding public.' PERSPICACITY -- a clear understanding acted on 'with delicacy and serenity'; she 'speaks rarely -- only when she feels her opinions would make a difference,' in 'cadenced, well thought out words' free of 'braggadocio or exhibitionism.' GENTILITY -- 'good manners and right conduct at all times,' never losing her temper, never using obscene words, 'always prim, proper and balanced.' And RECTITUDE -- integrity and uprightness, an 'unassailable reputation' for honesty and for 'not betraying friends or revealing secrets,' fighting 'not only for what is legally possible but... for what is ethical and moral,' leaning on 'deep spirituality' and a belief 'in the innate goodness of humans' and their capacity 'to rise above themselves.' He then reveals the device: the four words -- MODESTY, PERSPICACITY, GENTILITY, RECTITUDE -- spell MPGR, the initials of Minerva Perez Gonzaga-Reyes.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

The eulogy she refused: declining the Court's customary grand Session-Hall tribute (where robed justices individually eulogize a retiree), Justice Gonzaga-Reyes asked instead for a modest family get-together -- and even instructed CJP 'not to praise her,' a caveat he gently overrode as a jurist bound 'to the truth.'

### Anecdote 2

Zero backlog: CJP's proudest claim for her is that she left a docket with 'zero backlog,' having 'decided every ripe controversy assigned to her' -- the legacy she bequeathed to her successor and the public.

### Anecdote 3

The hidden acronym: after praising her MODESTY, PERSPICACITY, GENTILITY and RECTITUDE, CJP reveals the device -- the four words' initials, M-P-G-R, are her own (Minerva Perez Gonzaga-Reyes).

### Anecdote 4

Speaks rarely, but tellingly: he notes she 'never speaks out of turn' and offers her views only 'when she feels her opinions would make a difference,' in 'cadenced, well thought out words' free of 'braggadocio or exhibitionism.'
