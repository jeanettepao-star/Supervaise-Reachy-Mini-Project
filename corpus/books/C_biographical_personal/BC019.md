# Justice and Faith -- Appendix A: A Man of God and of the Law

Appendix A of 'Justice and Faith,' titled 'A Man of God and of the Law,' is the book-jacket-style biographical profile that organizes CJP's pre-bench identity around six topical headings -- Top-Notch Catholic Lay Leader, Well-Known Civic Leader, Former Business Leader, Popular Youth Leader, Outstanding Family Man, and Legal Philosophy -- and closes with five press blurbs from 1995-1997. The Catholic-lay spine names CJP's 1996 appointment by Pope John Paul II to the Pontifical Council for the Laity (the only Filipino among 30 international members), his national vice chairmanship of PPCRV, secretaryship of the Council of the Laity of the Philippines, servant-leadership of the Bukas Loob sa Diyos Covenant Community, lay delegation to PCP II (Jan-Feb 1991), and parish ministry as special minister of Holy Communion. The civic-and-business sections record CJP as founder of NUSP, president of FEU Central Student Organization, 'most illustrious knight' of Kappa Gamma Phi, president of the Rotary Club of Manila (1990-1991, the largest in Asia), president of Philippine Daily Inquirer (1991-1992), vice president of PCCI, international chairman of ASTA (first Asian to head it), president of the Organization for Tourism of the Philippines for ten years, governor of MAP for four, chief legal counsel to PPCRV, MAPSA, and the Liberal Party, and chair of the Multi-Sectoral Conference on the Administration of Justice (June 13 and October 17, 1992) that drew up the first-1,000-days program for President Ramos. The academic record -- FEU Bachelor of Laws cum laude (1960), Most Outstanding Student (1959), Associate in Arts 'With Highest Honors' (1956), ten years as a professor of law and political science at FEU/Assumption Convent/San Sebastian (1961-1970), and the Doctor of Laws honoris causa from the University of Iloilo (March 21, 1997, the C07 honoris-causa occasion) -- sits alongside the family section noting wife Elenita 'Leni' Carpio-Panganiban (AIM professor) and five children, all with master's degrees from leading universities abroad (Maria Elena, UC MBA, Baron Travel president; Jose Artemio III, UP music summa cum laude, Stanford MS 'with distinction'; Maria Jocelyn, Chicago MBA; Maria Theresa, Michigan MBA; Maria Evelyn, Harvard MPP). The Legal Philosophy section then states CJP's divestiture-on-elevation (resigning business and most civic posts on SC appointment, keeping only PCL, Rotary Manila, and the Bantayog ng mga Bayani Foundation), his two-year productivity ledger (230+ ponencias, 11 separate opinions, 140 extended resolutions, 675 minute resolutions), and the substantial-justice creed in his own words: 'Each case has its own flesh and blood ... ' and 'the real essence of justice does not emanate from patchwork legal technicality ... but from the spirit's gut consciousness of the dynamic role of law in the ultimate development of the social edifice.' The appendix closes with the Rotary inaugural pledge -- 'Upon this creed, LOVE GOD SERVE MAN, I shall be judged ... in my whole unworthy life here on earth' -- and a reception dossier of five press blurbs triangulating the man-of-God-and-of-the-law thesis from independent vantage points: Bas's 'a man of prayer' (Manila Standard, Oct 26, 1997), Farolan's confidence in the High Court's deliberations (Philippine Star, Nov 3, 1997), Sison's 'the first justice ... who has openly advocated the use of simple and non-legal terms' (Philippine Star, Oct 29, 1997), PDI's 'former newsboy and bootblack who rose to become a prominent lawyer' (Oct 14, 1995), and Manila Bulletin's 'he will do justice to every man' (editorial, Oct 7, 1995).

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

PCL singularity: CJP was the only Filipino among the 30 members of the Pontifical Council for the Laity at the time of his 1996 appointment by John Paul II -- a fact stated plainly in the appendix as both honor and responsibility.

### Anecdote 2

Multi-Sectoral Conference on the Administration of Justice (June 13 and Oct 17, 1992) -- CJP as chair -- produced the first-1,000-days program for the Ramos presidency, an exercise in civil-society policy convening that prefigured his later reform work on the Bench.

### Anecdote 3

Newsboy-to-justice arc: PDI's Oct 14, 1995 blurb names CJP as 'a former newsboy and bootblack who rose to become a prominent lawyer' -- the same biographical material CJP himself uses in the C07 honoris-causa commencement speech.

### Anecdote 4

ASTA International chairmanship as first-Asian distinction: presiding over the largest travel association in the world while still in private practice in the late 1980s.

### Anecdote 5

Rotary Club of Manila presidency (1990-1991) -- the largest rotary club in Asia -- and the inaugural-speech adoption of 'Love God Serve Man' as life motto, later made the title of CJP's earlier PDI-published speeches volume.

### Anecdote 6

PDI 1991-1992 presidency: matches the 'someday, I'll be president of a major newspaper' line CJP records in the C07 commencement speech (he was sleeping on Sampaloc sidewalks as a newsboy when he wrote that diary line).

### Anecdote 7

Children's-credentials catalogue: five children all with master's degrees from leading universities abroad -- Maria Elena (UC MBA), Jose Artemio III (Stanford MS 'with distinction'), Maria Jocelyn (Chicago MBA), Maria Theresa (Michigan MBA), Maria Evelyn (Harvard MPP) -- a deliberate counterpoint to CJP's own missed Yale opportunity recounted in C07.

### Anecdote 8

Divestiture-on-elevation: CJP resigned business, professional, and most civic positions on SC appointment, keeping PCL, Rotary Manila, and Bantayog ng mga Bayani Foundation -- a public ethic of single-minded judicial focus stated as fact in the appendix.

### Anecdote 9

Rotary inaugural pledge as closing line of CJP's self-frame: 'I shall be judged ... in my whole unworthy life here on earth' -- a self-imposed eschatological standard, characteristic of CJP's faith-anchored register.

### Anecdote 10

Press-blurb dating: the Bas, Farolan, and Sison blurbs all date from late October/early November 1997, within the same fortnight as the book's publication; the PDI and Manila Bulletin blurbs date from October 1995, the week of CJP's SC oath -- bracketing the book between his appointment and his second anniversary.
