# Justice and Faith -- Ch. 5: Profiles of Supreme Court Justices

## Profiles of Supreme Court Justices

(Remarks made by Justice Panganiban - who was master of ceremonies during the retirement rites held in honor of Justice Justo P. Torres Jr. on October 22, 1997 - in his introduction of the justices of the Supreme Court, as each one of them, in accordance with a hallowed tradition, formally presented to the honoree a memento of his term, something he had used to discharge his function as a jurist. Only ten members of the Court were mentioned, because two - Justice Teodoro R. Padilla and Justice Regino C. Hermosisima Jr. - had retired by then; and two others - Chief Justice Andres R. Narvasa and Senior Justice Florenz D. Regalado - were on official business abroad. The introductions are arranged according to the order in which the justices participated in the program. The introduction for Mr. Justice Torres is the longest, because he was the honoree.)

Mr. Justice Reynato S. Puno

Like a trained surgeon, he uses his pen with laser-like precision to separate and excise fabrication from truth and pretension from reality. In the process, he gives life to populist causes and libertarian ideals. Daring, gutsy and erudite, he -  like Justice Holmes - oftentimes wages lonely battles against conventional wisdom with his stirring dissents and insightful opinions.

Mr. Justice Jose C. Vitug 

An icon of the academe, he is the ultimate teacher and compassionate friend, in whose restrained language the subtlety and rationality of the law find cadenced expression. 

Justice Flerida Ruth P. Romero

The gracious and precious gem of the Court, its only lady member, always proper in conduct and in language, charming in her ways and moods, but equally tenacious in forensically battling and overpowering her combative if sometimes chauvinistic colleagues. 

Justice Ricardo J. Francisco

The essential magistrate to whom law is a jealous master, who never winces or falters in upholding the majesty of the law, regardless of personal consequences and possible public disapproval, always justifying his actions and decisions with only one standard - the quiet comfort of a disciplined conscience. 

Justice Vicente V. Mendoza 

The classic jurist, molded in the ivy-league traditions of Yale, where he still hobnobs annually with the best and brightest legal minds of the world. His ponencias are always scholarly, well-researched, cerebral, lofty and impeccable. 

Justice Santiago M. Kapunan

The perfect gentleman of the bench, suave, noble. A firm believer in the rule of law, he is indefatigable, thoroughly committed, but never imperious.

Justice Jose A.R. Melo 

The consensus builder of the Court and the living barometer of its leanings and positions on various issues. He is respected for his objectivity, fairness, lucidity and openness to new ideas and contrary views. 

Justice Josue N. Bellosillo 

The paragon, the pith and soul of the career magistrate; quick to spot lesions and mischiefs in the judicial hierarchy, but equally adept at defending and promoting the uprightness and welfare of lower court judges. Diligent, assiduous and methodical, he is single-minded about his work and his mission. 

Justice Hilario G. Davide Jr. 

The epitome of the Filipino jurist, makabayan, maginoo, marangal, matapang, masipag, mapagkakatiwalaan, may mabuting asal at diwa, may pagpapahalaga sa kapwa at may paniniwala sa Pilipino at sa kanyang kakayahang maging malaya, maunlad at mapayapa. 

Justice Justo P. Torres Jr. 

Plucked out from the Court of Appeals where he was then the thirteenth most senior member, he was appointed to the Supreme Court by President Ramos on March 13, 1996,. Now, upon his retirement, he also ranked thirteenth among the fifteen members of the Supreme Court. Incidentally, his father, Jus to Sr., ranked thirteenth in the bar examinations of 1925. Yes, thirteen is the lucky number of Justice Tito. The only important place where he is not Number Thirteen is in the heart of Rose, where he is Number One and only!

Mr. Justice Torres' stint in the Court has been regrettably short, spanning only a period of 1 year and 7 months, but he has definitely left his mark in the annals of Philippine jurisprudence. Although senior in chronological age, he is nonetheless very young in mind and in spirit. Verily, he is a head-on activist, who is canalized neither by tradition nor by precedent. He looks at each case with enthusiasm and freshness, ever mindful of its vigor, effect on society in general. Without discounting the relative safety of well-entrenched jurisprudence, he is nonetheless an irrepressible visionary and an ebullient reformer who does not hesitate to try new solutions to resolve recurring problems. God-loving, egalitarian and congenial, he is a connoisseur of fine language. He will now deliver his valedictory address which may give us an opportunity to explore the labyrinth of his vibrant mind and indomitable spirit in the judicial cosmos. Friends, may I invite you to rise and welcome Mr. Justice Justo P. Torres Jr.

## Summary

Chapter 5 of 'Justice and Faith' reproduces CJP's master-of-ceremonies introductions of the then-sitting members of the Supreme Court at Justice Justo P. Torres Jr.'s retirement rites on October 22, 1997 -- each delivered as the named justice rose, per a 'hallowed tradition,' to present the honoree with a memento of his term. Only ten justices are introduced because Padilla and Hermosisima had retired and Narvasa and Regalado were abroad on official business. The vignettes are deliberately plural: Puno is 'a trained surgeon' wielding 'laser-like precision' and waging Holmes-like lonely dissents; Vitug is 'an icon of the academe' whose restrained language renders law in 'cadenced expression'; Romero, 'the gracious and precious gem of the Court, its only lady member,' tenaciously battles 'sometimes chauvinistic colleagues'; Francisco is 'the essential magistrate' guided solely by 'the quiet comfort of a disciplined conscience'; Mendoza is 'molded in the ivy-league traditions of Yale,' scholarly and impeccable; Kapunan is 'the perfect gentleman of the bench,' firm but never imperious; Melo is 'the consensus builder' and 'the living barometer' of the Court; Bellosillo is 'the paragon, the pith and soul of the career magistrate,' diligent and methodical; and at the rhetorical climax CJP shifts into Tagalog to name Davide 'the epitome of the Filipino jurist -- makabayan, maginoo, marangal, matapang, masipag, mapagkakatiwalaan.' The longest introduction is reserved for the honoree, Justice Torres, whose March 13, 1996 Ramos appointment is woven into a 'lucky 13' running joke (CA seniority 13, SC seniority 13, his father Justo Sr. 13th in the 1925 bar), softened only by Rose's heart, where 'he is Number One and only'; the closing invites the audience to rise for Torres's valedictory.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Lucky-13 running motif on Torres -- appointed March 13, 1996; ranked 13th in CA seniority before elevation and 13th in SC seniority on retirement; his father, Justo Sr., ranked 13th in the 1925 bar -- relieved only by Rose's heart, where he is 'Number One and only'

### Anecdote 2

The hallowed memento tradition -- each justice presents the honoree with a personal symbol of office at the retirement rite, with CJP's introductions framing each handover

### Anecdote 3

Composition note as integral to the script -- Padilla and Hermosisima already retired; Narvasa and Regalado abroad; the bench is therefore introduced as ten, not fourteen

### Anecdote 4

Davide-as-rhetorical-peak -- CJP switches into Tagalog precisely at the Chief's profile, signaling that the catalogue of Filipino virtues is the speech's climax

### Anecdote 5

Puno-as-Holmes -- CJP places Puno in the Holmes lineage of lonely dissents, the only justice given a comparator from American jurisprudence
