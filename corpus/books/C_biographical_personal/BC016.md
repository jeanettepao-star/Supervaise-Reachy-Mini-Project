# Justice and Faith -- Ch. 9: Jesus, Shepherd and Leader of All Time

Chapter 9 of 'Justice and Faith' is CJP's BLD reflection on John 10:27-30 (the Good Shepherd discourse), delivered the night before the 1995 Philippine elections and offered to newly elected officials -- senators, congressmen, governors, mayors -- as the leadership measure. He opens with an unusual-leader catalogue (wooden cross, thorns crown, reed scepter, rusty-nail ring, 'Crucify Him' constituents) before naming the only King who resurrected from the dead, then expounds five leadership qualities of Jesus as the chapter's spine. First, clear vision and mission -- the Sermon on the Mount as 'the greatest teaching of all time,' preference for the poor and peacemakers, parables designed to make listeners 'discover something for themselves' (Lk 10:36, 7:42, 20:16). Second, courage and conviction -- Albert Nolan's 'Jesus Before Christianity' on the courage to question tradition, sinners-and-tax-collectors company, the searing Mt 23:25-27 woes against Pharisees as 'whitewashed tombs.' Third, servant-leadership -- Mk 10:42-45 ('Anyone among you who aspires to greatness must serve the rest') is set against Filipino patronage politics in which leaders 'arrogate unto themselves power...allocate huge discretionary funds...dispense patronage and favors'; the foot-washing (Jn 13:14-16) is the example. Fourth, prayer and faith -- the mountain retreat (Mt 14:23-24), the Gethsemane sweat 'like drops of blood' (Lk 22:44), 'Father, forgive them' and 'into Your hands I commend my spirit' from the cross, the Lord's Prayer as the model. Fifth, justice and love -- Romans 14:17, Mt 26:35-40, Isaiah 42, the adulteress in Jn 8:1-11 ('Let the man among you who is sinless be the first to cast a stone'), and St. Paul's hymn to love (1 Cor 13). He closes with a judge-pays-the-fine analogy -- the speeding lady-driver case in which the judge imposes a P500 fine then removes his robe, walks to the court cashier and pays it himself because the law-breaker is his daughter ('Justice was served -- and so was love') -- and reads the atonement as the cosmic instance of this convergence: Jesus the judge imposed the law of God for sin, then bore the guilt and paid the penalty Himself. 'Such is the Leader in Jesus. Such is the Shepherd that is Jesus. Such is the Shepherd of my soul.'
