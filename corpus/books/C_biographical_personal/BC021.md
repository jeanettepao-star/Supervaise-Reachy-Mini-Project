# The Bio-Age Dawns on the Judiciary -- Foreword (by CJ Hilario G. Davide Jr.)

Foreword

FROM THE CHAMBERS OF

Hilario G. Davide Jr.

CHIEF JUSTICE

Innovations and new discoveries in science and technology have been occurring at virtually the “speed of thought," to adopt Bill Gates’ colorful phrase. That statement is not entirely an exaggeration when one ponders the wonders of the human mind’s creativity and inventiveness. Our laws and judicial doctrines would, sooner rather than later, be tested to the limits in adapting to a rapidly changing world. The Judiciary must be on its toes to keep in step with these developments so that it can successfully grapple with complex scientific and technological issues. Unfortunately, there is, as yet, no body of legal literature which addresses  this looming challenge to the knowledge and skills of our brethren in the Bench and Bar. Happily, however, this is the subject of Justice Artemio V. Panganiban’s latest book, The Bio-age Dawns on the Judiciary.

The uneasy relationship between science and jurisprudence is immediately juxtaposed in the very first chapter of this book when Justice Panganiban tackles the role of the courts in the dawning of the bio-age with its myriad of legal, technological, and ethical problems and consequences. Dissecting a fictional case framed by Dr. Franklin M. Zweig on  "human cloning, genetic engineering, cell-line development, and patenting of genes,” Justice Panganiban  "Filipinized" the case and discusses its legal mutations and possible implications in the administration of justice and the rule of law. It may easily be overwhelming to be confronted by the unfamiliar argot of the bio-sciences and their novel repercussions on the body politic.

As in his previous books, Part II (consisting of eight chapters) is a first person account of what this jurist had previously termed as the “judicial jousts” in which he has played a prominent, if not the leading, role. Evoking the image of white knights in their shining armor, the justices are also portrayed as dragon-slayers of the evils that bedevil our country and people.

Indeed, the Supreme Court recently promulgated its landmark decision in the impeachment case against the Chief Justice. The decision is a tribute to the strength and fortitude of our democratic institutions and their leaders who have reaffirmed their faith and confidence in the Supreme Court as the sole arbiter of constitutional questions. Still following the months of incessant assault on the independence and integrity of the Supreme Court, the publication and release of Justice Panganiban’s book is timely, and reading it provides a welcome diversionary pre-occupation.

As Justice Panganiban’s seventh book as a sitting magistrate, The Bio-age Dawns on the Judiciary faithfully chronicles the events in the immediately preceding twelve months of his eighth anniversary in the Supreme Court. It has been a source of amazement to me and our colleagues how he has managed this while writing many ponencias and separate opinions, plus hundreds of minute resolutions. We are grateful for his energy as we benefit from his display of his prolificacy and mental dexterity in this legal treatise which extricates the possible from the hypothetical, the emerging from the established, the literature in science, and the law in art.

Hilario G. Davide Jr.

## Summary

Foreword by Chief Justice Hilario G. Davide Jr. to 'The Bio-Age Dawns on the Judiciary.' Observing that science and technology advance at the 'speed of thought' (Bill Gates), Davide argues the Judiciary must keep in step to grapple with complex scientific and technological issues, notes the absence of legal literature on the subject, and commends Justice Panganiban's book — the latest in his tradition of yearly reports — as filling that gap.
