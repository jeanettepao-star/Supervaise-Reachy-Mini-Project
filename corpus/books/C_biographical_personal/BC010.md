# A Centenary of Justice -- Back Matter: Davide Foreword, Accolades & Aquino Book Review

This row captures the back matter of 'A Centenary of Justice' (Supreme Court Printing Press, December 2001, 413 pages) -- paratext written ABOUT the book rather than by CJP himself, comprising Chief Justice Hilario G. Davide Jr.'s Foreword, the institutional Accolades, and Justice Hilarion L. Aquino's Book Review (the page Index is omitted as pure navigation). Davide's Foreword supplies the authoritative structural map: the book has twenty chapters, of which the first twelve recount the 'Centenary Celebrations' -- some fifty festivities across all thirteen judicial regions -- and the remainder give 'an insider's first-person retelling' of the Court's 'Judicial Jousts,' all rendered with literary skill that lends 'vicarious involvement' to ordinarily 'prosaic prose.' The Accolades distill CJP's public reputation: the Asian Institute of Journalism and Communications praises his 'lucidity of thought' and prodigious output; the University of Iloilo (1997) calls him a 'relentless crusader against infringement of civil liberties'; Far Eastern and Angeles Universities honor him as 'A Man for God and Law'; and Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Maynila cites his 'unrelenting commitment to the rule of law.' Aquino's Book Review -- fixing this as CJP's fifth book -- frames it through a 'concert artist' metaphor and a writer who 'shares of himself without reserve,' then maps its themes and CJP's avowed biases: his consistent opposition to the death penalty, his concern over 'the erosion of the protection due laborers,' his pro-active call for the Court to use its rule-making power on DNA and biotechnology, the 'charming' E-Values for Lawyers ('E-values, for an E-age'), his careful explanation of the Estrada-case inhibition (with the caution that 'too frequent... voluntary inhibition may in fact be a form of dereliction'), the IPRA case that 'blurs the... line between law and policy,' and his 'profound disappointment' at the Perez v. Estrada live-coverage ban. The row is preserved as reputational and structural meta-data, not as CJP's own doctrine.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

The official structure: Davide's Foreword fixes the book's architecture -- twenty chapters, the first twelve on the centenary 'Celebrations' and the rest on the 'Judicial Jousts' -- a useful key for navigating the volume.

### Anecdote 2

'A Man for God and Law': among the Accolades, Angeles University's Medal of Honor (1999) crystallized CJP's public image in that phrase, while the University of Iloilo (1997) called him a 'relentless crusader against infringement of civil liberties.'

### Anecdote 3

The concert-artist metaphor: reviewer Hilarion Aquino casts the book as a musical performance of 'pianissimos and fortissimos,' 'allegro' and 'adagio,' and CJP's body of work as 'one masterpiece after another' in which it is 'not easy to distinguish' the magistrate from the writer.

### Anecdote 4

A frank reviewer's caution: even while admiring CJP's openness about his biases, Aquino gently warns that 'a too frequent recourse to the expedient of a voluntary inhibition may in fact be a form of dereliction of one's duty' -- a pointed counterweight to CJP's Estrada-case recusal.

### Anecdote 5

Fifth book: Aquino's review fixes 'A Centenary of Justice' as CJP's fifth book and locates it within a continuing series of judicial-literary 'masterpieces,' closing with the anticipatory 'Abangan!'
