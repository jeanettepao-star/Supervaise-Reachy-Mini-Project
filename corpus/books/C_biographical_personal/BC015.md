# Justice and Faith -- Ch. 8: Be Not Afraid

Chapter 8 of 'Justice and Faith' is CJP's BLD prayer-meeting reflection on the Sunday-after-February-5,-1995 gospel -- Luke 5, in which Peter, weary after an all-night fruitless catch, obeys Jesus's instruction to lower his nets in daylight ('if you say so, I will lower the nets') and the two boats fill to sinking capacity. CJP turns Peter's obedience into the paradigm of faith ('without obedience, everything is useless in God's eyes' -- Chrysostom via Fernandez), then leavens the gravity with a Filipino-English-translation joke. He then unfolds three directions for 'Be not afraid,' the theme JPII chose for the Petrine ministry and anchored in 'Crossing the Threshold of Hope' (1994). First, 'do not be afraid to be holy' -- the universal call to holiness from the 1987 Synod of Bishops, Vatican II, and Bishop Teodoro Bacani's 'The Filipino Laity: Called to Be Holy' (1988); holiness defined not by piety but by 'loving accomplishment of whatever it is God wills for me here and now'; Mother Teresa ('Are we not all called to holiness?') and San Lorenzo Ruiz (the first Filipino Catholic saint, a layman) are the anchors, with a Mercedes/jeep/bicycle wives joke as the comedic illustration. Second, 'do not be afraid to go into mission' -- JPII's 'As the Father has sent me, so I send you'; the Pope looks to 55 million Filipino Catholics to lead Asia's New Evangelization to its 2.5 billion non-Catholics, echoing PCP II (1991)'s thrust under Archbishop Legaspi; the BLD's 1994 mission statement (restoring the Filipino family, forming BECs, strengthening the parish, breaking the yokes of poverty and oppression, raising prayer warriors) is the concrete response. Third, 'do not be afraid of failure' -- the Covenant House P60 million plan, discerned since 1988 and confirmed in BLD discernment sessions, becomes the corporate test: 'The Lord does not directly hand out money or land. He uses His faithful ones as His instruments.' He closes with a fourfold refrain echoing Peter -- 'If you say so, Lord, we will lower the nets. If You say so, Lord, we will build Your Covenant House. If You say so, Lord, we will not fear. If You say so, Lord, we will have victory.'

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Peter's reluctant obedience at daybreak (Luke 5) -- weary from a fruitless all-night catch, he lowers the nets only because Jesus says so; two boats fill 'to almost sinking capacity'

### Anecdote 2

Filipino-English-translation joke -- politician-actor's 'Do not afraid. Strengthen your inside. For in the long of time, we will success!' -- used to soften the gravity of the 'banish fear' theme

### Anecdote 3

Mother Teresa, when called a 'living saint': 'What is so extraordinary about that? Are we not all called to holiness?'

### Anecdote 4

San Lorenzo Ruiz as the first Filipino Catholic saint and a lay person -- used to anchor the universal-call doctrine in a Filipino face

### Anecdote 5

Three-husbands-at-the-pearly-gates joke -- Juan (teacher) gets a Mercedes Benz, Paolo (lawyer) a jeep, Segundo (politician) a bicycle; Juan later sobs because his wife is on a bicycle too

### Anecdote 6

Covenant House P60 million plan as the test case of corporate faith funding -- discerned since 1988, confirmed by prophecy and discernment sessions

### Anecdote 7

Closing fourfold refrain -- 'If you say so, Lord, we will lower the nets...we will build Your Covenant House...we will not fear...we will have victory' -- Peter's words turned into a BLD decision rule
