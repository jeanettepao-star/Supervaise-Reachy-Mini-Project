# Justice and Faith -- Back Matter: From the Men of Justice and Faith (accolades)

The back matter of 'Justice and Faith,' titled 'From the Men of Justice and Faith,' is a short anthology of accolades from six named figures across ecclesiastical, judicial, academic, and legal-press vantage points -- Chief Justice Andres R. Narvasa (Foreword excerpt reprinted, naming the 'twin beacons' that have guided CJP's professional and personal life); Jaime L. Cardinal Sin (Oct 20, 1995 dinner speech in CJP's honor, calling him 'a very dear friend and lay collaborator ... a truly Catholic lawyer, faithful to the magisterium and loyal to the Lord Jesus Christ'); Bishop Edmundo M. Abaya, then-recent former Vice President of the CBCP (Sept 8, 1995, saying he is 'above all, a man of God and of our Church'); retired Justice Isagani A. Cruz (Oct 15, 1997 letter, 'proving to be one of the most competent members of the Supreme Court today'); Dr. Enrique P. Syquia, publisher of The Lawyers Review (Oct 31, 1997, attesting that CJP is 'the most prolific decision writer in the Supreme Court; but despite the quantity of his decisions, they are also 'quality' as well as clear and readable'); and Dr. Benjamin G. Tayabas, President of Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Maynila (Nov 2, 1995, naming CJP as 'an exemplary model for the students and faculty of Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Maynila'). The composition is itself the argument: by closing the book with six independent institutional voices -- Cardinal Archbishop of Manila, former CBCP Vice President, Chief Justice, retired Justice, legal-press publisher, and public-university president -- the back matter triangulates the man-of-God-and-of-the-law identity that the Appendix has just stated as fact, and brackets CJP's first two SC years between two clusters of endorsements (September-November 1995 around his oath; October-November 1997 around the book's publication and his second anniversary). Reprinting Narvasa's 'twin beacons' formula at the back matter closes the volume on the same integrative frame with which it opened -- a deliberate compositional move that makes Justice and Faith the keystone of CJP's self-presentation as a debut author.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Cardinal Sin hosted a dinner in CJP's honor on October 20, 1995 -- only ten days after CJP's SC oath -- and there delivered the line about CJP being 'a very dear friend and lay collaborator' and 'a truly Catholic lawyer, faithful to the magisterium' -- the timing makes the endorsement an early public welcome from the Cardinal Archbishop of Manila to the new SC justice.

### Anecdote 2

Bishop Abaya's Sept 8, 1995 endorsement predates CJP's Oct 10, 1995 oath -- meaning the Church's recognition of CJP as 'a man of God and of our Church' was in place before the JBC's final list was confirmed; the dating implicitly hints at the spiritual-discernment process CJP himself describes in C06 ('Faith Brought Me to the Supreme Court').

### Anecdote 3

Tayabas's Nov 2, 1995 endorsement (in the third week of CJP's tenure) anchors the academic-civic vantage point: a public-university president identifying CJP as 'an exemplary model' immediately after his arrival on the Bench.

### Anecdote 4

Cruz's Oct 15, 1997 letter and Syquia's Oct 31, 1997 attestation come at the very moment of CJP's second anniversary on the Bench and the book's publication -- the late-1997 endorsements close the temporal arc the early-1995 endorsements opened.

### Anecdote 5

The Syquia line is the only accolade that explicitly engages a potential criticism (the quality-vs-quantity tradeoff) and rebuts it: 'despite the quantity of his decisions, they are also 'quality' as well as clear and readable' -- the 'despite' word is doing the rhetorical work, acknowledging that the criticism could be raised and then refuting it.

### Anecdote 6

Six independent institutional vantage points -- Cardinal, Bishop, Chief Justice, retired Justice, legal-press publisher, public-university president -- are arranged in the back matter so no single endorsement carries the whole weight; the strategy is plural attestation, not amplification.
