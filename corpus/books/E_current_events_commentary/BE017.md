# With Due Respect (Vol. 6) -- Ch. 1: Destiny and Aspiration

# Destiny and Aspiration
Date:       2009-09-06
Publisher:  Philippine Daily Inquirer (Opinion)
Source:     With Due Respect (2011)
By: Artemio V. Panganiban


One can aspire for, plan and win a seat in the Senate. But the presidency? That's destiny." That was how Ninoy Aquino, the campaign manager of the Liberal Party for the 1971 senatorial polls, then characterized the ascent to the top during a press conference at the now defunct Au Bon Vivant Restaurant in Manila.

**Destiny**

Indeed, many brilliant officials aspired and certainly deserved to reside in Malacañang, among them, Claro M. Recto, Salvador "Doy" Laurel, Gerardo Roxas, Jovito R. Salonga, and Ninoy himself. But sadly, none of them landed in the Palace. Who would have thought that an unpretentious, guileless, and reluctant Cory Aquino would be propelled to the presidency? And now, who would have thought that her son, Noynoy, would be the next reluctant "presidentiable"?

Noynoy had pledged to support Mar Roxas. He was content in promoting his friend's ambition. But his mother's death altered their best-laid plans. To his credit, Mar immediately recognized the changed landscape and unconditionally endorsed Noynoy as the Liberal Party standard-bearer.

Fidel V. Ramos too was not thinking of the presidency until he worried that the Edsa legacy he fought and nearly died for self. And despite being rebuffed by the ruling party, he courageously marched on to capture the elusive fortress, with Corys' help.

Joseph Estrada was content to be the king of San Juan, until the new Edsa rulers ousted him from his enclave. Thus, eh had to seek other avenues-the Senate, the vice presidency, and only two and half years of his six-year term, he has embarked on a new journey to regain his destiny.

Considering the formidable odds facing him, not the least of which are the legal questions of whether as a former convict who was stripped of his political rights and as a former president who-his detractors claim—is constitutionally barred from seeking his previous position, his victory would truly be destiny of the highest order. Yes, destiny it is if he can rise again as the savior of the masses he always loved and of the country he never abandoned.

**Succession**

Destiny it was also for those who rose by succession rather. Garthan by election. Vice Presidents Elpidio Quirino, Carlos P Garcia, and Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo ascended because they were ta the right place at the right time. Carlos P. Romulo was the initial VP choice of President Manuel Roxas (Mar's grandfather). But Romulo begged off, preferring to be ambassador to the United States (See Romulo, Crusade in Asia, p. 16). With the untimely demise of Roxas in 1948, the unheralded Quirino inherited the man tle.

The colorful Manila Mayor Arsenio H. Lacson was President Ramon Magsaysay's preferred teammate. Declining his nomination as VP, Lacson quipped, "Everybody knows a vice-president's job is to wait for the president to die. I am afraid Magsaysay is a very healthy man and would make a healthier president." (Abueva, Ramon Magsaysay: A Political Biography, p. 240) When Magsaysay died in a plane crash in 1957, VP Garcia took over, to Lacson's chagrin.

Remember too that Ms. Arroyo was initially gunning for the top job but settled for the vice presidency upon realizing that Erap could not be defeated in the 1998 race.

**Ambition**

While destiny may have propelled many, Diosdado Macapagal and Ferdinand Marcos prepared themselves and meticulously planned their way. Both were brilliant lawyers, nay, bar topnotchers. To steel himself, Macapagal added a doctoral degree in economics to his credentials. Both worked their traditional way to power, by scheming, dealing, and winning the people's vote.

This is the same ambitious road that Manuel Villar is taking. Sipag at tiyaga, so his battle cry goes; first to the House and the speakership, then to the Senate and the Senate presidency. Doy Laurel saw the future in him. On his deathbed, he asked his nephew and political chieftain Jose Macario Laurel to recruit Villar into the Nacionalista Party. Incidentally, Doy Laurel wanted the presidency too. But like his father, Jose P. Laurel, who gave way to Magsaysay in 1953, Doy withdrew in favor of Cory in the 1986 snap election.

Villar revitalized the NP, accumulated logistics through his business empire, and methodically campaigned, using time-tested media exposures and well-planned town hall forays, landing him at the top of the latest surveys.

As of now, VP Noli de Castro is neither a hero of destiny nor of ambition. But if he must land at the Palace, it will have to be via sheer piloted desire, which he told me during President Cory he did not have yet. But the yellow the unmistakable signal that President Arroyo want immediate action. He should now desire the preside slip away.

**Summation**

As 2010 looms, I think the battle royale will be among these four: Noynoy Aquino, the yellow man of destiny; Jo the resurgent hero of the masa; Manny Villar, the epitome of logistics and planning; and Noli de Castro, the choice Arroyo and the Lakas-Kampi-CMD. Which will prevail or aspiration? We should know soon enough.

Francis Escudero, Loren Legarda, Jejomar Binay Gibo Teodoro, Bayani Fernando, Richard Gordon, Eddie Villa may slide down to the VP derby, or endorse one of Ed Panlilio and Grace Padaca did last Friday. Better to win as VP than to lose as president. (September , 6 2009)

## Summary

Chapter 1 of 'With Due Respect' (Vol. 6), CJP's September 6, 2009 Inquirer column, handicaps the looming 2010 presidential race through Ninoy Aquino's old maxim that one can 'aspire for, plan and win a seat in the Senate' but the presidency is 'destiny.' Under 'destiny,' he notes that deserving aspirants -- Recto, Doy Laurel, Gerardo Roxas, Salonga, Ninoy himself -- never reached Malacañang, while the 'unpretentious... and reluctant' Cory was 'propelled' to it, and now her son Noynoy, another 'reluctant presidentiable,' was catapulted after Cory's death 'altered' his plan to back Mar Roxas (who then 'unconditionally endorsed' him). Ramos pursued the presidency only to save the Edsa legacy, and Estrada -- once 'king of San Juan' -- was on 'a new journey to regain his destiny' despite legal clouds over a former convict and a former president running again. Under 'succession,' VPs Quirino, Garcia and Arroyo rose 'at the right place at the right time' (Romulo having declined Roxas's VP slot for the US ambassadorship, and Arsenio Lacson having quipped that a VP merely waits 'for the president to die'). Under 'ambition,' the planners Macapagal and Marcos -- both bar topnotchers -- 'meticulously planned their way,' a road Manuel Villar now travels under 'sipag at tiyaga,' having been recruited into the Nacionalista Party at Doy Laurel's deathbed wish. CJP forecasts a 'battle royale' among Noynoy ('the yellow man of destiny'), Erap ('the resurgent hero of the masa'), Villar ('the epitome of logistics and planning') and Noli de Castro (Arroyo's choice), with also-rans likely to 'slide down to the VP derby' -- since it is 'better to win as VP than to lose as president.'

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Ninoy's maxim: CJP anchors the column on Ninoy Aquino's 1971 line that the Senate can be won, but the presidency is 'destiny.'

### Anecdote 2

Lacson's quip: he relays Arsenio Lacson's joke that a VP merely waits 'for the president to die,' and that the healthy Magsaysay 'would make a healthier president' -- before Garcia, not Lacson, succeeded.

### Anecdote 3

Romulo's declined slot: he notes Carlos Romulo turned down Roxas's VP offer to be US ambassador, leaving Quirino to inherit the presidency in 1948.

### Anecdote 4

Doy Laurel's deathbed wish: he recounts that Doy Laurel, who had withdrawn for Cory in 1986, asked on his deathbed that Villar be recruited into the Nacionalista Party.
