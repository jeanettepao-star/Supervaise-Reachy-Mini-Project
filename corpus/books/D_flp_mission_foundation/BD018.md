# A Centenary of Justice -- Front Matter (Cover, Centennial Prayer, Contents, Foreword, Preface)

Front matter of 'A Centenary of Justice' (December 2001), the annual chronicle of Justice Artemio V. Panganiban's sixth year on the Supreme Court (Oct 11, 2000 - Oct 10, 2001), published during the Court's 1901-2001 centenary under the theme 'Katarungan at Bayan Magpakailanman' (Justice and Country Forever). The opening pages reproduce the SC Centenary Logo and Justice Leonardo A. Quisumbing's exegesis of it -- purple scales for equality and majesty, and an equilateral triangle that doubles as the Greek letter delta, meaning 'change,' to signify the Court's openness to reform -- followed by the Centennial Prayer for the Courts. In his Foreword (15 October 2001), CJ Hilario G. Davide Jr. calls the launch 'an unfailing annual ritual' and the book 'the fifth book in a series that chronicles the preceding twelve months of his magistracy,' produced amid the Estrada-Arroyo succession crisis; Davide records that he and Panganiban voluntarily inhibited themselves from the unanimous succession decision (Chapter 13) and that 'our recusal has further served to strengthen the independence of the judiciary and the rule of law.' CJP's Preface explains the Court's founding rationale -- June 11, 1901 (Act 136, the Judiciary Law of the Second Philippine Commission) is reckoned the SC's birthday because it established an INDEPENDENT judicial hierarchy, unlike the executive-attached Royal Audiencia and the American-era court. He describes chairing the Centenary Executive Committee (50-plus projects across 13 judicial regions, June 2000 to June 2002), his sixth-year output (over 100 ponencias and 10 separate opinions; a six-year total above 650 ponencias and 60 separate opinions), and the book's two-part design mirroring his dual duties: Part I (Centenary Celebrations, Chapters 1-12) and Part II (Judicial Jousts, Chapters 13-20, covering Estrada v. Desierto/Arroyo, the death penalty, Cruz v. Secretary of Environment, Ang Bagong Bayani, Perez v. Estrada, Firestone Ceramics, Bengson v. HRET, and Social Weather Stations v. Comelec). Two governing achievements close the preface: the zero-backlog program that cut the SC backlog from ~6,000 cases in 1986 to fewer than 50 ('I am not clapping yet... zero backlog is a continuing effort'), and his simple-language writing philosophy -- against pedantic legalese and aimed so 'even high school graduates would comprehend me,' echoing how Christ reduced the Ten Commandments to two while humans complicated them into countless laws. The matter also logs court-composition changes (Purisima→Sandoval-Gutierrez; Gonzaga-Reyes→Carpio; the new U-shaped conference table) and international engagements with the chief justices of China, Hong Kong, and New Zealand.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

The zero-backlog turnaround: the SC backlog fell from about 6,000 cases in 1986 to ~2,000 in 1998, ~1,000 in 2000, and fewer than 50 by 2001 -- achieved by redistributing all cases docketed 'GR 120000' or lower to senior justices who had no backlog. CJP refuses to celebrate prematurely: 'I am not clapping yet... zero backlog is a continuing effort.'

### Anecdote 2

The Greek-delta logo: the equilateral triangle supporting each disc of the balance in the SC Centenary Logo doubles as the Greek letter delta, the mathematical symbol for 'change' -- encoding the Court's openness to reform directly into its centennial emblem.

### Anecdote 3

The mutual recusal: both CJ Davide and Justice Panganiban voluntarily inhibited themselves from the landmark unanimous decision on Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo's succession to the presidency (Chapter 13), turning what could have read as absence into a deliberate strengthening of judicial independence.

### Anecdote 4

The robe and wig in Wellington: after the Christchurch conference, NZ Court of Appeals President Ivor Richardson invited Panganiban to try on the gala robe and wig worn by New Zealand magistrates -- one of several international-engagement vignettes preserved in the book's photo captions.
