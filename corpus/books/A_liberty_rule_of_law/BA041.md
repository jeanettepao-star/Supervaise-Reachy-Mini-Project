# Justice and Faith -- Ch. 2: Selected Quotations from Ponencias and Opinions

Chapter 2 of 'Justice and Faith' is a curated anthology of direct quotations from CJP's first-two-years ponencias and opinions (October 1995 to October 1997), arranged in ten lettered topical subsections (A through K, with E omitted): A. On Promoting Substantial Justice, B. On Upholding the People's Sovereignty, C. On Protecting the People's Rights, D. On Interpreting the Constitution and the Laws, F. On Protecting Human Life, G. On Comforting the Innocent, H. On Compassion for the Repentant, I. On Promoting Cultural Values and Religious Faith, J. On Enhancing Trade and Industry, and K. On the Conduct of Judicial Officers. Signature passages anchor each section -- from Frivaldo v. Comelec's titular 'the real essence of justice does not emanate from quibblings over patchwork legal technicality. It proceeds from the spirit's gut consciousness of the dynamic role of law as a brick in the ultimate development of the social edifice'; through Philippines Today v. NLRC's 'each case has its own flesh and blood and cannot be decided simply on the basis of isolated clinical classroom principles'; Leonor v. CA's 'the Supreme Court is not just a toothless promoter of procedural niceties... [but] the fountain of speedy, adequate and substantial justice'; Obosa v. CA's 'law must be understood not by the letter that killeth but by the spirit that giveth life'; the Tabuena Dissent's Nuremberg-analogy holding that 'a moral choice was not only possible. It was in fact available' and that 'before [mercy] can be invoked, there must first be justice'; Santiago v. Comelec's Separate Opinion treating initiative, referendum and recall as 'institutionalized legacies of the world-admired EDSA people power' that must be 'carefully nurtured and zealously protected' while abuse is 'resolutely struck down'; the Manila Prince Hotel Dissent's bidding-as-ridiculous-sham critique with its closing 'do not do unto others what you do not want others to do unto you'; and Valat v. Judge Ramos's standard that 'ignorance of the law on the part of a judge is not only most ignominious, it is also prejudicial to litigants and the administration of justice as a whole.' Together the subsections present CJP's signature doctrinal voice across his first two years on the bench.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Frivaldo's 'venerability in Philippine political life' -- invoking life-circumstance to justify substantial-justice over technicality

### Anecdote 2

Philippines Today's flesh-and-blood maxim -- applied to NLRC labor litigation

### Anecdote 3

Tabuena Dissent Nuremberg analogy -- military officers of the Third Reich vs civilian officials of the Marcos era; CJP holds the Marcos-era defendants to a stricter standard since 'a moral choice was not only possible. It was in fact available'

### Anecdote 4

Republic v. Sandiganbayan policy -- 'eleven years have passed since the government started its search' for ill-gotten wealth, demanding resolution on the merits 'free of delaying technicalities and annoying procedural sidetracks'

### Anecdote 5

Santiago v. Comelec Separate Opinion -- majority's burning-the-house-to-exterminate-the-rats critique against blanket restriction on people's initiative

### Anecdote 6

Manila Prince Hotel Dissent -- bidding-as-ridiculous-sham critique against allowing losing Filipino bidders to match the winning foreign bid; 'Only in the Philippines!'

### Anecdote 7

Fortune Motors' wholesale automotive financing scheme -- pedagogical opening explaining the trust-receipt mechanism for car dealers as exemplar of CJP's noncriminal-introduction style
