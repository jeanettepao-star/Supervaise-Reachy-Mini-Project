# Automation or Confusion

Outline of the extemporaneous talk delivered by retired Chief Justice ARTEMIO V. PANGANIBAN as Guest of Honor and Speaker during the Makati Business Club meeting, April 20, 2010, Rigodon Ballroom, Peninsula Hotel.

---

DESIDERATA

To have a credible and democratic turnover of power via HOPE: Honest, Orderly and Peaceful Elections, if possible, automated.

REALITY

Automation has many problems: limited time, defective automation system, lack of transparency, spotty Comelec preparations, inadequate testing of PCOS, and inadequate orientation of teachers and voters.

FUNDAMENTALS OF AUTOMATION

Precinct-specific ballots; precinct-specific PCOS machines; automated counting at precincts; automated transmission of results; proclamation based on automated results.

SOME AUTOMATION PROBLEMS

PCOS or ballots are misdelivered; PCOS fail to function at the precincts; PCOS are stolen, disabled or burned; ballots are erroneously printed; election results are transmitted wrongly; massive disenfranchisements due to long queues.

If the foregoing problems happen only in a few towns, the problems would be localized. But if they occur in too many places and involve more ballots than the margin of the leading presidential candidate over the next, no one would be proclaimed winner.

POSSIBLE LEGAL CONSEQUENCES

Failure of elections: when no election is actually held. Failure of proclamation: when no clear winner. Massive disenfranchisement. Massive electoral fraud: retail and wholesale.

POSSIBLE POLITICAL CONSEQUENCES

Presidential Succession by July 1, 2010: Vice President, Senate President, Speaker. GMA Holdover: not expressly provided by the Constitution but supported by jurisprudence, the latest of which is Seneres vs Comelec (April 16, 2009).

BRAINS AND BRAWNS

Necessary Elements for GMA's holdover: Supreme Court; Armed Forces; Philippine National Police; Filipino people including Church, INC, and People Organizations; and the International Community, especially the US, to prop up the economy.

WAYS TO AVOID HOLDOVER OR MILITARY RULE

Overwhelming victory by the presidential winner; overwhelming victory and proclamation of the VP winner; vigilance before, during and after election day.

TIMING PROBLEMS

From Election Day, May 10, 2010, to the end of GMA's term on June 30, 2010 is 61 days. Many things can happen during that period.

NOTES

Failure of Elections is distinguished from Failure of Proclamation. Congress, not Comelec, canvasses and proclaims newly elected President and Vice President, to be started no later than 30 days after Election Day. In case of a tie, one shall be chosen by the vote of a majority of all the Members of Congress (Art. VII, Sec. 4). Presidential Succession: if a President shall not have been chosen, the VP-elect shall act as President until a President shall have been chosen and qualified. Where no President and Vice President shall have been chosen or qualified, the President of the Senate, or in case of his inability, the Speaker of the House of Representatives shall act as President until a President or a Vice President shall have been chosen and qualified (Art. VII, Sec. 7).

## Summary

Published April 20, 2010 as a structured policy analysis paper on the risks of automated elections for the May 10, 2010 Philippine elections, this document by CJP is organized as a series of headers: desiderata (HOPE: Honest, Orderly and Peaceful Elections, if possible automated), reality (limited time, defective system, spotty Comelec preparations, inadequate PCOS testing), fundamentals of automation (precinct-specific ballots and machines; automated counting, transmission and proclamation), and automation problems (misdelivery, PCOS failures, theft/burning, erroneous ballots, wrong transmissions, long-queue disenfranchisements). The critical threshold: if problems involve more ballots than the margin between the leading presidential candidates, no winner can be proclaimed. Legal consequences: failure of elections, failure of proclamation, massive disenfranchisement, massive electoral fraud. Political consequences: presidential succession (VP, Senate President, Speaker) or GMA holdover -- 'not expressly provided by the Constitution but supported by jurisprudence, the latest of which is Seneres vs. Comelec (April 16, 2009).' For a holdover to work: 'Brains and Brawns' -- SC, AFP, PNP, Church, INC, People Organizations, and the US to prop up the economy. GMA's three options: (1) exit gracefully and protect her back through the House, SC, Sandiganbayan, Ombudsman, Comelec and Military; (2) extend reign via Cha-cha (people's initiative, constituent assembly or con-con), martial law (60 days; Congress can revoke; SC reviews factual basis -- but 'emergency rule has no constitutional authority'); or (3) prevent via HOPE elections, vigilant media and people, People Power, CBCP, Twitter revolt, or full-scale revolution. Timeline: Election Day (May 10) to GMA's term end (June 30) = 61 days. 'Many things can happen during that period.' Closes with Einstein: 'Insanity is committing the same errors and expecting different results.'
