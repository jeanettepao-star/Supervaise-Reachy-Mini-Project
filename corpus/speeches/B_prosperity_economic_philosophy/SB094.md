# Rights, Duties and Perks of Independent Directors

Address delivered by retired Chief Justice ARTEMIO V. PANGANIBAN during a Breakfast Roundtable of the Institute of Corporate Directors on September 28, 2011.

---

I thank Dr. Jesus P. Estanislao, chairman of the Institute of Corporate Directors for inviting me to this morning's Breakfast Roundtable. Due to the length of our topic, I will immediately cut to the chase and discuss six topics.

NATURE OF INDEPENDENT DIRECTORSHIP

FIRST, What is an Independent Director or ID? To begin with, an ID like any other member of the board of directors must qualify as a director under the Corporation Code. He must own at least one share of stock and be elected in accordance with the provisions of the Code, the Articles of Incorporation and Bylaws of the Corporation concerned.

All directors, whether independent or not, are trustees or fiduciaries of the shareholders. As such, they must always uphold the welfare and interests of the corporation and of the shareholders, above and beyond their own individual or partisan interests.

Members of the Board of Directors, including the Independent Directors, collectively exercise the corporate powers of the corporation, and conduct all corporate business and control all property of the corporation. While executive directors hold positions in management apart from their directorships, and are therefore entitled to compensation for their work as executives on top of their compensation as directors, the Independent Director gets no such additional income. Precisely because he as an Independent Director must remain independent of management, he may not perform other work for the corporation for which he could receive additional compensation.

SRC****'****S DEFINITION OF IDS

SECOND, The Securities Regulations Code or SRC institutionalized the Independent Director. Section 38 of the SRC defines an ID as a person other than an officer or employee of the corporation, its parent or subsidiaries, or any other individual having a relationship with the corporation, which would interfere with the exercise of independent judgment in carrying out the responsibilities of a director.

DISTINCTIVE DUTY OF IDS

THIRD, from the foregoing negative covenants, which are worded like the Ten Commandments, comes the sterling requirement that the ID must be independent of management, which in turn is installed or elected by the controlling stockholders. He is expected to fiscalize and counter-check management and even the controlling shareholders who elected him to office. He is expected to uphold ever zealously at all times the laws of the land, the articles and bylaws of the corporation, the canons of corporate ethics and the manual of corporate governance.

The implicit assumption and unspoken expectation behind the creation of the post of Independent Director is that the person elected as such will be sufficiently courageous and independent as to be able to effectively fiscalize and counter-check whatever the other directors and the management are doing, and not hesitate to voice contrary opinions and actively oppose the management if and when necessary.

SAFEGUARDING ID****'****S INDEPENDENCE

FOURTH, like the Judicial Department of government where I came from, the SRC demands independence from IDs. However, IDs do not enjoy constitutional guarantees of security of tenure and security of compensation like judges do. In actual practice, an Independent Director will only become such if he is acceptable to the board majority and the controlling shareholders. By law, directors have a term of one year. Their continuation in office and their eventual reelection depends on the controlling shareholders and the management.

IMPROPER SEC IMPOSITIONS

FIFTH, I think we can all agree that the system of independent directorship should be strengthened to encourage them to perform their main job of courageously and intelligently checking management and the controlling stockholders. However, some recent SEC moves have been counterproductive.

The SEC proposed a 5-5 plan: that an ID can sit on the board of directors of any given corporation and its subsidiaries and affiliates for only five consecutive years, and only in not more than five publicly listed corporations and their subsidiaries and affiliates simultaneously in any given year.

Like the Ten Commandments and the SRC, these prohibitions are NOT provided in the SRC but are SEC-originated inventions and impositions. By way of analogy, can Supreme Court justices who are to be appointed yearly by the President and whose compensation is dependent totally on Congress be expected to be independent? By the same token, can we truly expect full independence from IDs who depend on the votes of the majority to be elected yearly, and on the board of directors to fix their compensation periodically? Surely, instead of restricting the prerogatives of IDs, the first order of the day should be to strengthen their independence and integrity so they can perform their lonely work effectively.

AVAILABLE REMEDIES

SIXTH, what are the remedies of those who disagree sincerely with and are affected unjustly by SEC inventions and impositions? First, take the route of persuasion. Second, take the administrative route. Third, take the judicial course and show that the SEC abused and exceeded its power and authority. Fourth, sue the SEC officials in the Office of the Ombudsman.

May I conclude this address with the famous saying that the end never justifies the means. However laudable its motives, the SEC must not impose unreasonable, whimsical and illegal inventions beyond its legal mandate to do.

Thank you. I am now ready for your reactions and questions.

## Summary

Delivered September 28, 2011 at the Institute of Corporate Directors Breakfast Roundtable -- invited by Chairman Dr. Jesus P. Estanislao -- this six-topic address by CJP analyzes the nature, duties, independence safeguards, SEC propositions and remedies for Independent Directors. An ID must own at least one share, be elected per the Corporation Code, serve as a trustee/fiduciary of the corporation above private interests, and receive no additional compensation beyond director fees. SRC Section 38 defines IDs through negative covenants ('other than an officer or employee... having a relationship... which would interfere with independent judgment') -- 'worded like the Ten Commandments.' The ID's distinctive duty: fiscalize and counter-check management and controlling shareholders; uphold laws, articles, bylaws, ethics canons and governance manuals; be 'sufficiently courageous and independent' to oppose management when necessary. Unlike judges, IDs have no constitutional security of tenure or compensation -- their continuation depends on controlling shareholders. On the SEC's proposed 5-5 plan (five consecutive years per corporation; maximum five PLCs simultaneously): 'like the Ten Commandments and the SRC, these prohibitions are NOT provided in the SRC but are SEC-originated inventions and impositions.' The SC justice analogy: 'Can SC justices appointed annually by the President and whose compensation depends totally on Congress be expected to be independent? By the same token, can we truly expect full independence from IDs who depend on the votes of the majority?' The solution: 'instead of restricting the prerogatives of IDs, the first order of the day should be to strengthen their independence and integrity so they can perform their lonely work effectively.' Four remedies: persuasion, administrative route, judicial route (showing SEC exceeded its authority), and Ombudsman complaints against SEC officials. Close: 'the end never justifies the means. However laudable its motives, the SEC must not impose unreasonable, whimsical and illegal inventions beyond its legal mandate.'

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

The Ten Commandments analogy for Section 38: the SRC's definition of IDs, like the Ten Commandments, is worded as negative covenants ('a person OTHER than an officer or employee... having a relationship... which would interfere'); just as the Ten Commandments say 'Thou shalt not,' the SRC says 'not this, not that' -- and the 5-5 plan adds prohibitions that are not in the original text.

### Anecdote 2

The SC justice analogy: 'Can Supreme Court justices who are appointed yearly by the President and whose compensation is dependent totally on Congress be expected to be independent? By the same token, can we truly expect full independence from IDs who depend on the votes of the majority to be elected yearly?' -- the institutional analogy that exposes the SEC's flawed reasoning.

### Anecdote 3

Four remedies enumerated: (1) persuasion (professional engagement with SEC); (2) administrative route (formal SEC processes); (3) judicial route (showing SEC exceeded its authority); (4) Ombudsman complaints against SEC officials. A progressively escalating set of options for those harmed by the 5-5 plan.
