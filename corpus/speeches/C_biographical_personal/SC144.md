# A Salutary Life

Message delivered by Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban during the retirement ceremony honoring Court of Appeals Justice Santiago Javier Ranada, held on November 10, 2006, at the Court of Appeals New Session Hall, Centennial Building, Manila.

---

Magandang hapon po sa inyong lahat. Nagagalak po ako sa ating pagkikita ngayon upang parangalan ang isang magiting na kaibigan at masusing mahistrado. Indeed, I am happy to be with you today to mark the retirement of Justice Santiago Javier Ranada, a distinguished member of the Court of Appeals.

By now, you have already heard a lot of things about Justice Ranada. But there is actually a lot more about him that remains to be told — that which concerns him not only as a seasoned jurist, but also as an obedient son, a faithful husband and loving father, a genteel person, a gentleman boss, and an illustrious Laoagueño.

OBEDIENT SON

There are many things that I have in common with our honoree. For one, I am also a junior because my father's name is also Artemio. But I have refrained from using Jr. after my name so that every good thing that I do may be credited to my late father, who had practically commanded me, contrary to my wishes to become a chemical engineer, to pursue a career in law.

It was also his father's wish that led Justice Ago or Jun Ranada to become not just a lawyer, but a judge as well. The late Santiago Ranada Sr., a sagacious judge in Pasay City, had whispered at his death bed to Jun's mother that he wanted his junior to be a judge, just like him. Having already established a successful law practice, he found the request difficult to accede to. But eventually he took the road his father had fated him to take. And so, he became a judge of the Court of First Instance in 1978. From there, he took the long climb up to becoming a justice of the Court of Appeals. And thus by his illustrious career, has he truly honored the memory and wish of his late father.

FAITHFUL HUSBAND AND LOVING FATHER

Consistency is one of Justice Jun's principal virtues. Evelyn, his wife of 39 years, relates with fondness that for the past 42 years, he has had only two barbers, and most important, he has had only his one and only love for the past four decades. All of his five children finished their studies. Three of them are now married and have gifted him and Evelyn with seven grandchildren.

GENTLEMAN BOSS

As a boss, Justice Jun is known to his staff as a gentleman under any and all circumstances. He is soft-spoken, patient, and very discreet. He does not take offense easily, neither does he give any. During the nearly three decades he has served the judiciary, our honoree found the greatest enjoyment holding trials in his court.

HOPE AND FEAR

I am told that Justice Jun looks forward to retirement with mixed feelings of hope and fear — 80 percent of hope and 20 percent of fear. Now that my own retirement is just weeks away, I can understand his jitters.

But like me, and all retiring jurists, Jun has a lot to thank for and look back to the years he has spent in the judiciary. On top of that to-thank-for list would be a salutary life, a decent job, the rare opportunity to serve the judiciary and to be remembered patriotically beyond this life.

Mabuhay ka, Justice Jun! Maraming salamat po.

## Summary

Delivered November 6, 2006 at the retirement celebration of Court of Appeals Justice Santiago 'Jun' Javier Ranada -- CJP three weeks before his own mandatory retirement -- this address by CJP opens with a personal parallel: 'I am also a junior because my father's name is also Artemio. But I have refrained from using Jr. after my name so that every good thing that I do may be credited to my late father, who had practically commanded me, contrary to my wishes to become a chemical engineer, to pursue a career in law.' Justice Ranada's career similarly began with a deathbed wish: his father, Santiago Ranada Sr. (a Pasay City judge), whispered at his deathbed that he wanted his junior to become a judge; despite an established law practice, Ranada eventually honored the wish, becoming a CFI judge in 1978 and climbing to the Court of Appeals. CJP presents five character portraits: obedient son (honored the deathbed wish); faithful husband ('Evelyn relates that for the past 42 years, he has had only two barbers, and most important, he has had only his one and only love for the past four decades'); loving father (five children, three married, seven grandchildren); gentleman boss (soft-spoken, patient, discreet; 'found the greatest enjoyment holding trials in his court'); and illustrious Laoagueño. CJP notes: 'Justice Jun looks forward to retirement with mixed feelings of hope and fear -- 80 percent hope and 20 percent fear. Now that my own retirement is just weeks away, I can understand his jitters.' He closes: 'On top of that to-thank-for list would be a salutary life, a decent job, the rare opportunity to serve the judiciary and to be remembered patriotically beyond this life. Mabuhay ka, Justice Jun!'
