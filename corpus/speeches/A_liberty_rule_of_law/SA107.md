# A Toast to Justice Carpio

Transcript of the extemporaneous remarks given by retired Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban during the surprise party in honor of Justice Antonio T. Carpio on his 60th birthday at the Esplanade, Seaside Boulevard, Mall of Asia Complex, Pasay City.

---

I am greatly honored to have been invited to greet Justice Antonio T. Carpio today, his 60th birthday. Justice Carpio is a man of a few words. I do not mean that his vocabulary is short or that he has nothing to say. Quite the contrary, he has mastered the English language. What I mean is that he does not waste words, he does not speak merely to impress or write merely to show-off. Rather, he goes for the jugular. He does not go around the bush.

During Court sessions, he speaks and writes emphatically, with authority, backed up by deep research and unassailable logic. Pity the person on the other side of the argument. He or she is always overwhelmed.

Justice Carpio is a man of deep convictions and enormous moral courage. He is always guided by basic values like integrity, honor, patriotism and accountability. He hates forked tongues, two-faced Januses, grafters, and schemers.

He backs up his conviction with resolute action. He never wavers or compromises his basic principles. He resigned his Cabinet posts during the Ramos presidency when he could no longer agree with the policies of his superiors.

He was Gloria Macapagal Arroyo's counsel prior to her ascension to the presidency; he was her first appointee to the Supreme Court. But he did not hesitate to strike down her unconstitutional and irregular actions, especially the many attempts to extend her reign unconstitutionally. In fact, he was ready to resign his magistracy had the Court given its imprimatur to the people's initiative to shift our presidential form of government to the parliamentary system.

He could have just kept quiet and went along sheepishly. But he chose to speak out and to speak pointedly, nay harshly, as befits his office as a member of the highest court of the land. To him, it is more important to be correct, to be honor bound than to be titled grandiosely and be damned in history.

Friends, join me as I offer a toast to a man of conviction, honor and courage. Justice Antonio Tirol Carpio, may his tribe increase and may he continue to be the conscience of the nation and the nemesis of schemers and thieves. Mabuhay!

## Summary

Delivered October 25, 2009 as a birthday toast for Justice Antonio Tirol Carpio on his 60th birthday, this brief tribute by CJP opens with the governing professional portrait: Carpio is 'a man of few words' not because his vocabulary is short but because 'he does not waste words, he does not speak merely to impress. Rather, he goes for the jugular. He does not go around the bush.' In court sessions, 'he speaks and writes emphatically, with authority, backed up by deep research and unassailable logic. Pity the person on the other side.' He is 'always guided by basic values like integrity, honor, patriotism and accountability. He hates forked tongues, two-faced Januses, grafters, and schemers.' CJP identifies four specific acts proving his convictions: he resigned Cabinet posts during the Ramos presidency when he could no longer agree with his superiors' policies; he was GMA's counsel before her presidency and her first SC appointee, but 'did not hesitate to strike down her unconstitutional and irregular actions, especially the many attempts to extend her reign'; he was ready to resign his magistracy if Lambino v. Comelec had prevailed (the people's initiative for a parliamentary shift); and he chose to 'speak out and to speak pointedly, nay harshly' rather than keeping quiet. CJP's defining assessment: 'To him, it is more important to be correct, to be honor bound than to be titled grandiosely and be damned in history.' Toast: 'Justice Antonio Tirol Carpio, may his tribe increase and may he continue to be the conscience of the nation and the nemesis of schemers and thieves.'
