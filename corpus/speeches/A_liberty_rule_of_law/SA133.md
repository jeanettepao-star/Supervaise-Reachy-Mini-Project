# The Role of the 21st Century Judge in Governance

Address delivered on February 16, 2007 by retired Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban as resource speaker during a seminar in Cebu City sponsored by the Philippine Judicial Academy and the Values for Development Foundation.

---

At the outset, allow me to thank Justice Ameurfina A. Melencio-Herrera, chancellor of the Philippine Judicial Academy, for inviting me to be the resource speaker on Democracy and Development: the Role of the 21st Century Judge in Governance. This topic is close to my heart. I believe it is a restatement of my own judicial philosophy of Liberty and Prosperity Under the Rule of Law.

SAFEGUARDING LIBERTY

Safeguarding liberty, or democracy, for that matter, has long been a traditional expectation from our courts. Their role is to be the great equalizers when individual freedoms are buffeted by the awesome powers of the State. These epic constitutional struggles between the government and its citizens are written in the annals of our nation's history.

In litigations involving civil liberties, the scales of justice should weigh heavily against the government and in favor of the people, particularly the poor, the oppressed, the marginalized, the dispossessed, and the weak. Laws and actions that restrict fundamental rights come to the courts with a heavy presumption against their validity. This policy is commonly referred to as heightened or strict scrutiny.

Consistent with this policy, the Supreme Court during my incumbency as Chief Justice promulgated three landmark decisions: one upholding the right of Congress to summon executive officials for investigations (Senate v. Ermita); one scuttling the Calibrated Preemptive Response policy and ruling in favor of the people's right to peaceful assembly (Bayan v. Ermita); and one recognizing the fundamental rights of the people under a state of national emergency (David v. Arroyo).

NURTURING PROSPERITY

While safeguarding liberty is a fairly common task for the judiciary, nurturing prosperity is something even seasoned jurists may not readily understand. Nonetheless, I maintain that the judiciary has a duty to nurture prosperity and to alleviate poverty.

Our 1987 Constitution contains several provisions involving the economic rights of our people. The Constitution commands the State to promote a just and dynamic social order that will ensure the prosperity and independence of the nation and free the people from poverty. The Constitution also mandates a more equitable distribution of opportunities, income and wealth.

In litigations affecting prosperity, development and the economy, the courts must defer as much as possible to the actions of the political branches of government, namely the Presidency and Congress. This approach is referred to as deferential interpretation of laws and executive actions. In Tanada v. Angara, the Court deferred to the wisdom of the Senate when it upheld that legislative body's consent to the Philippine ratification of the World Trade Organization Agreement. In La Bugal-B'laan Tribal Association v. Ramos, the Court affirmed the constitutionality of the Mining Law allowing 100-percent foreign investments in large-scale mining.

RULE OF LAW

The safeguarding of liberty and the nurturance of prosperity must always be planned, worked on and carried out in accordance with pre-agreed rules and procedures. What differentiates libertarianism from authoritarianism is the rule of law. No person, no matter how powerful or talented, can be above the law. Everyone, rich or poor, powerful or powerless, must follow pre-agreed rules.

EPILOGUE

To my mind, the peculiar facts and distinct circumstances of the Philippines make the formula Liberty and Prosperity Under the Rule of Law still the most viable economic and judicial philosophy here. After all, during the years of Martial Law, authoritarian rule was proven to be incapable of producing meaningful long-term economic progress. Our 1987 Constitution has imposed upon the entire judiciary the duty to strike down grave abuse of discretion committed by any branch or instrumentality of the government. By prudently exercising this constitutional duty, the 21st century judges are really pursuing their proper roles in the governance of our country.

Maraming salamat po.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Three landmark Panganiban Court decisions: (1) Senate v. Ermita -- congressional investigative power vs. executive privilege; (2) Bayan v. Ermita -- CPR policy struck down; the right to peaceful assembly maximum-tolerance standard restored; (3) David v. Arroyo -- fundamental rights protected under a state of national emergency. These three form the definitive liberty-safeguarding legacy of the Panganiban Court.

### Anecdote 2

Martial law as the negative historical proof: 'during the years of Martial Law, authoritarian rule was proven to be incapable of producing meaningful long-term economic progress' -- the most direct historical argument against authoritarianism in the corpus.

### Anecdote 3

21st century judge as governance actor: 'by prudently exercising this constitutional duty [to strike down grave abuse of discretion], the 21st century judges are really pursuing their proper roles in the governance of our country' -- the constitutional justification for judicial involvement in governance.
