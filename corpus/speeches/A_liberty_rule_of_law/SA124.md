# The Role of CPAs in Safeguarding Liberty and Nurturing Prosperity

Address delivered by retired Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban during the induction of officers of the Association of Certified Public Accountants in Public Practice, held on January 29, 2008 at the Mandarin Oriental Hotel, Makati City.

---

May I thank your President, Reynaldo S. Altre, for inviting me today to be your inducting officer and guest speaker. Having already retired from active public life, I no longer accept speaking invitations. However, your president was quite insistent. He reminded me that he and his firm have been the auditors of our small group of companies for about 40 years already, since 1968. So, I could not refuse.

I decided to speak on the role of certified public accountants in general, and of your organization, the Association of Certified Public Accountants in Public Practice in particular, in safeguarding the liberty and nurturing the prosperity of our people.

Liberty and Prosperity under the Rule of Law — this was my guiding philosophy during my more than eleven years in the Supreme Court from 1995 to 2006; ten years as associate justice and one year as Chief Justice. Now, it is still my favorite clarion call.

THE IMPERATIVES OF LIBERTY AND PROSPERITY

Immediately upon assuming office as Chief Justice, I declared my vow to lead a judiciary characterized by four Ins: integrity, independence, industry and intelligence; and one that is morally courageous to stand its ground against the onslaughts of influence, interference, and indifference.

I envisioned a judiciary that is impervious to the plague of ships: kinship, relationship, friendship and fellowship. And one that battled what I call the ACID problems of limited access to justice by the poor, corruption, incompetence and delay in the delivery of quality judgments.

This vision of a reformed judiciary and a revitalized legal profession was directed towards two loftier goals of safeguarding the liberty and nurturing the prosperity of our people under the rule of law.

THE ROLE OF COURTS AS THE GREAT EQUALIZERS

It is easy to see the role of courts as the great equalizers between the citizens and the government. The battle for civil liberties is a never-ending saga of trials and triumphs for the judiciary and for our people.

Ironically, how much lawyers know about protecting civil liberties seems to be negatively correlated with how little they have thus far devoted to nurturing the prosperity of our people. This is the reality that persists despite the Mandates of the 1987 Constitution: to alleviate the plight of the marginalized and the poor; to promote a just and dynamic social order that will ensure the prosperity and independence of the nation and free the people from poverty; and to achieve a more equitable distribution of opportunities, income and wealth.

I am truly convinced that the prosperity of our people requires as much nurturing in the present century as that accorded to liberty in the past. To be relevant, courts must be constantly attuned to the needs of the present and the vagaries of the future, so that they can respond in a timely and prudent manner to the people's ever-expanding well-being.

ACPAPP****'****S ROLE IN LIBERTY AND PROSPERITY

Professional organizations like the ACPAPP form part of what is broadly referred to as civil society. Civil society has been in the forefront of the fight for liberty. Popular action has brought down a dictatorship; it has also rejected a once populist, but later perceived to be corrupt, administration. Thus has People Power been carved into the consciousness of our citizenry: lying latent while restrained, but deadly when unleashed.

From the foregoing discussion, it is obvious that civil society in general and ACPAPP in particular assist the three great branches of government in their common effort to uplift our people from the plagues of misgovernance, tyranny, poverty and want. Specifically, it can play a significant role in promoting economic development by supporting judicial initiatives along this line.

CONCLUSION

ACPAPP as a microcosm of civil society has a big stake in ensuring the liberty and prosperity of our people. The road to attaining the twin objectives of liberty and prosperity may be long and difficult. But with your help and active participation, the vision becomes clearer and our resolve firmer. Please join me in tirelessly searching and fearlessly espousing both jobs and justice, food and freedom, investments and integrity, economics and ethics, development and democracy; in short, Liberty and Prosperity.

Maraming salamat po.
