# Visionary Leadership By Example

Keynote address delivered by retired Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban on the opening day of the 9th National Ayala Young Leaders Congress (AYLC 2007) on February 7-9, 2007, at the San Miguel Corporation Management Training Center, Alfonso, Cavite.

---

May I thank Mr. Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala, chairman and CEO of the Ayala Corporation, for inviting me to keynote the 9th National Ayala Young Leaders Congress. May I also commend the Ayala Corporation itself for launching this program in 1998 and for doggedly pursuing it during the past nine years as its strategic investment on the youth, and ultimately, in the future of this country.

I accepted this invitation to dialogue with this year's crop of well-selected campus personalities because I, too, believe that building the confidence and honing the leadership skills of our youth leaders is a worthy investment in national development.

MEMORIES OF MY OWN STUDENT DAYS

My presence here today brings back nostalgic memories of my own student days some 50 years ago, and of my own search for identity, pursuit of excellence, chase for exuberance, and quest for enduring values. I was very poor and orphaned early in life. I had nothing. No worldly treasures to keep, no reputation to protect, no close relatives to defer to.

But I organized and headed the National Union of Students of the Philippines, which was born as a rebellion against a decadent student milieu. The NUSP was founded on the proposition that the best in a person and in society can be attained only in freedom, dignity, equality and shared responsibility.

CLEAR IDEA OF GOALS AND METHODS

Every leader must have a clear idea of what ultimately he or she wants to accomplish, and of the specific ways of how to achieve the declared goals. I refer to this statement of goals, values and methods as the vision-mission of every leader or organization.

TWO ROLES OF THE CHIEF JUSTICE

The Chief Justice has two major roles: first, he is the highest magistrate of the land, the primus inter pares among the fifteen members of the Supreme Court; and second, he is the leader of the entire judicial branch of government.

Upon being inducted into office, I announced my vision: a judiciary that safeguards the liberty and nurtures the prosperity of our people under the rule of law. My mission was to lead a judiciary characterized by four Ins: independence, integrity, industry and intelligence; to reform the justice system by fighting the four ACID problems (limited access to justice by the poor, corruption, incompetence, and delay in the delivery of quality judgments); to revitalize the legal profession by breeding competent and ethical lawyers; and to grant all judicial employees maximum benefits, asking for DHL: dedication to duty, honesty in every way, and full loyalty to the Supreme Court.

SAFEGUARDING LIBERTY

In litigations involving civil liberties, the scales of justice should weigh heavily against the government and in favor of the people, particularly the poor, the oppressed, the marginalized, the dispossessed, and the weak. The Court during my incumbency promulgated three landmark decisions upholding these principles.

NURTURING PROSPERITY

While safeguarding liberty is a fairly common task for the judiciary, nurturing prosperity requires more conscious effort. In litigations affecting prosperity, development and the economy, the courts must defer as much as possible to the actions of the political branches of government.

RULE OF LAW

The safeguarding of liberty and the nurturance of prosperity must always be planned, worked on and carried out in accordance with pre-agreed rules and procedures. The rule of law differentiates democracy from the rule of the mob.

My Vision is not liberty or prosperity; it is liberty and prosperity. These twin beacons of justice must go together; we cannot sacrifice one for the other; they may otherwise be stated as freedom and food, democracy and development, ethics and economics, integrity and investments.

EPILOGUE

On his first day in office, the new chief executive of one of the top ten Philippine companies asked how many cars were assigned to his office. He was stunned to learn there were more than five dozens. He retained only one and ordered the immediate sale of the remainder. On his second day, an assistant visited with a briefcase full of cash, his personal share of advertising commissions. He ordered them deposited in the company's bank account. On his third day, he arrived at 8:00 a.m. daily without comment. In those first three days, Manuel V. Pangilinan led his subordinates in practicing prudence in spending, integrity in money matters and on-time office arrival. After a few years, the worth of PLDT multiplied tenfold and its corporate ethics, a hundred fold.

Having a clear vision and mission is an essential beginning. Leading by example is the best, if not the only, way to accomplish the vision and mission. My short answer to AYLC's crucial question of how to lead during these challenging times: visionary leadership by example.

Maraming salamat po.

## Summary

Delivered February 7, 2007 as the keynote at the 9th National Ayala Young Leaders Congress -- invited by Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala; Ayala Corporation has run AYLC since 1998 as its 'strategic investment on the youth' -- this address by CJP is the most comprehensive post-retirement statement of his vision-mission framework. He opens with a striking personal admission: 'I was very poor and orphaned early in life. I had nothing. No worldly treasures to keep, no reputation to protect, no close relatives to defer to' -- the absence of protection-worthy stakes as the source of his independence and reformist drive. He founded NUSP 'as a rebellion against a decadent student milieu... founded on the proposition that the best in a person and in society can be attained only in freedom, dignity, equality and shared responsibility.' He presents the Chief Justice's dual role (primus inter pares + leader of the judicial branch) and his full vision-mission: vision (judiciary safeguarding liberty and nurturing prosperity under the rule of law); four missions for three constituencies -- (1) four Ins (independence, integrity, industry, intelligence) for 15 justices and 2,000 judges; (2) ACID problems (access, corruption, incompetence, delay) to overcome; (3) EC (ethics and competence) to revitalize the legal profession; (4) DHL (dedication, honesty, loyalty) for 26,000 judicial employees. Three landmark Panganiban Court decisions (Senate v. Ermita, Bayan v. Ermita, David v. Arroyo) exemplify liberty safeguarding; Tanada and La Bugal exemplify deferential interpretation for prosperity. Rule of law: 'My Vision is not liberty or prosperity; it is liberty and prosperity. These twin beacons of justice must go together... freedom and food, democracy and development, ethics and economics, integrity and investments.' He closes with the MVP/PLDT three-day story (fourth telling: 60 cars kept one; briefcase cash deposited; arrived at 8 AM without comment; result: PLDT's worth multiplied tenfold and corporate ethics a hundredfold). 'Visionary leadership by example.'

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

'No worldly treasures to keep, no reputation to protect, no close relatives to defer to': CJP's most candid statement of the paradoxical advantage of being poor and orphaned early -- the absence of material and relational stakes as the source of independence.

### Anecdote 2

My Vision is 'not liberty or prosperity; it is liberty and prosperity': the governing conjunction that distinguishes CJP's philosophy from all frameworks that sacrifice one for the other -- 'freedom and food, democracy and development, ethics and economics, integrity and investments.'

### Anecdote 3

MVP/PLDT fourth telling with outcome: the only telling that precedes the tenfold/hundredfold outcome with the specific '8:00 a.m. daily' detail -- 'on his third day, he arrived at 8:00 a.m. daily without comment.'
