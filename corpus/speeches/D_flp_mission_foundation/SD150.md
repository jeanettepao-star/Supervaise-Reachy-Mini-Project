# Spreading the Gospel of Liberty and Prosperity

Keynote address delivered by Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban during the National Forum on Liberty and Prosperity, held on August 24-25, 2006, at the Manila Hotel.

---

I am extremely delighted to see all of you here today for this National Forum on Liberty and Prosperity. Over a month ago, on July 20, 2006, an academic gathering was convened at the San Beda College to mull over the scholastic foundations of the twin philosophy of Liberty and Prosperity. Today, we have invited the members of the judiciary as well as representatives of the bar, the legislative and the executive branches of government, the business community, the media, and civil society, to discuss how each of these sectors can contribute to the safeguarding of the liberty and the nurturing of the prosperity of the Filipino people.

VISIONS FOR THE JUDICIARY AND THE LEGAL PROFESSION

Upon assuming the chief justiceship of the Philippines on December 21, 2005, I immediately vowed to lead a judiciary characterized by four Ins: independence, integrity, industry and intelligence. To accomplish this vision, I pledged to continue and strengthen the Supreme Court's ongoing Action Program for Judicial Reform, with special focus on what I call the four ACID problems that corrode justice in our country. Likewise, I envisioned a revitalized legal profession — one that is responsible, dependable and morally upright; one that courageously upholds truth and justice above everything else. I asked three things from all the 26,000 judicial officials and employees all over the country: dedication to duty, honesty in every way, and loyalty to the judiciary, or DHL.

I have always been quick to point out, however, that all these visions and objectives must lead to two loftier goals of safeguarding the liberty and nurturing the prosperity of our people.

JUSTICE AND ECONOMIC GROWTH

The United Nations Development Program, the World Bank and the Asian Development Bank have announced that their goals of alleviating poverty and propelling economic growth cannot be attained, unless there is a well-functioning judicial system that enables the State to regulate the economy and empower private individuals to contribute to economic development by confidently engaging in business, investments and other transactions.

LIBERTY AND PROSPERITY AS JUDICIAL POLICY

Over the last ten years, Philippine jurisprudence is replete with examples showing this policy of dual focus. First, in cases involving liberty, the scales of justice weigh heavily against government and in favor of the people, especially the poor, the oppressed, the marginalized, the dispossessed and the weak. Laws and actions of government and its instrumentalities come to the court highly suspect in their constitutional validity, should they restrict the fundamental rights of our people. Second, in cases involving prosperity and development issues, deference is accorded to the political branches of government, namely, the Presidency and Congress.

CONCLUSION

Ladies and gentlemen, the next two days will provide you ample opportunity to identify ways of implementing, enhancing and strengthening the twin beacons of Liberty and Prosperity within your respective spheres of influence. The results of this conference will lay the groundwork for the forthcoming Global Conference on Liberty and Prosperity scheduled on October 18-20, 2006. During that global gathering, we hope to spread the gospel of Liberty and Prosperity beyond our shores and unto the world.

Maraming salamat po!

## Summary

Delivered August 25, 2006 as the opening address at the National Forum on Liberty and Prosperity -- the second domestic forum, preceded by the July 20, 2006 academic forum at San Beda; this National Forum brings together judiciary, bar, legislative, executive, business, media and civil society -- this address by CJP restates his full vision-mission: four Ins (independence, integrity, industry, intelligence); APJR with special focus on ACID; revitalized legal profession (responsible, dependable, morally upright); EC (competent and ethical lawyers); DHL (dedication, honesty, loyalty for 26,000 judicial employees); and the governing hierarchy: 'all these visions and objectives must lead to two loftier goals of safeguarding the liberty and nurturing the prosperity of our people.' He presents the UNDP/WB/ADB three-way consensus: 'poverty alleviation and economic growth cannot be attained, unless there is a well-functioning judicial system that enables the State to regulate the economy and empower private individuals to contribute to economic development.' The dual judicial standard: strict scrutiny for liberty (scales weigh heavily against government, favor the poor and marginalized) and deferential interpretation for prosperity (defer to Presidency and Congress). He closes by pointing to the coming October 18-20 Global Conference: 'The results of this conference will lay the groundwork for the forthcoming Global Conference on Liberty and Prosperity. During that global gathering, we hope to spread the gospel of Liberty and Prosperity beyond our shores and unto the world.'
