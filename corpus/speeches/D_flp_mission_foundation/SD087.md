# To a Higher Level

Transcript of the extemporaneous Closing Remarks of retired Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban after Dean Sedfrey Candelaria's Public Lecture as the holder in the Ateneo de Manila of the CJ Panganiban Professorial Chair on Liberty and Prosperity, held on September 19, 2012, at Justitia Room, 4th Floor, Ateneo Law School, Rockwell, Makati City.

---

Maraming salamat po Dean Candelaria, sa inyong napakalalim nguni't kaaya-ayang pagpapaliwanag. The Bible says man does not live by bread alone. And I add, neither can he live without it. So here is the check for one hundred thousand pesos for your professorial chair.

Really, I am very pleased that we started our Chief Justice Panganiban Professorial Lecture Series with Dean Candelaria who set a very high standard. Incidentally, I wanted to study in Ateneo when I was a young man. But we were so poor, my parents could hardly afford to send us to school. That is why I had to peddle newspapers and shine shoes when I was a young boy. And when I was in college, my parents died, both of them. My father did not even graduate high school; he was a clerk in the Bureau of Land Management. My mother did not finish elementary school. We were four siblings and I was the youngest.

We were living in Cataluna Street in Sampaloc, Manila and my first ambition was to enroll at the University of the Philippines for my college degree. I finished high school at the Mapa High School. My classmates there and I used to go to Diliman. We were looking at the Oblation and we promised one another we would study hard so that we would be given scholarships at the University of the Philippines. Well, my classmates enrolled there after our high school. We were all granted scholarships, but I could not enjoy mine because my parents could not afford the bus fare, the fifteen-centavo ride at that time, between Cataluna Street in Sampaloc, Manila to the UP campus in Diliman, Quezon City.

Chief Justice Davide, my esteemed predecessor in the Supreme Court, is here. He was Chief Justice for seven years before me. And I learned plenty from him. For one thing, he instituted a long-range judicial reform program called Action Program for Judicial Reform in which he asked me to assist. That reform program was so successful that the World Bank placed it in its website, as a model for all developing countries that wanted World Bank assistance. Unfortunately, our successors in the Court did not find it good enough to be continued.

Hopefully, the incumbent Chief Justice, who holds an economics degree from Ateneo and a law degree from UP, will continue and update that program of Chief Justice Davide.

I also greet the faculty and students of Ateneo de Manila. As I said, I have always wanted to enroll here but the best I could do was to be guest speaker during your graduation ceremony in 2006. At that time, I said, One of the graduates here will surely be a bar topnotcher during my term as Chief Justice. And true enough, I think three Ateneo graduates, during that year, were in the top ten during the bar exam of that year. And the number one topnotcher was a lady, Joan de Venecia.

Let me now respond to our lecturer. With his scholarly discourse, Dean Candelaria has raised the standard of our lecture series that we hope to have in all major law schools in the Philippines. He immediately lifted the issue of liberty and prosperity to the international plane.

I was thinking, lawyers should not only be interested in themselves, not even only in their professions, but likewise in our people, in our country; to safeguard the liberty of our people is a given, to see to it that their civil and political rights, as well as their human rights, are observed strictly. We safeguard the liberty of our people by the judiciary's strict scrutiny of the actions of government.

However, in matters of the economy, which we lawyers do not really understand except perhaps for Dean Candelaria, we should defer to the political branches of government because the political branches are composed of officials elected by the people. They are charged with the duty of freeing people from disease, from poverty, from disability. And so therefore, we lawyers, and we in the judiciary, must defer as much as possible to these elected representatives on matters that involve the economy.

Before I graduated from the Supreme Court, I convened the Global Forum on Liberty and Prosperity as my valedictory conference held on October 18-20, 2006. I invited justices and lawyers from all over the world, 300 of them, to the Philippines. We discussed liberty and prosperity on a global basis. Canadian Chief Justice Beverley McLachlin came from her station in Ottawa to the Philippines. She was enamored by the theme of liberty and prosperity that she said, Please add the phrase under the rule of law to the theme. So I agreed to change the title of my legal philosophy to Liberty and Prosperity under the Rule of Law.

She intended to stay for only three days but she fell in love with the Philippines, especially with Ateneo de Manila, that she stayed four more days. And Ateneo de Manila appreciated her so much, Ateneo gave her a Doctoral Degree in Law, Honoris Causa. So you have a co-alumnus, the Chief Justice of Canada by the name of Beverley McLachlin, who holds a doctoral degree from the Ateneo de Manila.

Dean Candelaria, as I said, spoke of liberty and prosperity in the international sense and we will not go into that deeply. Suffice it to say that I am thankful to all of you for coming today and for gracing this occasion. Maraming maraming salamat po.

## Summary

Delivered September 19, 2012 as the closing remarks at the inaugural lecture of the FLP Chief Justice Panganiban Professorial Chair Series -- the first lecture delivered by Dean Sedfrey M. Candelaria at Ateneo de Manila Professional Schools -- this brief address by CJP opens with the sharpest honorarium presentation in the corpus: 'The Bible says man does not live by bread alone. And I add, neither can he live without it. So here is the check for one hundred thousand pesos for your professorial chair.' He notes that Dean Candelaria 'set a very high standard' and 'immediately lifted the issue of liberty and prosperity to the international plane.' He reflects on his lifelong Ateneo aspiration ('too poor; had to peddle newspapers and shine shoes; both parents died in college'), noting he finally got to speak at the 2006 Ateneo graduation where he predicted a bar topnotcher -- Joan de Venecia became number 1 and three Ateneo graduates made the top ten. CJ Hilario Davide Jr. is present; CJP credits him with the APJR ('so successful the World Bank placed it in its website as a model for all developing countries') and notes with regret that 'our successors in the Court did not find it good enough to be continued'; he expresses hope that incumbent CJ Sereno ('holds an economics degree from Ateneo and a law degree from UP') will continue and update it. He briefly restates his judicial philosophy (strict scrutiny for civil liberties; deferential interpretation for economic matters) and describes the Global Forum (October 2006) where CJ Beverley McLachlin suggested adding 'Under the Rule of Law' -- then 'fell in love with the Philippines, especially with Ateneo de Manila, that she stayed four more days; Ateneo gave her a Doctoral Degree in Law, Honoris Causa. So you have a co-alumnus, the Chief Justice of Canada by the name of Beverley McLachlin.'
