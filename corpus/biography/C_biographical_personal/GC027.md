# Chapter 7: Lawyer in the Marketplace

Chapter 7 covers the twenty-three-year stretch (1963-1986) of Art's life as a private lawyer in the marketplace. After three years apprenticing with Salonga, he opened his own firm with the dean's blessing, taught law at two schools (Sampaloc university-belt and an exclusive convent), and pursued worldly success driven by what the chapter names as 'the deprivations of poverty' as 'an unhealed wound': the explicit goal 'to become prosperous and be able to control the lives of other people,' to ensure his five children would never know the deprivations he had. In 1967 he founded Baron Travel Corporation with 5,000 pesos of his own participation, 100,000 from friends, and 20,000 from his father-in-law Joe Carpio -- total ₱105,000 capital that he kept by buying back every benefactor's shares at book value 'but never less than par.' The travel agency, after a money-losing start, became successful, drawing Art into the Philippine tourism leadership network: Benigno Toda Jr. (PAL), Alfredo Montelibano (Sheraton), Antonio Delgado (Hilton), Enrique Zobel (InterContinental), John Gokongwei (the Padre Faura/Pedro Gil land that became Robinsons Place Manila). The international summit came at the 1985 Rome convention of the American Society of Travel Agents (ASTA -- 20,000 members, 128 countries), where on November 15 he was elected chairman of the International Division, the first and only Filipino, the first Asian to hold the post: 'I'd like to believe that my election is really a triumph of the American dream where everyone, even the lowliest in society, can aspire for and be given the opportunity to lead, to serve and to succeed.' Parallel directorships of Vicente Tan's three companies -- Continental Bank, Overseas Insurance Corporation, Pentagon Mines Inc. -- ended in martial-law catastrophe when General Fabian Ver closed them in 1973-1974 'with neither due compensation nor due process,' and Tan himself was detained for years on the false suspicion of being a 'front for Communist China,' never recovering his wealth. Art then explored Hong Kong, where Mao's Cultural Revolution had catalyzed an industrialization-into-financial-hub trajectory; as counsel to a rising conglomerate he amassed $2 million in Hang Seng shares ($20M in today's money) and used the dividends to acquire a 10-acre, 182-townhouse Texas property in the South Central US growth corridor for $1.35 million, valued at $5M+. He lived the imagined glory: first-class travel, Rolls Royce limousines, Piaget and Patek Philippe watches gifted by the conglomerate's watch-collecting owner, society-pages photographs, high-society gossip fodder, half the year abroad. But the polarity was felt acutely: 'no peace and quiet ... no fulfillment in the overindulgence of his appetite and ego ... alienation from his wife and children.' The double catastrophe came in pairs: Margaret Thatcher's 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration announcing the 1997 return of Hong Kong to China sent the Hang Seng down >20% and bankrupted the conglomerate (with reported trader suicides); the Mexican peso devaluation collapsed Texas real estate, the mostly-Mexican tenants left, three months of missed mortgage payments triggered automatic bank foreclosure with no right to repurchase. 'He felt dismally poor again.' Leni's preference for the Philippines decided the next move -- Art declined the US-recovery option and returned home, joining the Rotary Club of Manila in 1983 (the same year as Major General Fidel V. Ramos, the Chief of the Philippine Constabulary who would become President in 1992 and appoint Art to the Supreme Court in 1995). The civic resurrection led to RCM presidency in 1990; at the Manila Hotel Fiesta Pavilion induction Art declared 'our most important commitment is for the Manila Rotary to be for the poor, the afflicted and the weak ... the *raison d'etre*, the reason for being, of Manila Rotary - to help those who cannot help themselves' -- quoting Matthew 25:40 explicitly: 'whenever we do these acts of charity and love for the needy, we really do them for God.' Director roles at PCCI and MAP, the 1986 EDSA Revolution restoring his many friends to top government posts under Cory Aquino, and his law practice 'picked up and flourished' -- closing the chapter at the threshold of the spiritual conversion that the next chapter would describe.
