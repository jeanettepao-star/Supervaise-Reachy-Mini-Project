# Chapter 12: Guided by the Word

Chapter 12 narrates two episodes in which Art was 'guided by the Word' through Scripture readings that decisively shaped his judicial conduct. The first is the January 2001 EDSA II constitutional crisis. After the impeachment trial's 11-10 vote not to open the envelope linking President Joseph Estrada to a multi-million peso secret bank account, cabinet officials resigned en masse, the Armed Forces of the Philippines and Philippine National Police defected to Vice-President Gloria Macapagal Arroyo by the weekend of January 19, EDSA crowds began preparing to march toward Estrada supporters massed at Mendiola, and Cardinal Sin made earnest appeals through Monsignor Socrates Villegas to prevent a bloodbath. Art monitored the unfolding situation on television, sleeping at 1:00 AM and waking at 4:00 AM. Praying through the scenarios -- Estrada could not avert violence without military support, GMA could not legally take over with a sitting president, and the absence of action would invite a coup that would dissolve all constitutional offices including the Supreme Court -- Art concluded that the only constitutional path was for GMA to be sworn in immediately. The day's Gospel reading was Mark 3:20-22, in which Jesus's family says 'he is out of his mind' and the scribes say 'he is possessed by Beelzebub' -- a scriptural authorization, Art read, for proposals that initially sound mad. He called Chief Justice Hilario Davide, who had been up since 3:30 AM at his customary scripture reading, meditating on Isaiah 62's 'Restoration of Zion' and already sensing he must act to restore normalcy. Davide was receptive: 'Each was affirming a deep sense that their mandate was divinely inspired,' and 'both men were going beyond the normal course of things, taking extraordinary steps to save the Constitution and the country they loved and served.' Davide called an emergency session of the Supreme Court -- 12 of 15 justices attended, the rest out of town -- and after animated constitutional debate they agreed to support GMA's noon oath-taking on the condition she submit a written request. All present magistrates attended the oath-taking; Davide administered. Art's role was 'not widely known' -- by deliberate choice. The same discretion describes Art's wider judicial conduct: he 'shunned company to avoid being misconstrued as fraternizing or conspiring with litigants and their relations.' The second episode is Art's visit to the National Bilibid Prison in Muntinlupa. Invited but cautioned against by Supreme Court security -- the facility housed persons convicted of serious crimes, some sentenced by the Court itself -- Art was decided by Matthew 25:36 ('I was in prison and you came to visit me'). 'Better to risk the ire of the inmates than to risk the ire of the Lord.' Arriving via Sampaguita Road to a maximum-security building surrounded by hectares of fruit-bearing trees, he was met by 500 hardened inmates with a band playing 'Mabuhay,' the national anthem, hip-hop and folk dances, Sinatra / Joni James / Johnny Mathis covers, and a comedy skit on jail life. He opened bravely: 'How many among you have I personally sentenced to life imprisonment or to death?' About 30 hands went up; whistle calls. 'How many feel wrongly sentenced?' No hands. He spoke extemporaneously in Tagalog of hope, distinguishing the inmates from those 'imprisoned by greed' (the anxious rich), 'imprisoned by illness' (the terminally ill), and 'imprisoned by their minds' (the insane and feeble-minded) -- those without recourse, while the inmates could look forward to commutation or pardon. Yellow handwritten pardon-pleas were pressed into his hand at the exit. One life-termer convicted of syndicated estafa asked him in the vernacular: weren't all commandments summed up in 'love God and love fellowmen' -- so why had Art, a good Catholic, condemned him to life when the confessor had asked only for the rosary? On the twilight ride home and through a night of study, Art rereread Matthew 25 -- and found Christ himself separating sheep from goats and sending some to 'eternal punishment.' He concluded that God is a God of justice as much as a God of love, that 'just as much as there existed human and physical laws, so too existed spiritual laws,' that both temporal and divine codes permit reparation through 'repentance, restitution and reformation,' and that 'fulfilling his role in dispensing justice was one avenue for the lost to find a way back to the Father.' Art was at peace. He said a prayer of thanks, turned off the light, retired, and slept soundly that night.
