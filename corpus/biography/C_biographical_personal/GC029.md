# Chapter 9: Among Bishops, Cardinals and a Pope

Chapter 9 traces how Art's renewed Catholic faith found expression in Church leadership through a chain of invitations from Henrietta 'Tita' de Villa, then president of the Council of the Laity of the Philippines. Beginning as Special Minister of Holy Communion at his San Antonio parish in Makati, where his voracious Bible reading and study of papal encyclicals became part of his 'vocabulary,' Art was first invited to give a testimonial at the Laity Week assembly during the Feast of San Lorenzo Ruiz -- where his 'unabashed' declaration of Christ astonished Tita and convinced her he belonged in the Council. Through Tita he became a lay participant in the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines (PCP II, January-February 1991), called by CBCP President Archbishop Leonardo Zamora Legazpi as the first plenary council where lay faithful voted (consultatively) before the bishops voted (deliberatively), with both votes always concurring -- what Tita called 'a time of grace' and the chapter calls a 'historic breaking away from tradition.' For over a month Art conversed with Catholic Church leaders, including Cardinal Sin. After PCP II, Tita recruited him as national vice-chairperson and chief legal counsel of the newly founded Parish Pastoral Council for Responsible Voting (PPCRV), the non-partisan, non-sectarian, non-profit volunteer organization launched in October 1991 by Tita, Cardinal Sin, Comelec Commissioner Haydee Yorac, Bishop Gabriel Reyes, Bayani Valenzuela, and 30 other lay leaders, growing to 300,000+ members nationwide after CBCP endorsement. Art publicly defended the Church's involvement in the 1992 elections, tracing the line from the CBCP's 1986 Joint Pastoral Exhortation on the Snap Elections -- Cardinal Vidal's call to 'vote into office the persons you believe God wants you to put into office' and the post-election condemnation of 'unparalleled' fraud -- through Vatican II, PCP II, and three subsequent pastoral letters, distinguishing legitimate non-partisan electoral integrity work from impermissible partisan endorsement. In 1996, when Tita was appointed Philippine ambassador to the Holy See and asked Cardinal Sin's approval to nominate Art as her replacement to the Vatican's Pontifical Council for the Laity, Sin instantly agreed -- and Art served from 1996 to 2002 as the lone Filipino member of the dicastery, also acting as executive secretary of the 1995 Papal Visit and World Youth Day Manila by designing the Central Committee (CBCP-led) / Operations Committee (Cardinal Sin-led) structure that resolved the organizational impasse. Tita's read of his contribution -- 'Art explains the law in a way that is informative, educative and readable' -- would prove diagnostic of his later judicial style. The chapter closes with Art's address to the Dominican-Siena Schools National Convention articulating, on doctrinal authority (Lumen Gentium 31, Christifideles Laici 15, PCP II 96), that the lay vocation has a 'secular character' making 'politics, government, business, industry and finance' the proper terrain of Christian witness -- words the biography labels 'prophetic' for the road about to unfold, a road leading to 'one of the most painful and purifying episodes of his life' through one person from the politically charged environment he had steered away from.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Laity Week testimonial: Tita's stunned reaction to Art 'unabashedly declaring himself for God,' calling all present 'sinners' whose salvation is 'only through the mercy of God,' and her instant conclusion that he should be in the Council of the Laity

### Anecdote 2

Archbishop Legazpi's PCP II directive: 'Usually, in a Plenary Council, only the ordained participated. This time, he wanted to include laypersons' -- modeled on Vatican II inviting lay auditors

### Anecdote 3

PCP II's procedural innovation: consultative votes (lay) cast FIRST, then deliberate votes (hierarchy) -- 'the outcomes of both were always in agreement'

### Anecdote 4

PPCRV launch October 1991 -- ~1000 Manila Archdiocese laity attend; expansion to 300,000+ nationwide within a month after CBCP support

### Anecdote 5

Art's published article defending Church engagement: traces the line from CBCP's 1986 Joint Pastoral Exhortation through Cardinal Vidal's 'vote into office the persons you believe God wants you to put into office' to the post-election condemnation of 'unparalleled' fraud

### Anecdote 6

Cardinal Sin's instant approval of Art as Tita's PCL replacement -- 'He knew Art by then, from his participation in the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines in 1991'

### Anecdote 7

1995 Papal Visit organizational design: Art creates the Central Committee (CBCP-led) / Operations Committee (Cardinal Sin-led) structure that resolves Tita's repeated failures to chart a working organization

### Anecdote 8

Dominican-Siena Schools convention: Art's prophetic articulation that the lay vocation has a 'secular character' making politics, government, business, industry and finance the proper terrain -- words that would describe his Supreme Court appointment before it happened

### Anecdote 9
