# Chapter 2: Return to the City - Boyhood Years

Chapter 2 follows the post-war family return from Candaba to Sampaloc, Manila, and the formation of CJP's working-student identity from roughly age 9 to 18. After a brief stint as a bootblack, Dayunior pursued newspaper-selling on Cataluña Street with the enterprise that would mark his whole life: building a wooden *cariton* to carry more copies, instituting a credit-and-collect system to beat other boys to the doors, and tracking the 2- or 3-centavo commissions on the 10- or 15-centavo dailies (Manila Times, Manila Chronicle, Philippine Herald, Manila Bulletin in the mornings; Evening News, Daily Mirror in the afternoons). Through years of sleeping on the sidewalk near the news dealer's apartment to get first crack at delivery trucks -- and one near-fatal jeepney fall on España and Lerma Streets that a 6x6 military truck nearly turned terminal -- he supplied his own school money, supported his family in small ways, and built the *alkansya* he would keep into professional life as a trophy. In a diary on the cold pavement he wrote 'Today I am just a newsboy. Someday, I will be president of the Manila Times' -- a line redeemed in March 1991 when he became president of the Philippine Daily Inquirer, and again in 2011 when Marixi Prieto invited him back as a regular Sunday columnist; he later donated an entire year's professional fee to the Inquirer Newsboys Foundation. Schooling alternated with the route: Juan Luna Elementary School (where Grade IV's strict Mrs. Isabel Fajardo and her ruler 'could be credited with my being disciplined the rest of my life'), then Victorino Mapa High School from 1950 to 1954 -- one of four Manila public high schools named after Filipino jurists (Arellano, Araullo, Mapa, Torres), the largest with over 6,000 students, with classmates like future Senate President / 9th VP Arturo M. Tolentino, SGV co-founder Washington SyCip, and future SC justice and Comelec chair Jose Melo -- where Dayunior graduated Honorable Mention in the top 10 of about 1,000 seniors. He earned a UP full scholarship but could not afford the 15-centavo daily bus fare to Diliman; he tried for a UST scholarship but lost it to the third Holy Trinity question, replying 'Jesus, Mary and Joseph' to the old Dominican priest. FEU sat five minutes and two streets away from Cataluña, the walking-distance fallback that would become the pivot of his life. The chapter closes with his father's discovery of 'chemical engineer' in the yearbook 'Ambition' entry and the gravely-spoken request: that the son who carried Artemio Sr.'s name pursue law instead -- 'so he would truly live in me.' Dayunior agreed with a two-year escape clause, not knowing that Temi would die during his first pre-law year and that the filial vow would become irrevocable.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Building the wooden *cariton* and inventing the slip-under-door-then-return-for-payment credit system to outrun other newsboys -- small-business enterprise at 12 or 13

### Anecdote 2

The morning treat ritual after 8 a.m. sales: sweetened *saba* bananas with ice (5 centavos) at the corner Chinese sari-sari store, splurging on Alpine or Carnation milk (7 centavos) on a good day

### Anecdote 3

Near-fatal jeepney jump on España/Lerma -- climbing the rear platform to collect change, falling when the light turned green, a 6x6 military truck nearly crushing him, lying flat under the wheelbase to clear it; 'I cheated death and swore never to jump from a running vehicle again'

### Anecdote 4

Lying awake on the cold pavement near the newspaper-dealer's apartment, comparing himself to schoolmates sleeping in beds, then writing in his diary: 'Today I am just a newsboy. Someday, I will be president of the Manila Times'

### Anecdote 5

Building a radio set in his third year of high school with money partly from aunt Arsenia and partly from newsboy earnings, then giving the finished radio to her in gratitude -- the gesture that opened him to 'the joy of giving'

### Anecdote 6

The *alkansya* (coin bank) -- CJP kept it as a trophy long into professional life, showing it to wife and children as the artifact of accomplishment

### Anecdote 7

Mrs. Isabel Fajardo's ruler at Juan Luna Grade IV -- 'she would use to tap our hands when they were dirty or to whip our buttocks when we did not study well' -- and her son Ernie reconnected with CJP through Yupangco neighbors in retirement

### Anecdote 8

The UP Oblation pledge with Mapa classmates -- promising to study hard for UP admission

### Anecdote 9

The UST Dominican interview -- third question 'Name the three persons in one God,' wrong answer 'Jesus, Mary and Joseph,' scholarship lost

### Anecdote 10

Father's yearbook discovery and the law-career conversation: 'I was a *pilosopo* who tended to argue,' the agreement with the two-year escape clause that would be foreclosed by Temi's death
