# Chapter 10: Appointment to the Supreme Court

Chapter 10 chronicles the arduous and ultimately providential road to Art's appointment as Justice of the Supreme Court of the Philippines. After the 1986 transition, Senator Jovito R. Salonga -- Art's law-school dean turned three-time poll-topping Senate President -- asked his protégé to take over as personal counsel of the Upper House upon Justice Abraham Sarmiento's elevation to the Supreme Court. Concurrently counsel of the Liberal Party, Art served Salonga from 1987 to 1991 and absorbed the motto that would become his own: things money cannot buy -- honor, dignity, ethics, character and excellence. When Salonga lost the 1992 presidential race to General Fidel V. Ramos, the new chief executive -- Art's Rotary Club of Manila co-member -- invited him to dinner at Ambassador Henrietta de Villa's home and at a subsequent one-hour private meeting in Makati offered him Secretary of Justice. Art declined on three grounds: he had voted for Salonga, the PPCRV's political neutrality could be damaged, and Leni had made political office a marriage-contracted veto. He consulted the CBCP, where Archbishop Legazpi feared reputational damage and Bishop Bacani saw strategic value, before Leni's clinching line -- 'we already agreed when we got married many years ago that you could become anything, anything at all, except a politician' -- decided the matter. Ramos, amused ('Ander de saya ka pala...dalawa na tayo'), pivoted to offer the non-political Supreme Court seat instead. But the road to the bench was not direct. Article VIII Section 8 of the 1987 Constitution had created the Judicial and Bar Council precisely to prevent arbitrary presidential picks, and Chief Justice Andres R. Narvasa as JBC chair adamantly opposed Art's nomination -- defending the council's independence even while acknowledging Art's qualification. Across seven Supreme Court vacancies from 1992 to 1994, Art's name was submitted to the JBC and seven times he was rejected: appellate justices were given priority, a former client surfaced with a 25-year-old complaint, and the 'others more qualified' rationale was repeatedly cited. Art wept before God, asking 'How long, O God, must I bear these frustrations and the embarrassment of being repeatedly rejected?' but clung to Romans 8:28. In January 1995, broken in spirit, he surrendered: 'Take, oh Lord, all that I am, all that I will ever be. Take my personal ambition, my liberty, my career. Do what you will with them and I will follow whatever You want me to do.' Six months later, after Justice Camilo Quiason's retirement opened the eighth vacancy, Justice Jose A.R. Melo prodded Art to try once more. At 1:00 AM on Saturday July 29, 1995, Art woke and prayed two hours, falling asleep at 4:00 AM without a sign. The next morning at parish Mass, with eyes closed imagining the ascending Jesus, he heard Luke 11:1-11 read -- 'Ask and you shall receive, seek and you shall find, knock and the door shall be opened to you' -- and broke into cold sweat, gasping internally, 'My Lord, are you asking me to persist in my quest?' A week later on August 6, the transfiguration Gospel (Luke 9:28-36) gave the confirmation he had asked for: 'This is my Son, my Chosen One. Listen to Him.' He resubmitted his name. On Wednesday August 9, leading the BLD South Sector prayer meeting at St. James School Sucat with Leni, he publicly declared his discernment. On September 13 the JBC nearly excluded him; one unnamed member's plea -- 'let Panganiban's name stay in the list in the meantime' -- kept him in. On September 20, 'by some mysterious reason, no one objected.' For the first time in three years he passed. Ramos called: 'How did you do it?' -- 'Well, sir, I didn't do it. The Lord did. Hallelujah!' Two weeks of intercession by BLD, the Carmelites and Pink Sisters followed before the presidential appointment call: 'I am the Lord's instrument. He has chosen you, and I therefore appoint you justice of the Supreme Court.' On October 18, 1995, before the BLD assembly, Art declared that 'all my actions and all my decisions will only be in accordance with His commandments and His gospel,' pledging to serve 'with fortitude, integrity, competence and prudence.'

### Anecdote 1

### Anecdote 2
