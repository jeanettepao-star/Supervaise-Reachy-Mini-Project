# Chapter 10: Appointment to the Supreme Court

__Chapter 10__

*“For I know the plans I have for you,” declares the Lord, “plans to *

*prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future*__*\.” Jeremiah 29:11*__

__Appointment to the Supreme Court __

One year after the transition of power from the dictatorship to the democratic government under President Corazon C\. Aquino, Art was invited by his mentor Jovito Salonga to serve as Counsel of the Senate\. Salonga had been elected as senator in the 1987 elections and topped the polls for the third time\. He had run twice before and in both times garnered the most votes among all the senatorial candidates\. This lawyer, lawmaker and defender of human rights was highly esteemed by his colleagues in the Upper House and voted him to the position of Senate President\. He, in turn, looked to his protégé to join him\. “It was indisputable evidence of my high regard for him,” Senator Salonga said\. “The work involved rendering written opinions on controversial legal issues before the Senate and handling cases in court\.” 

Aside from his abilities as a lawyer, Salonga perceived what he described as “a radical change in Art in terms of outlook, his sense of

values, his insights, even his conversation\.” He wrote this observation in the Preface of “Love God, Serve Man,” a collection of Art’s speeches and papers published in 1994 by the *Philippine Daily Inquirer*\. “Evidently, he had undergone a profound transformation that I had not noticed 

before\. This must have been one additional reason, entirely apart from his legal background, why I relied on him for advice on some legal and political problems that confronted us in the Senate, after my election to that body in 1987\.” 

Salonga was not the first to extend an invitation to Art to join the government\. Then Executive Secretary Rafael Salas had asked him in the

late 1960s to serve as a technical assistant in Malacañang or a judge\. The offer was politely declined\. Art’s main consideration was financial\. Compensation of public officials was not sufficient to sustain the education of his children; much less, provide for his family’s needs\. He could not forget his own financial woes as a student and did not want to

put his children through the difficulties he went through\. 

__Counsel to Mentor __

These were no longer his concerns when Senate President Salonga

asked him to be his personal adviser as well as legal adviser to the Upper House of Congress in the bicameral legislature of the Philippines\. “In my professional life as a lawyer, I was being given by him,  unconsciously I am sure, the greatest honor I had received so far\.  And he was doing so, not by awarding me a plaque of gold or conferring on me an honorary degree, but by asking me to take over as his personal legal counsel upon the appointment to the Supreme Court of his then lawyer, Justice Abraham Sarmiento …\.” Later in his address delivered on July 16, 2016, during the Third Philippine Conference on New Evangelization at the University of Santo Tomas Quadricentennial Pavilion, Art shared that “\[n\]othing is more exhilarating than to be counsel of one’s former mentor\.” 

Salonga was a giant throughout Art’s life and career\. Art would repeatedly acknowledge his learnings from the man who had taken him under his wing\. “I can write volumes about him and speak endlessly of my gratitude, but let me sum up in one sentence his foremost teaching: While it is desirable to have some of the necessities that money can buy – like adequate food on the table, sufficient clothing, sports, and recreation for the body, and even a car and a house for the family – we should never ever forget that it is far more important to aspire even more ardently for things that money cannot buy, like honor, dignity, ethics, character and excellence\.” 

Art became the personal legal counsel of Senate President Salonga and concurrently counsel of the Liberal Party, of which Salonga was president\. He served in these positions for four years from 1987 to 1991\. The following year, Salonga ran for the highest office in the land and intimated to his protégé to prepare for the possibility of becoming his executive secretary should he succeed in his bid to become president\. It was not meant to be, as Salonga lost to General Fidel V\. Ramos who became the 12th President of the Philippine Republic\. 

__A President’s Offer __

While Art did not support the candidacy of Ramos, they were in fact friends\. Both had joined the Rotary Club of Manila \(RCM\) at the same time when then General Ramos was Chief of the Philippine Constabulary\. Their association and interaction in the RCM fostered a congenial relationship and, more importantly, engendered in Ramos an appreciation of Art’s capabilities as a leader\. 

That appreciation became evident when FVR, as the President was widely known, extended an invitation to him to join the Cabinet\. It was during a dinner hosted by Ambassador Henrietta de Villa that FVR first broached the idea\. The newly elected Chief Executive was a Protestant and was hoping to enlist Catholic lay leaders like Art and Ambassador De Villa to help him run the government\. 

A few weeks after FVR was declared winner of the electoral exercise, he called Art to ask him to meet with him in his private office in Makati City\. The hour\-long meeting between just the two of

4

 

them was a discussion of the efficacy of the rule of law in Philippine society, the role of the courts and the expectations of the people from their newly elected head of state\. It was the preamble to President\-elect Ramos’s primary agenda\. He again offered Art a seat on his Cabinet as Secretary of Justice\. 

Art was definitive in his response and respectfully declined\. He explained that he had neither campaigned nor voted for him and forthrightly declared that his vote had been cast for Salonga\. He continued to say that since he did not support Ramos’s bid, he did not deserve to be part of the official family\. Besides, Art added, his wife Leni did not want him to be involved in a political job\. 

Ramos countered with a characteristic retort, “I know you did not campaign for me\. You did not even vote for me\. You voted for your mentor, Jovy Salonga\. But that’s all right\. I do not want to be the president only of those who voted for me\. I want to be president of all Filipinos\. Anyway, I have known you a long time and I know you will serve with competence and integrity\.” He had seen firsthand how Art had conducted himself as president of their Rotary Club and as national vice\-chairman of the Parish Pastoral Council for Responsible Voting \(PPCRV\)\. Ramos gave Art two weeks to reconsider his refusal\.

He went into a period of prayer, reflection and consultation with, 

5 

among others, the leaders of the Catholic Bishops’ Conference of the Philippines\. They were also the spiritual advisers of the PPCRV\. Two different positions were taken by the membership\. 

The group led by Archbishop Legazpi was concerned that Art’s  acceptance could adversely reflect on the PPCRV and the Catholic Church\. There was a possibility that certain quarters would cast suspicion on the political neutrality of PPCRV\. The appointment might give rise to the suspicion that the Commission on Elections’ citizens’ arm had been secretly working for erstwhile presidential candidate Ramos\. 

Bishop Ted Bacani, however, felt that accepting FVR’s offer would be a good way to show the new Protestant president that the Catholic laity was ready to help him achieve his announced goal of promoting the welfare of the Filipino people\. It also strategically placed Art in proximity to the head of state to oppose any possible anti\-Catholic initiatives–an unfounded fear, given that FVR was above religious tugs\-of\-war\. 

Ultimately, both groups agreed that the decision was his to make\. Art consulted Leni whose answer gave him the clarity to decide\. She said, “We already agreed when we got married many years ago that you 

could become anything, anything at all, except a politician because I 

6 

cannot play the role of a politician’s wife\.” 

Art reverted to the president, affirming his earlier decision\. He respectfully declined the unsolicited offer and explained that while the esteemed bishops were split in their opinions, his wife was absolutely against it\. The president smiled amusingly and replied, *“Ander de saya ka pala…dalawa na tayo\.*  \(I see you are henpecked\. That makes two of us\)\.” Ramos then countered and offered him a position Art could not refuse\. He said, “Please help me just the same by joining the Supreme Court\.” It was a nonpolitical position and an offer that set the lawyer on course to the highest court of the land without having to climb the judicial ladder\. 

__The Arduous Journey to the Supreme Court __

Somewhere in most, if not all, lawyers’ minds is a vision of wearing the long, black robes as a sitting magistrate in the august halls of the Supreme Court\. The generous offer of President Ramos certainly appealed to Art\. However, it did not sit well with members of the judiciary\.  In 1987 the Judicial and Bar Council of the Philippines was constitutionally mandated to recommend appointees to the judiciary and the Office of the Ombudsman for the President’s consideration\. The JBC was conceived to attain and preserve the independence of

7 

the judiciary, and no Philippine head of state could arbitrarily choose to fill any vacancy in the local courts\. The 1987 Philippine Constitution \(Article VIII Section 8\), which had created the Judicial and Bar Council, tasked the JBC to screen nominees for judicial appointments\. The council found it improper for President Ramos to express his choice before it had submitted their list of nominees to him\. Chief Justice Andres R\. Narvasa was the sitting JBC chairperson\. He vehemently opposed Art’s nomination to convey the independence of the Council to President Ramos\. The conservative jurist made it clear that the JBC could not be influenced\. He was adamant and uncompromising despite his personal friendship with Art, who he admitted was qualified for the post\. 

It was a painful and overextended journey that spanned three years of being interviewed by the Council and then getting rejected\. There were seven vacancies created in the Supreme Court from 1992 to 1994 due to the retirement of the incumbents\. Each time there was a 

vacancy, Art’s name was submitted for consideration and each time he was vetted\. Objections raised against him alleged insufficiency of professional competence and purported sins and wrongdoings\. A former client suddenly surfaced to lodge a complaint with the JBC about the supposed mishandling of a case that happened 25 years earlier\.  To his mind, Art had sufficiently responded to the charges 

8 

against him but he was still bypassed in favor of appellate justices\. It was pointed out that while he might be qualified, there were others more so\. 

__The Humbling __

The practicing lawyer became increasingly discouraged\. Friends like Sonny and Bai stood by him in some of his darkest moments, praying with him and for him\. Once, after he had been rejected yet again, he broke down before them\. It was not the first time he had sobbed his pain\. He wept before God and directed his questions to Him\. “How long, O God, must I bear these frustrations and the embarrassment of being repeatedly rejected? Why must I suffer for charges and accusations that are absolutely baseless? I have repented for my sins and reformed my life; why do You still allow me to be chastised?” 

Yet, even as he felt defeated, Art held on to God, keeping faith in the Almighty’s wisdom and providence\. He took comfort in the Scripture verse from the book of Romans, Chapter 8: “God makes all things work together for the good of those who love Him and are called according to His purpose” \(v\.8\)\. 

After three years of screening and being bypassed seven times, he concluded that becoming a member of the Supreme Court was not part 

9

of God’s plan for his life\. It was a crushing conclusion to what had begun

as an exhilarating possibility\. He sought to come to terms with the seeming end of what could have been the apex of his professional career\. 

Bowed and broken in spirit, he came before God in January 1995\. “My heart was bleeding but I lifted up my ambition to the Lord with a solemn prayer: 

Take, oh Lord, all that I am, all that I will ever be\. 

Take my personal ambition, my liberty, my career\. 

Do what you will with them and I will follow 

Whatever You want me to do\.” 

__God Commands __

Six months later, another Supreme Court Justice Camilo Quiason reached retirement age and a vacancy was created\. A close friend of Art, Justice Jose A\. R\. Melo, now deceased, prodded him to try again\. “Don’t pass up the opportunity because the next vacancy will be in late 1997 already,” he was told\. Art’s taciturn response reflected the turn of his heart\. He had already laid his ambition at God’s feet and merely nodded at the suggestion\. The absence of peace, however, deprived him of sleep\. He woke up around 1:00 AM of Saturday, the 29th of June, 1995 with a gnawing question\. Why did his friend’s nudge towards another pitch for the highest tribunal keep reverberating within? He got 

10

up and prayed to God for over two hours\. “I told the Lord that I had already surrendered my quest to Him, that I had already given it up\. Why was I still bothered?” He waited for a sign from the Lord till sleep overtook him around 4:00 AM\. While no answer was forthcoming in the night, the light of day brought an intimation of what he was to do\. 

Art served as an Extraordinary Minister of Holy Communion in their parish\. The following day was a Sunday and he went to church to serve at the Eucharistic celebration\. As was his practice, he closed his eyes imagining the ascending Jesus as the parish priest read the day’s gospel\. The verses he heard made his heart leap: 

“Ask and you shall receive, seek and you shall find,

Knock and the door shall be opened to you\.

For whoever asks, receives; whoever seeks, finds;

whoever knocks is admitted\.” \(Luke 112:1\-11\)

Art believed God was speaking to him\. He held that God spoke through the Bible\. “Though written some two thousand years ago, the Scriptures are still sources of God’s personal messages, “ he said\. “For Jesus Christ is the same today, yesterday and forever\. I believe that if we read or listen intently and prayerfully, in full faith and confidence, 

11

God will speak to us personally as we are struck by a verse or a chapter in the Good Book that comes alive and answers our questions\.” 

So it was that when he heard the Gospel of St\. Luke, Art broke into a cold sweat and had goosebumps\. He gasped mentally, “My Lord, are you asking me to persist in my quest?” Uncertainty hovered in his mind\. Could he just be reading into the holy words, hearing only what he wanted to hear? He needed assurance that it was not a delusion that God was telling him to go after the vacated seat\.  Art sought another sign and a week later, inside the same church, he received what he claimed was God’s confirmation\. 

The gospel reading for that Sunday, the sixth of August was the transfiguration of the Lord Jesus on Mount Tabor\. 

“A cloud came and overshadowed them and the

Disciples grew fearful…\. Then from the clouds came a voice that said, ‘This is my Son, my Chosen One\. Listen to Him’” 

\(Luke 9:28\-36\)\.

The words “Listen to Him” was a resounding affirmation\. The week before, he felt Christ had told him, “Ask and you shall receive; seek and you shall find; knock and the door shall be opened to you\. Now he was 

12

hearing the heavenly Father’s command to “listen to Him\.” A growing conviction that he had been issued a divine directive was displacing his doubts\. He received an instruction for him to submit his name once again to the JBC for screening\. He followed\. 

Such was his confidence that God had spoken to him through the two messages that he disclosed the experience to family and close friends\.  He even boldly shared it during the BLD prayer meeting of August 9, 1995, disregarding the risk of ridicule should his discernment turn out false\. He was determined to pursue God’s will, keeping in mind his favorite Jesuit saying: 

“I will work as if everything depended on me

And I will pray as if everything depended on God\.”

__Promises Kept __

What he had offered to God, the Lord was now returning to him\. Rather than reaping a position gained from his own merits and connections, he would be attributing a beneficence from God\. He could now peacefully navigate the challenging process that would follow his submission to the screening process for the Supreme Court position\. Art had been through the interviews before\.  But whereas previously he had relied on his credentials and network, now he was responding to 

13

God in faith and holding on to the words he believed God had spoken 

to him in Scripture\. 

The scrutiny procedure was just as formidable as it had been, with a serious attempt to vote him out of the list during the JBC meeting on September 13, 1995\. It was the intervention of one member who had pleaded to keep his name on the list that forestalled his elimination\. It was argued, “We are meeting next week and if you insist on deleting it, so be it\.” It was a crucial save, although two days later the same council member intimated to Art that unless a miracle happened, he would be removed from the list\. 

Art turned to God, whose direction he earnestly believed he was following\. He entrusted his situation to Him and prayed that human forces, however exalted and powerful, would not prevail against the will of the Lord\. A week later, no one objected to the inclusion of his name in the list of recommendees submitted to the president\. It was the first time in three years that he got through the screening\. Art considered it nothing short of a miracle\. 

Two weeks later, President Ramos was informed that Art had made it to the JBC list\. The chief executive called and asked him pointblank, “How did you do it?” Art was momentarily caught off guard\. “Do what, sir?” he asked\. The president chewed on the tobacco 

14

he never lit and asked again, “How did you get the JBC to nominate you? How did you do it?” Art was exultant and gave the only answer he knew to be true\. “Well, sir, I didn’t do it\. The Lord did\. Hallelujah\!” 

While it took another two weeks to make his appointment known, President Ramos made good on his promise\. During that period before the news broke out, Art requested the BLD community to pray with him\. “During all our prayer meetings and during all the intercessory sessions, I asked them to storm the heavens with supplications and sacrifices,” he said\. Leni also went to the Carmelites and the Pink Sisters, appealing to them to include him in their prayers\. “It was not just I but also Leni and my whole family who clung to God’s discerned word\. We never doubted that He would be faithful and would make His will prevail\.” 

When the JBC submitted their list of nominees to the Supreme Court, the president called Art\. “I am the Lord’s instrument,” he said\. “God has chosen you and I therefore appoint you justice of the Supreme Court\.” Art’s immediate response was to praise God\. The appointment was the beginning of his career in the highest court of the land\. It happened after he had given up on the possibility of ever becoming a Supreme Court justice\. He could only credit God for making it possible\. 

When Art stood before the BLD on October 18, 1995, he declared, 

15

“Our God took His time because he wanted me to learn some lessons in humility and purification, to acknowledge my powerlessness\. He wanted me to recognize His power over all things, to cling to Him, to have steadfast faith in Him\. He wanted me to know that on my own I could do nothing; but with Him, I could do anything\. 

“Because it is my faith that God made this appointment possible, I hereby declare that all my actions and all my decisions shall only be in accordance with His commandments and His Gospel\. I hold office by God’s grace and I pledge to serve Him and our people with fortitude, integrity, competence and prudence\.”

## Summary

Chapter 10 chronicles the arduous and ultimately providential road to Art's appointment as Justice of the Supreme Court of the Philippines. After the 1986 transition, Senator Jovito R. Salonga -- Art's law-school dean turned three-time poll-topping Senate President -- asked his protégé to take over as personal counsel of the Upper House upon Justice Abraham Sarmiento's elevation to the Supreme Court. Concurrently counsel of the Liberal Party, Art served Salonga from 1987 to 1991 and absorbed the motto that would become his own: things money cannot buy -- honor, dignity, ethics, character and excellence. When Salonga lost the 1992 presidential race to General Fidel V. Ramos, the new chief executive -- Art's Rotary Club of Manila co-member -- invited him to dinner at Ambassador Henrietta de Villa's home and at a subsequent one-hour private meeting in Makati offered him Secretary of Justice. Art declined on three grounds: he had voted for Salonga, the PPCRV's political neutrality could be damaged, and Leni had made political office a marriage-contracted veto. He consulted the CBCP, where Archbishop Legazpi feared reputational damage and Bishop Bacani saw strategic value, before Leni's clinching line -- 'we already agreed when we got married many years ago that you could become anything, anything at all, except a politician' -- decided the matter. Ramos, amused ('Ander de saya ka pala...dalawa na tayo'), pivoted to offer the non-political Supreme Court seat instead. But the road to the bench was not direct. Article VIII Section 8 of the 1987 Constitution had created the Judicial and Bar Council precisely to prevent arbitrary presidential picks, and Chief Justice Andres R. Narvasa as JBC chair adamantly opposed Art's nomination -- defending the council's independence even while acknowledging Art's qualification. Across seven Supreme Court vacancies from 1992 to 1994, Art's name was submitted to the JBC and seven times he was rejected: appellate justices were given priority, a former client surfaced with a 25-year-old complaint, and the 'others more qualified' rationale was repeatedly cited. Art wept before God, asking 'How long, O God, must I bear these frustrations and the embarrassment of being repeatedly rejected?' but clung to Romans 8:28. In January 1995, broken in spirit, he surrendered: 'Take, oh Lord, all that I am, all that I will ever be. Take my personal ambition, my liberty, my career. Do what you will with them and I will follow whatever You want me to do.' Six months later, after Justice Camilo Quiason's retirement opened the eighth vacancy, Justice Jose A.R. Melo prodded Art to try once more. At 1:00 AM on Saturday July 29, 1995, Art woke and prayed two hours, falling asleep at 4:00 AM without a sign. The next morning at parish Mass, with eyes closed imagining the ascending Jesus, he heard Luke 11:1-11 read -- 'Ask and you shall receive, seek and you shall find, knock and the door shall be opened to you' -- and broke into cold sweat, gasping internally, 'My Lord, are you asking me to persist in my quest?' A week later on August 6, the transfiguration Gospel (Luke 9:28-36) gave the confirmation he had asked for: 'This is my Son, my Chosen One. Listen to Him.' He resubmitted his name. On Wednesday August 9, leading the BLD South Sector prayer meeting at St. James School Sucat with Leni, he publicly declared his discernment. On September 13 the JBC nearly excluded him; one unnamed member's plea -- 'let Panganiban's name stay in the list in the meantime' -- kept him in. On September 20, 'by some mysterious reason, no one objected.' For the first time in three years he passed. Ramos called: 'How did you do it?' -- 'Well, sir, I didn't do it. The Lord did. Hallelujah!' Two weeks of intercession by BLD, the Carmelites and Pink Sisters followed before the presidential appointment call: 'I am the Lord's instrument. He has chosen you, and I therefore appoint you justice of the Supreme Court.' On October 18, 1995, before the BLD assembly, Art declared that 'all my actions and all my decisions will only be in accordance with His commandments and His gospel,' pledging to serve 'with fortitude, integrity, competence and prudence.'
