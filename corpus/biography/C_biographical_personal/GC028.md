# Chapter 8: Turning Point

Chapter 8 is the central conversion narrative of the biography. It opens with the macro turning point: February 1986, EDSA People Power Revolution ending the twenty-year Marcos dictatorship, Cory Aquino taking the presidency, Juan Ponce Enrile and Art's co-Rotarian General Fidel V. Ramos defecting from the regime, and EDSA -- the 'Highway of the Saints' named for Epifanio de los Santos -- becoming the stretch where unarmed citizens faced armed soldiers in tanks for four days with rosaries and prayer as their only arsenal. The biography reads the bloodless revolution as 'an intervention of a power greater than human effort,' and argues that the same God acting on a nation's collective life was acting on Art's individual life in parallel: 'It would also be forever etched in Art's memory as the year of his own liberation from darkness to light.' The micro pivot opens in May, when Raul and Aurora Paredes invite Art and Leni -- whose marriage had been strained by Art's late-1970s practice decline, the Vicente Tan-Ver closures, and the years of HK/US/Europe extended absences -- to a 'weekend of rest and relaxation' at Mirador Villa in Baguio. The drive up brings two flat tires (one within an hour of QC, another in Tarlac), which the BLD hosts greet with congratulations on overcoming 'problems devised by the evil one.' Mirador, a Jesuit meteorological observatory on the second-highest peak that had evolved into a retreat venue, hosts Marriage Encounter Class 26 -- 16 couples in common quarters, a little chapel with the Blessed Sacrament exposed, the BLD Intercessory Ministry rotating 24-hour prayer, the Praise Ministry singing arrivals in, alumni couples serving as 'auxiliaries' to honored guests. Fr. Pascual Adorable SJ ('Fr. Ado,' the 'lightning rod of God') delivers homilies that 'penetrate deep into our body, soul and spirit'; presenting couples share severe-and-reconciled marriages, one of years-long separation healed when both came to Christ. By Sunday noon Art and Leni are 'miraculously transformed from a divided couple to a united whole, completely surrendering all cares and pains to our Lord.' Antonio 'Sonny' and Barbara 'Bai' de los Reyes, the presenting couple, noticing Art's receptivity, invite the Panganibans to the Life in the Spirit Seminar -- the BLD's charismatic-renewal introductory course. There, in the darkened LSS hall as the choir sings 'Here I am Lord,' Art with eyes closed sees 'a clear vision of our risen Christ' calling him; he feels 'spiritual tongues of fire burning my sins and transforming me into a new creation,' answers 'Yes, Lord, here I am. I will do whatever pleases you,' and identifies the moment as his Personal Pentecost. Three weeks later Art and Leni return to Mirador as auxiliaries for the next ME class. Sonny and Bai's weekend shepherding dinners at the Panganiban residence run past midnight with questions about feelings and differences and shared scripture readings. Bai prescient warns Art that 'given time, [you] could go back to your old life, especially your love for socializing and partying'; Art smiles and stays the course. Fr. Gerry Santos, a newly-ordained priest who would later become monsignor, catechizes the un-formed convert on basic doctrine, biblical stories and papal encyclicals; Art reads over 100 books on the Christian religion, mostly Catholic and some Protestant, including all 16 volumes of William Barclay's *Daily Study Bible*. BLD service appointments follow -- council of servant-leaders, prayer-leadership roles, joint headship of BLD Foundation and Treasury Ministry with Leni, fundraising for the Christ the Redeemer bronze statue at Mirador Hill and for the Covenant House multi-purpose retreat/conference facility in Dasmariñas, Cavite (both later subjects of a BLD coffee-table book). Art's new pattern of sharing testimony -- 'Once we are converted we begin to glow ... unconsciously we become witnesses of Christ. We become evangelizers ... We cannot go to heaven alone' -- closes the chapter at the threshold of the convergence of his civic, professional, and religious lives. From this point forward the biography will read every subsequent chapter through the post-Personal-Pentecost frame: 'Since my baptism in the Spirit in 1986, I surrendered to the Lord all my ambitions and aspirations.'
