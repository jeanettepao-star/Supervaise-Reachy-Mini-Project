# Chapter 5: Keeper of Dreams

Chapter 5 is the shortest in the biography and pivots on a single meeting. On the 1958 NUS train from Tutuban Station to a 10-hour ride to Naga City -- the same trip on which Art's appendix would burst on Mt. Mayon -- Art surveyed the compartment, espied an empty seat beside a petite *colegiala* by the window, and 'instinctively decided it was the best place for the half-day journey.' The young woman was Elenita 'Leni' Carpio, editor of the St. Scholastica's College paper and the German Benedictines' anticipated novice, sent by the nuns to represent SSC at the seminar -- a decision the chapter notes they would come to regret as the friendship with the NUS president 'derailed her vocation.' Frankie, Art's cousin and confidant on the trip, watched Art pay 'particular attention to her during the conference party,' and heard the verdict: 'she was conservative, a nice person, and he would like to see more of her.' On the day of the organized tour of Mt. Mayon, Cagsawa Ruins and Naga City, Art's appendix ruptured and the chance for leisure time with her was foreclosed by emergency hospitalization. The courtship that followed had Art commuting with Frankie to her residence by jeepney to the nearest stop and then by taxi to her house, to 'camouflage' his economic standing -- unnecessary, the chapter notes, since the Carpios held no social-status prejudices and received him cordially. The stricter requirements came from Leni herself: shared faith life as the ground floor, weekly Mass with her family, monthly Communion, monthly Confession. Art complied dutifully -- initially as 'mere compliance to push his suit' but increasingly with utterances of the heart that the biography reads as real prayer ('the prayers of the future Mrs. Artemio Panganiban were not going unheard'). The marriage proposal succeeded against institutional opposition: the Benedictines wanted Leni in the novitiate, and the Cubao parish priest Fr. Anselmo Bustos refused to perform the rite -- the wedding was delegated to his assistant on the morning of April 8, 1961. The Cubao parish church would later be promoted to the Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception; on their diamond anniversary decades and five children later, Art would read the unintended Marian dedication as 'another indication of the Blessed Mother's presence in his life,' joining the FEU chapel in his accumulating Marian thread.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

The Naga-train aisle introduction: Art surveying the compartment, espying the vacant seat beside the petite Leni, his voice 'louder and friendlier than he had intended' -- and her nodding and moving closer 'to make room or to keep a distance, it wasn't clear to him'

### Anecdote 2

Frankie's role: the cousin/uncle/best friend traveling on the trip noticed Art 'paid particular attention to her during the conference party,' then heard the confidence that 'she was conservative, a nice person, and that he would like to see more of her'

### Anecdote 3

The Mt. Mayon appendix rupture day -- the very day of the organized tour with Cagsawa Ruins and Naga City sights, the chance to spend more leisure time with Leni was foreclosed by emergency hospitalization

### Anecdote 4

The respectable-taxi-arrival ruse: commuting with Frankie by jeepney to the nearest stop near her residence, then taking a cab to her house to camouflage his economic standing -- 'unnecessary,' the chapter notes, 'The Carpios were decent people who held no social-status prejudices'

### Anecdote 5

The Sunday-Mass-with-Carpio-family routine: Art dutifully attending, receiving Communion, kneeling and crossing himself, going to monthly Confession -- 'mere compliance to push his suit' that nonetheless became real prayer

### Anecdote 6

Fr. Anselmo Bustos's refusal to officiate at the wedding -- the parish priest declining the matrimonial rite, delegating it to his assistant, the Benedictines wanting Leni in the novitiate

### Anecdote 7

The diamond-anniversary retrospective -- Art on the 60th anniversary noting that the Cubao parish where they were married had since become the Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception, an unintended Marian dedication
