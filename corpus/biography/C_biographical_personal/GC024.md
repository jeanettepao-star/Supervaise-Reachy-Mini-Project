# Chapter 4: The Law Student

Chapter 4 covers Art's four-year law course at FEU (1956-1960), framed by Psalm 138:7's 'Though I walk in the midst of trouble, you preserve my life.' The chapter opens with the physical setting: Felipe Mendoza's 1957 Science Building (later UNESCO-recognized in 2005), Pablo Antonio's Arellano Hall housing the Institute of Law (commissioned by Dr. Nicanor Reyes, the FEU founder), and the Our Lady of Fatima Chapel decorated by four National Artists -- Botong Francisco's Stations of the Cross and Crucified Christ, Maximo Vicente's life-sized statues, Napoleon Abueva's fiber-glass *La Pieta* inspired by Michelangelo, and Vicente Manansala's unique blue-mosaic Our Lady of Fatima on the facade. The SCA office sat below the chapel, and Art felt drawn to the chapel even before he had 'gotten his spiritual moorings.' The teachers form a register of named formation: Dr. Alejandro R. Roces (Dean of Arts and Sciences) on the truly educated person; Dr. Teodoro Evangelista (FEU president) on education for the life of the mind; Dean Francisco M. Africa on argumentation and debate; Dr. Benito F. Reyes on philosophy and 'theosophy' (whose preacher-philosopher path Art declined out of filial loyalty to Temi's law wish); Prof. Angelica P. Mendoza on botany; Prof. Rosario P. Melchor on English grammar; Prof. Primo Tongco trying to convert the poor student to Marxism ('communism was Christianity,' 'to each according to his need, from each according to his ability' -- 'sounded true' to Art); and Fr. James Sheehy SSC continuing the Columban influence after Fr. Nolan ('a good Catholic must not only be personally pure but must also lead others to purity'). At the Institute of Law itself, Art's faculty was a roll-call of mid-century legal power: Dean Jovito R. Salonga (the role model -- Harvard MA + Yale JSD, a combination the rival ivies normally didn't permit, later Senate President); Neptali A. Gonzales (also future Senate President); Edgardo L. Paras and Salvador Esguerra (future SC justices); Arsenio Solidum, Antonio Lucero, Luis B. Reyes, Angel Mojica, Jose Leuterio (future Court of Appeals justices); and the young Ivy League returnees Juan Ponce Enrile, Manuel Montecillo, Reynaldo Alejandro, Augusto Caesar Espiritu. Salonga monitored Art's grades 'like a father' and admonished, 'I don't care if you are the president of the Central Student Council of FEU and head of the National Union of Students of the Philippines. Your priority as a law student should be your academics.' Two near-death medical episodes punctuate the law years: in 1958, leading the NUS Naga seminar against communism, Art climbed Mt. Mayon and his appendix burst -- misdiagnosed at St. Mary's Maternity Clinic in Tabaco, Albay (the only available clinic, gendered or not) as indigestion, treated with castor oil and then morphine, he slept 24 hours and was dancing that evening; two weeks later, surgery at FEU Hospital under Dr. Ricardo Alfonso, who told him 'Your appendix ruptured two weeks ago. The Lord must love you,' the rupture's atypical posterior-left location and the surrounding fat tissue having contained the toxins long enough for surgical intervention. He paid only the six-peso student fee he had previously opposed as student leader ('You win, Doctor'). A sophomore-year scholarship loss due to over-extended student-leader time was rescued by Uncle Ben's 150 pesos with strict admonition; the scholarship was recovered the next semester. The Marulas, Polo, Bulacan house Art, his brother Nardo and uncle Frankie built with their bare hands became Agang's last home; she succumbed to tuberculosis during Art's fourth year, Nardo administering streptomycin shots when funds allowed. The chapter culminates in the 1960 bar examinations: hospitalized at FEU Hospital on the first Sunday with severe gastroenteritis and flu, discouraged and ready to quit, Art was reached by Salonga whose hand-shake-longer-than-usual was accompanied by 'Art, take the exam; you will get well.' He walked over flooded R. Papa Street to the University of the East and finished #6 -- Bachelor of Laws cum laude -- with Salonga later writing in the 2007 Foreword to *A Test of Courage* that 'Art might have placed first or second if he had not been sick.' Art dropped the 'Jr.' from his name upon admission to the Bar 'to honor him and his last wish ... so that he would truly live in me.'

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

The Mt. Mayon appendix burst (1958): Art leading the NUS Naga seminar against communism, taking a tour break to climb Mayon, severe abdominal pain, friends carrying him down to Tabaco, the only clinic being St. Mary's Maternity (for women), the doctor's indigestion diagnosis and castor oil prescription that came up immediately, finally morphine, 24-hour sleep, then dancing that evening as if nothing had happened

### Anecdote 2

Two-weeks-later FEU Hospital surgery: Dr. Alfonso (surgeon-director) asking 'You want to pay the full hospitalization or just the six-peso student fee?' -- Art conceding 'You win, Doctor' and withdrawing his student-leader opposition to the mandatory fee

### Anecdote 3

Dr. Alfonso's verdict: ruptured appendix in atypical posterior-left location, fat tissue containing the toxins, 'Your appendix ruptured two weeks ago. The Lord must love you'

### Anecdote 4

Sophomore scholarship loss: too much student-leader time, grades fell, lost the scholarship -- Uncle Ben gave 150 pesos to cover tuition (135 pesos), books (10-20 pesos) and allowance, with the strict warning 'If you don't get good grades, I won't support you for the second semester' -- scholarship recovered

### Anecdote 5

Building the Marulas, Polo, Bulacan house with brother Nardo and uncle Frankie -- sleeping on bare ground at the construction site to guard the materials

### Anecdote 6

Agang's tuberculosis decline: streptomycin shots when funds allowed, administered by Nardo; succumbed during Art's fourth year

### Anecdote 7

1960 bar exams: hospitalized at FEU Hospital with severe gastroenteritis and flu on the first Sunday; Dean Salonga's hands-shaking-longer-than-usual encouragement ('Art, take the exam; you will get well'); walking over flooded R. Papa Street to the University of the East venue; #6 finish

### Anecdote 8

Dean Salonga's 2007 Foreword to Art's *A Test of Courage*: recounting the bar-exam ordeal, saying Art 'might have placed first or second if he had not been sick'

### Anecdote 9

Prof. Primo Tongco's Marxist seduction attempts: 'You are bright. You do your best when you are a professional, but you should not get paid according to your ability...' -- 'That was very attractive to me. I was very poor and what he said appealed to me and sounded true.'

### Anecdote 10

Dr. Benito F. Reyes wanting Art to follow him into 'theosophy' (combining religion and philosophy, finding God through reason not faith) -- Art deciding against because of Temi's law-career wish
