# Chapter 13: 21st Chief Justice of the Philippines

Chapter 13 narrates Art Panganiban's elevation as the 21st Chief Justice of the Philippines, succeeding Hilario G. Davide Jr. The chapter opens with Art alone in his chamber after eight in the evening, reviewing decisions of lower tribunals, and uses that quiet image to introduce the costs of the office: full divestment from his law firm, sale of all his shares, resignation from fifteen professional and civic associations (he retains only the Pontifical Council for the Laity, the Rotary Club of Manila, and the Bantayog ng mga Bayani Foundation, all canonically permitted for incumbent justices), severely restricted social life, and a strict no-contact regime -- no visitors or calls into his chambers, no exceptions even for letters marked 'personal,' and public requests that audiences not lobby him about pending cases. The central narrative moment is the December 7, 2005 announcement on Art's sixty-ninth birthday: addressing Supreme Court employees at the Pan Pacific Hotel, he is handed a scribbled note reporting that DZBB has aired President Gloria Macapagal Arroyo's appointment of him as Chief Justice. Thunderstruck, he apologizes that he cannot finish his remarks, finds Leni in the audience, and they slip past waiting reporters to drive to the prayer room at Santuario de San Antonio, where he kneels with her beside him and offers himself anew to God. The chapter sets out Art's understanding of the role -- citing Anthony Lewis's Gideon's Trumpet on what a justice brings to the task; insisting that magistrates 'live in a world of reason where logic, evidence and conscience are their only yardsticks'; describing how peer testimonials (Justice Antonio T. Carpio: 'undoubtedly the most prolific writer in the Court, bar none'; Justice Angelina Sandoval-Gutierrez on his 'preeminent judicial craftsmanship'; CJ Davide on his 'mental dexterity' across non-legal subjects) document 1,200 full-length decisions over a decade and ten published books since 1995. A pro-poor stance, rooted in Magsaysay's 'those who have less in life should have more in law,' shapes the substantive direction of his ponencias. A vignette of an elderly widow lobbying him at communion, and his gentle but firm redirection -- pray to the Lord, not to him -- captures the cost and the integrity of his pastoral-and-judicial dual role at the apex of an institution he leads as primus inter pares over an estimated 2,000 justices and judges and 26,000 court personnel nationwide.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

December 7, 2005, Pan Pacific Hotel: addressing Supreme Court employees on his 69th birthday, Art is handed a slip of paper mid-speech -- 'I heard on DZBB that the president had appointed you as chief justice.' He stares at the audience, apologizes that he cannot finish, and leaves the stage.

### Anecdote 2

After the announcement, Art finds Leni in the audience and they slip out past waiting media reporters to their parked car, instructing the driver to take them to Santuario de San Antonio's prayer room -- where Art kneels with Leni beside him and offers himself once more to God.

### Anecdote 3

On taking office, Art divests entirely from his law firm, sells all shares, and resigns from 15 nonjudicial organizations -- keeping only the Pontifical Council for the Laity, Rotary Club of Manila, and Bantayog ng mga Bayani Foundation, all allowed for incumbent justices.

### Anecdote 4

Written instructions to the Supreme Court security force forbid anyone from entering his chamber without express permission; mail marked 'personal' is treated no differently from official mail because 'there was nothing personal about matters pending before the Court.'

### Anecdote 5

An elderly widow at Santuario de San Antonio reproaches Art at communion: 'Chief Justice, I take communion from you every Sunday, but you never seem to remember my petition...' Unfazed, he tells her not to lobby him at church but to pray to the Lord, since he too prays before deciding.

### Anecdote 6

Quoting Anthony Lewis's Gideon's Trumpet at Justice Antonio Martinez's retirement: '[A] justice brings to the task only his character, his intellect, his education, his experience, his human understanding, his imagination.'

### Anecdote 7

At a 2005 Chile international forum, Art gives five lectures on bioscience -- moving CJ Davide to describe his 'mental dexterity' as the ability to 'extricate the possible from the hypothetical, the emerging from the established, the literature in science and the law in art.'

### Anecdote 8

Justice Antonio T. Carpio, his colleague on the Court, calls him 'undoubtedly the most prolific writer in the Court, bar none' -- a verdict he wears without ostentation.
