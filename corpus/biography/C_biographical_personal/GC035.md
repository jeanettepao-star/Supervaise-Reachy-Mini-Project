# Chapter 15: Papal Award

Chapter 15	

					       __Papal Award				               __

	*“*__*You make known to me the path of life; you fill me with joy in your presence, with eternal pleasures at your right hand\.” *__

__						Psalm 16:11__

	The retired Chief Justice of the Philippines arrived at the Manila Cathedral early\. It was a momentous day\. He was going to be conferred the papal award Pro Ecclesia et Pontifice in recognition of his contribution as president of the Manila Cathedral Basilica Foundation in funding, retrofitting, restrengthening and updating with new technologies the “Mother Church of the Philippines\.” It was no small matter to receive the highest possible honor bestowed on laypersons by the Pope\. As much as the Chief Justice was elated, a part of him was quiet and reflective\. He had come to a point in his life where accolades were viewed from the perspective of eternity\. He counted his blessings daily, taking into account what had been and the unlikelihood of reaching his present station\. 

	He made his way inside the still empty Church, save for a few of the people preparing for the ceremony\. Kneeling before the altar, the man prayed\. His life story was a remarkable ascent from poverty to the comfortable life he was able to provide for his family\. He counted his blessings and remembered with gratitude the ironic turn of events throughout the years\. While he himself had been unable to enter the university of his first choice, as his family did not have the means to provide for the daily commute, he was able to send all his five children to pedigreed US institutions\. He, who had missed the opportunity to study in Yale on a full scholarship with board and lodging because he couldn’t raise the funds for an airplane ticket– and was later refused a visa by the US Embassy due to his student activism–now lived with the comforts of life that money could buy\.  

	The boy who peddled newspapers early in the morning had become president of one the largest circulating broadsheets, a part\-time position he held for two years before he joined the Supreme Court\. Eventually, he returned as a columnist who held a soft spot for the newsboys to whom he donated his initial journalist fees when he joined the *Philippine Daily Inquirer\.* The ironies of his life were not lost to him and he could only acknowledge the God he had barely known as a boy\. 

 

__A Presence Missed__

	He sat on the pew, a sense of poignancy overcoming him\. Today was a highlight in his spiritual life but his Leni was no longer with him\. She had passed away almost a year before, a sudden departure on Easter Sunday that left him devastated\. She had been teaching graduate students for 39 years at AIM, patiently stroking traditional Chinese pastels on delicate rice paper and travelling occasionally when tragedy struck\. The night before her passing, Leni and the Chief were online with their children and grandchildren abroad\. They had a happy exchange and there was no indication of what was to happen within hours\. She had no heart condition, and her blood pressure was stable, so the heart attack that triggered her demise baffled the Chief Justice\. 

	The memory of the ambulance ride played in his mind\. She had suddenly weakened in the early morning and broken into a cold sweat\. He held his wife’s hand as he held on to God\. He kept holding on to the Father as she slipped quietly away in the emergency room of the Makati Medical Center\. The finality brought an ache that remained and was deeply felt especially on occasions he so wanted to share with her such as that day of his awarding\. Faith that she was with the Lord tempered the sadness, but the grief was there nonetheless\. 

On the ninth day of his wife’s demise, he delivered a eulogy that he published in his weekly column in the *Philippine Daily Inquirer\.* It registered as the most read column of the national broadsheet\. There he shared his reflections and recollected a memorable event in their life together\.__	__

A year before Leni’s death, they celebrated their diamond wedding anniversary in a quiet chapel tucked in the Tagaytay countryside\. The milestone occasion was an intimate affair with their immediate family, a handful of select friends, and 86 incumbent archbishops and bishops of the Philippines\. He remembered there was a gentle rain on that afternoon as they entered the private Carmelite Missionaries Center of Spirituality lit up by a thousand flickering lights\. The Manila Cathedral Choir sang the entrance march as they walked down the aisle, Leni resting her arm on her husband of sixty\-one years\.

	“We had turned our weaknesses into strengths and our differences into opportunities to grow in the Lord and to be happy by faithfully obeying Him who is much greater than us,” he wrote in his April 17, 2023 column in the *Philippine Daily Inquirer\.*  “My Leni and I have found true love, contentment and happiness in offering all that we were and all that we had to Him who created us in His image as temporary visitors on earth and as permanent citizens of heaven\.”

	A sigh escaped from within\. It was a happy time when they renewed their vows before His Eminence Jose F\. Cardinal Advincula Jr\., who officiated the Eucharistic celebration\. An inspired homily of another good friend of his, Most Reverend Soc Villegas, Archbishop of Lingayen\-Dagupan, carried the tone of a personal conversation with the couple\. He enjoined them to continue passing on wisdom gained from their experience and making Christ known through their lives\.

	The recollections remained fresh in his mind\. These were like prayers being offered from his heart–gratitude that they had made it to 61 years by the grace of God\.  As much as he missed Leni, he was consoled that she was in a happy place, resting in the bosom of the Father\.    

__Applause for All__

	Guests were beginning to arrive and he got up to acknowledge the greetings and congratulatory well wishes\. Being among people uplifted him and he was genuinely glad for the presence of the many who filled the Cathedral\.  The pews were occupied by those who had been part of the project to retrofit, restrengthen, restore and renovate the Basilica founded 446 years ago\. Besides the donors, contractors, owners’ representatives, planning managers and engineers, there were the carpenters, masons, electricians and other workers in attendance\. He had specifically requested that those who had physically toiled in fortifying the Cathedral be included in the guest list\.  

	The simple, solemn investiture took place during the Eucharistic celebration presided by Cardinal Advincula\.  CJ Art, dressed in an all\-white ensemble, was visibly moved\. It was an honor beyond his imaginings\. He was at once overwhelmed and peaceful as the decoration was conferred on him\. When he took to the rostrum to respond, the new papal awardee faced a church full to capacity\. Addressing the congregation, he said, “The award belongs to all of you, not just to unworthy me\.” 

 It was a time of gratitude and he recalled the many to whom he felt indebted for nurturing his faith and providing inspiration in his spiritual sojourn\. He reached into his memories dating as far back as the 1950s when Fr\. Michael Nolan began educating him on basic prayers and steering him from communist ideologies to the catechism of the Catholic Church\. He gave cognizance to Ambassador Tita de Villa, who connected him to the church hierarchy and enlisted him to serve in major Catholic lay organizations\. 

He paused momentarily when he mentioned his wife’s name, a quiver in his voice as he hailed Leni’s memory\. His audience held a respectful silence as he managed his grief while honoring the woman who had been his quiet but steady companion through shadows and light\. CJ Art breathed deeply, scanning the faces looking at him\. Incumbent Chief Justice Alexander G\. Gesmundo had foregone a judicial conference abroad to attend the ceremony\. His former student, Tessie Sy Coson, now the undisputed leader in banking, finance and entrepreneurship in the country, sat in the front row with his family\. He could see Ramon Ang and his daughter Cecile on the other side of the aisle with Fernando Zobel and his wife Kit\. Members of the Ty family were present together as were the late Bert and wife Sylvia Lina–all generous donors of the Cathedral\. They had made time to be there for him\. Acknowledging their presence, he disclosed that there had been another big donor whose generosity went with a request for anonymity\. Addressing the congregation, he said, “This award belongs to all of you, not just to unworthy me\.”

He wanted to name everyone including the workers but exercised restraint knowing the list was lengthy\. In the end, he chose to acknowledge the one name that really mattered\. “…I reiterate the vow I delivered when I first entered the Supreme Court in 1995 without passing through the judicial hierarchy and without holding any prior public or quasi\-public office…\[I  vow to\] hold office by God’s grace, and I pledge to serve Him and our people…\. I pledge to serve Him and our people with fortitude, integrity, competence and prudence… I dedicate all that I am and all that I will ever be to Him who is the Source of everything…\.”

__A Second Wind at 89__

	Chief Justice Art was in high spirits throughout the evening\. He was much engaged at the reception following the solemnities\. The company and the conversations stimulated him, and he interacted with his guests, enjoying the presence of people who had been part of his journey\. He went home that night, tired but happy\. It had been a full day\. 

	The weeks and months following resumed a routine that kept him busy and active\. He had meetings, received visitors or spent time on the greens for a few rounds of golf\. It was in the latter part of the day that he faced aloneness in the sanctuary of his seven\-bedroom home\. His five children now had their families, and their own houses–three in the USA and two in Metro Manila\. If there were no dinner engagements, he would sit with his thoughts\. He still had many plans and things he wanted to do\. Living fully invigorated him\. He had picked up a second wind\.

	The Chief Justice pondered the events of life from the vantage view of 89 years\. Though he had not been schooled in catechesis, a confluence of events led him to the firm acknowledgement that Divinity charted his course\. He deemed that circumstances and encounters, people he met, opportunities, as well as failures and losses, were alliances of destiny\. These led to a faith life that he valued more than the successes of a career, material gain and the esteem of men\. He viewed all that had been as an orchestration for him to realize a plan greater than he had envisioned for himself\.

	The days and months quickly passed, but there were markers that punctuated his progression to a relationship with God\. Some might have been perceptible, others not as apparent\. Yet when he reflected on his spiritual journey, the Chief Justice believed with certainty that the Christian God he had raged against had been with him from the moment of his conception\. It did not feel so to him then–growing up in humble circumstances, losing his parents early, hawking newspapers to pay for his studies, and sleeping on sidewalks\. Only by understanding with faith did he grasp that everything unique to him led to the ultimate purpose of his life\.

	When he graduated from the Court upon turning 70, he thought his chase for earthly glory was over\. He wanted to dedicate his remaining years in service to God\. He spurned offers to rejoin the government as a senior member of the President’s cabinet, or as head of a large government corporation, or to embark on a new diplomatic career as an ambassador\. But to help him financially contribute to charitable, philanthropic and spiritual causes, he accepted independent directorships and adviserships in conglomerates that enabled him to tap their resources towards worthy causes\.

Dusk was setting in, extending shadows before nightfall\. The man looked up at the sky, quietly watching the streaks of light fade into the darkness\. He sat in a room full of memorabilia, souvenirs from travels abroad, books he had written, a collection of local and international honors, accolades and honorary doctoral degrees reaped throughout a distinguished career\.  There were citations framed on the wall and photographs with men and women of history, leaders and kingmakers who counted him as their equal\. The accomplishments and regard had so mattered to him at the time that he commissioned this private museum to house such glory\. That was many years ago\.

	Without discounting the achievements of an extraordinary life, these no longer held his heart\. Something had happened that caused him to see the value of things in the light of eternity\. He watched the stars begin to flicker in the night sky\. Tomorrow would be another day, but he was content to sit under the celestial sphere bright with a constellation overhead\.

	Still robust, hale and healthy in body and mind at 89 going on 90, he was regularly and amazingly striking golf and tennis balls four times a week\. He walked the jogging oval at the Manila Polo Club twice a week and could see himself easily reaching 100 years and beyond\. His powerful mind and body were still happily functioning\. 

Every night, he prayed that the Lord manifest His will for him\. It was an acknowledgement that he was a temporary resident of earth\. His goal was to someday take his place as a citizen of Heaven and rest in the bosom of the Heavenly Father\. Until then, he would continue to put his legal expertise at the disposal of the Lord\. Law had not been the path of his original intention, but the rise in a profession he had not chosen had the markings of a divinely ordained destiny\. After all the bends and curves, he could not dispute that his life had been guided by the hand of God\.  CJ Art had become a lawyer in the vineyard of the Lord, and it was a calling that he fully embraced with gratitude for the Holy Presence who had always been there through the valleys and peaks of his remarkable life journey\.
