# Chapter 15: Papal Award

Chapter 15, the closing chapter of the biography, opens with Art Panganiban arriving early and alone at the Manila Cathedral on the day he is to be conferred Pro Ecclesia et Pontifice -- the highest honor the Pope bestows on laypersons -- in recognition of his work as president of the Manila Cathedral-Basilica Foundation, which funded and led the engineering retrofit of the 'Mother Church of the Philippines.' Kneeling alone in the still-empty cathedral, he reads his life from the vantage of eighty-nine years and through a frame of eternity: the boy who slept on Sampaloc pavements selling newspapers has become president of the country's largest-circulating broadsheet and, later, its columnist; the lawyer denied a UP slot for a fifteen-centavo fare and Yale for an airplane ticket has sent all five children to top US universities; the one whose US visa was once denied for student activism has stood at the apex of the judiciary. The brightest day, however, carries a quiet ache -- his Leni has died almost a year before, on Easter Sunday, from a sudden heart attack at the Makati Medical Center. Art recalls the night before her passing, the ambulance ride, the eulogy column that became the most-read piece the Philippine Daily Inquirer has carried, and their 61st diamond wedding anniversary the year prior at the Carmelite Missionaries Center in Tagaytay, with Cardinal Advincula officiating, Archbishop Soc Villegas preaching, the Manila Cathedral Choir singing, and eighty-six incumbent archbishops and bishops attending. At Art's specific request, the investiture pews include the carpenters, masons, electricians and other workers who physically retrofitted the basilica, alongside donors, the incumbent Chief Justice Alexander G. Gesmundo, former student Tessie Sy Coson, the Angs, Zobels, Tys, and Linas. Quivering at his own mention of Leni's name, Art breathes deeply and reaffirms verbatim the 1995 vow he took on entering the Supreme Court -- closing a literary inclusio across the entire biography. The chapter ends with a meditation on what the long arc means: the Christian God he had once raged against was with him from his conception; the seeming failures were 'alliances of destiny'; the earthly identity is provisional and the citizen of Heaven is the permanent one. Still robust at eighty-nine going on ninety, playing golf and tennis four times a week and walking the polo club's jogging oval twice a week, Art prays each night that the Lord manifest His will and describes himself, in the last lines, as a lawyer in the vineyard of the Lord.

## Notable Anecdotes

### Anecdote 1

Art arrives early at the Manila Cathedral on the day of the Pro Ecclesia et Pontifice investiture, walks into a still-empty church, kneels before the altar, and prays -- counting blessings and reading the ironies of his life from the long view of eternity.

### Anecdote 2

Leni's last night: she and Art are online with the children and grandchildren abroad with no indication of what is coming; in the early morning she suddenly weakens and breaks into a cold sweat; in the ambulance Art holds her hand and holds onto God; she slips quietly away in the emergency room of the Makati Medical Center.

### Anecdote 3

The ninth-day eulogy column for Leni, published in the Philippine Daily Inquirer, becomes the most-read column the national broadsheet has carried -- capturing the marriage and Leni's quiet steadiness through six decades.

### Anecdote 4

Recalled: their 61st diamond wedding anniversary the year before, at the Carmelite Missionaries Center in the Tagaytay countryside, with Cardinal Jose F. Advincula Jr. officiating, an inspired homily by Archbishop Soc Villegas, the Manila Cathedral Choir, eighty-six incumbent archbishops and bishops, and Leni resting her arm on her husband of sixty-one years as a gentle rain falls outside.

### Anecdote 5

At his specific request, the cathedral pews on the day of his investiture include not only donors, contractors, and engineers, but the carpenters, masons, electricians, and other workers who physically toiled to fortify the basilica.

### Anecdote 6

Among the guests: the incumbent Chief Justice Alexander G. Gesmundo foregoes a judicial conference abroad to attend; Tessie Sy Coson, Art's former student, now an industry leader, sits in the front row with her family; Ramon Ang and his daughter Cecile, the Zobels and Linas and Tys, and one major anonymous donor -- make time to be present.

### Anecdote 7

Quivering at his own mention of Leni's name, Art breathes deeply, scans the faces, and concludes by reaffirming verbatim the vow he took in 1995 entering the Supreme Court: 'I hold office by God's grace, and I pledge to serve Him and our people with fortitude, integrity, competence and prudence... I dedicate all that I am and all that I will ever be to Him who is the Source of everything.'

### Anecdote 8

At eighty-nine going on ninety, Art keeps a routine of golf and tennis four times a week, walks the jogging oval at the Manila Polo Club twice a week, and can imagine reaching a hundred -- describing himself, in the chapter's final lines, as a 'lawyer in the vineyard of the Lord' awaiting his place as a citizen of Heaven.
