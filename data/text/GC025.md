__CHAPTER 5__

*Then the LORD God said, “It is not good for the man to be alone\. I will make a helper who is just right for him*__*\.”    Genesis 2: 18, *__[__*New Living Translation*__](https://biblehub.com/nlt/genesis/2.htm)

__    Keeper of Dreams__

Somewhere in those frenetic years of law school and the pursuit of intellectual and principled ideals, there was a train ride that would take Art on an unplanned route\. The campaign of the National Union of Students to educate student leaders on the abhorrence of communism included a seminar in Naga City\. It was a 10\-hour train ride from the Tutuban Station in Manila to the city in Camarines Sur, a long trip by any standard\. The railway transport was used by all the delegates to the NUS seminar\. Art was traveling with his cousin and best friend Frankie, who was enlisted to help in the preparation and distribution of materials for the conference\.

They arrived at the station together but when the young student leader boarded the compartment, he quickly surveyed the passengers on board and espied a vacancy beside a young *colegiala*\. He instinctively decided it was the best place for the half\-day journey and made his way towards her\. Art stopped when he reached the girl who was seated by the window and asked if the vacant spot was occupied\. He tried to make it appear like a disinterested inquiry, but the sound of his voice came across louder and friendlier than he had intended\. The petite college 

student looked up at the lean man standing in the aisle\. She was slightly built and appeared fragile\. After nodding her head, she moved closer to the side–whether to make room or to keep a distance, it wasn’t clear to him\.

He settled down beside her and cleared his throat\. Politely, he introduced himself and was gratified by a smile\. Elenita Carpio went by the name Leni and was representing St\. Scholastica’s College, the convent school run by German Benedictine nuns\. Founded as a learning institution to provide religious education for the children of Manila, it was primarily a girls’ school, although they accepted male enrollees when the 

nuns opened programs for music, fine art and interior design\.

The Benedictine sisters were particularly fond of Leni\. She had expressed an intention to join their congregation and they were anticipating she would be a good addition to their number\. A fine student, she was also the editor of their school paper and had been designated to represent SSC in the Naga seminar\. Their decision to send her did not factor in the meeting with the NUS president and the ensuing friendship that would derail her vocation\.

Any thought Art might have had of sleeping through the trip was gone\. They fell into hours of conversation and he was taken by her intelligence and gentleness\. By the time they reached their destination, she had made a deep impression on him\. Frankie noticed that while Art circulated among the delegates, he paid particular intention to her during the conference party\. Later, he intimated to his confidant that she was conservative, a nice person, and that he would like to see more of her\. It was unfortunate that on the day of the organized tour of Mayon Volcano, Cagsawa Ruins and Naga City, Art’s appendix ruptured\. He was brought to a hospital for medical treatment and missed the chance to spend more leisure time with her\.

# The Suitor

The episode was a mere hiccup in his intentions to get to know her better\. Together with Frankie, he would commute to her residence, get off at the nearest stop and take a cab to her house\. He wanted to make a good impression on her family and felt the appearance of a respectable arrival in a taxi would help to camouflage an economic standing that was far lower than her family’s comfortable station\. It was unnecessary\. The Carpios were decent people who held no social\-status prejudices\. They received the lean law student with cordiality and eventually accepted him when the relationship with their daughter had been formalized\.

It may have helped that he was a known student leader who excelled in his academics and was setting his sights on a career in a well\-regarded profession\. He was respectful, appropriate and clear about his intentions\. The more stringent requirements before accepting

his suit came from Leni herself\. Having been schooled and mentored by the good sisters in her convent school, she was steeped in the teachings 

and practices of the Catholic church\. Before Art began his pursuit, she could already foresee a life in the nunnery\. Any alternative to that future would necessarily also be grounded in a shared faith life\.

# Winning the Novice

So it was that Art found himself attending mass with her every Sunday\. It was her requirement that they would not only participate in the weekly service, but that he would receive holy communion and go to confession every month\. He dutifully accompanied her when the family went to the Eucharistic celebrations, knelt with them, crossed himself and bowed his head\. The man who grew up in nonsectarian schools furthered his education in the ecclesiastical expressions of Christianity\. He became familiar with church practices and while it was mere compliance to push his suit, there was little doubt that the prayers of the future Mrs\. Artemio Panganiban were not going unheard\.

The suitor himself may have learned to go beyond the formulaic recitations of the “Our Father,” “Hail Mary” and “Glory Be” that Fr\. Nolan had taught him\. He was connecting with the Divine in his desire to have her for his wife\. Utterances of the heart raised to the God at the altar are as well received as spoken words\. That the Lord had seen fit to answer him favorably when she accepted his marriage proposal was no small matter\. He had been up against a congregation that disapproved of the match\. The Benedictines of St\. Scholastica deemed she was best suited to join them than to marry an unchurched and uncatechized man\. 

They wanted her in the novitiate\. Even the parish priest, Fr\. Anselmo Bustos, was on the congregation’s side and refused to perform the rite of marriage\. The responsibility to officiate the matrimonial was delegated to the assistant parish priest\.

They were married in a simple ceremony on the morning of April 8, 1961, in her parish church in Cubao\. The local community church would later become the Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception\. It may not have been of particular significance to him then, but on their diamond anniversary decades and five children later, Art would remark that being married in a church dedicated to Our Lady of the

Immaculate Conception was another indication of the Blessed Mother’s presence in his life\.
