Reflection delivered by retired Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban, Member, Bukas Loob sa Diyos Covenant Community [BLD] Council of Servant Leaders, on October 18, 1995 — a week after being appointed Justice of the Supreme Court — during the BLD Corporate Prayer Meeting, Cuneta Astrodome, Pasay City.

---

The gospel reading tells of ten lepers who asked Jesus for healing. That is the equivalent to asking our Lord for a miracle. As we all know, leprosy is incurable even by modern science. The lepers were not only condemned as contagious outcasts of society. They were also shamed as forsaken sinners — with no hope in this life nor in the next.

This is why the gospel narrative says that the lepers kept their distance as they shouted, Jesus, Master, have pity on us. And Jesus showed His compassion. He healed all of them. But the irony is that only one — a Samaritan, a non-convert — came back to praise and thank our Lord.

Tonight, it is our intention to tell the Lord, that in BLD, we shall all go back to thank and praise the Lord. Tonight, this reflection will contain my own personal sharing on two points: first, the validity and effectiveness of our discernment process in BLD and second, the value of faith in obeying God's will after we discerned it.

You all know that I was recently appointed Justice of the Supreme Court of the Philippines. I took my oath only a week ago. Being elevated to the Supreme Court is the highest aspiration and dream of every lawyer. My serious quest began some three years ago.

From 1992 to 1994, at least seven vacancies in the Supreme Court were created due to the retirement of incumbent members. In each of these vacancies, my name was submitted for screening. But in all seven, I failed the test. Many objections were raised against me. These past three years of screening were one long story of frustrations and defeats. Many times, I cried and would ask the Lord: How long, O Lord must I bear this frustration and embarrassment of being rejected?

In all these instances, I never lost faith in His wisdom and providence. I always took comfort and consolation in Romans 8:28: God makes all things work together for the good of those who love Him and are called according to His purpose.

In January 1995, after seven by-passes, I humbled myself and bowed to what I thought was God's will for me. I uplifted my ambition to God with the prayer: Take, O Lord, all that I am, all that I will ever be. Take my personal ambition, my liberty, my career. Do what You will with them and I will follow whatever it is you want me to do.

In July 1995, a close friend asked me to try again. At about 1:00 a.m. of Saturday, July 29th, I woke up and could not sleep. I prayed for over two hours. Then the following Sunday, I listened intently to the parish priest as he read the gospel from Luke 11: Ask and you shall receive, seek and you shall find, knock and the door shall be opened to you. I perspired coldly and gasped, My Lord, are you asking me to persist in my quest?

The Lord's confirmation came after one week. On Sunday, August 6, 1995, the gospel was on the transfiguration of our Lord Jesus. From the cloud came a voice which said, This is my Son, my Chosen One. Listen to Him. These three words struck me. And then, I was sure what the Lord wanted me to do. So, I obeyed Him. I submitted my name again in the JBC screening.

During the JBC meeting on September 20, no one, by some mysterious reason, objected to my name and for the first time in over three years, I passed the screening. A proclaimed miracle! Thereafter, the list of nominees was sent to the President, who called me by phone saying in effect, I am the Lord's instrument. He has chosen you and I therefore appoint you Justice of the Supreme Court.

Our God took His time because He wanted me to learn some lessons in humility and in purification. He wanted me to acknowledge my powerlessness. He wanted me to recognize His power over all things.

And tonight, like that Samaritan leper who was cleansed and healed by the Lord, I come back to Him to give thanks with a grateful heart, to give praise to our holy God.

Thank You, Lord Jesus Christ!
