ENTERING THE LEGAL PROFESSION

Date:       2007-04-26

Publisher:  cjpanganiban.com

Source:     https://cjpanganiban.com/entering-the-legal-profession/

Address delivered by retired Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban as guest of honor and speaker during the 116th Foundation Day Celebration of the Philippine Bar Association, held at the Hotel Intercontinental on April 26, 2007.

---

I am deeply honored to have been invited as your guest of honor and speaker. Although I have already retired from the judiciary and am now a private citizen, the Philippine Bar Association has deemed me still worthy to address this important milestone: the 116th foundation day celebration of the oldest and largest voluntary aggrupation of lawyers in our country.

Born on April 8, 1891 as the Colegio de Abogados de Filipinas, the PBA has a pedigreed tradition dating back to the gloried days of Apolinario Mabini, Felipe Calderon, Cayetano Arellano and Jose Abad Santos, to name a few of its illustrious heroes and leaders.

THE 2006 BAR EXAM

Yesterday, at the Philippine International Convention Center, 1,893 law graduates who passed the 2006 bar examinations were sworn in as new members of the noblest profession in the world. The Supreme Court ended its summer session in Baguio and the justices motored to Manila to attend the solemn rites.

A graduate of the University of the Cordilleras (Noel Neil Q. Malimba) topped the test. For the nonce, the traditional rivals, Ateneo de Manila (Ricardo M. Pilares Jr.) and the University of the Philippines (Deborah S. Acosta) had to content themselves with a tie for second place. Far Eastern University (Gina Lyn R. Rubio) took the fifth place.

The 2006 bar will be remembered for many things. This batch, numbering 6,187, is the largest ever to take the examination. The year 2006 marks the first time that four non-Metro Manila schools copped four of the top ten places: University of the Cordilleras, University of San Carlos, Romualdez Educational Foundation, and University of Cebu.

THE BAR EXAM ILLUSTRADOS

Many bar illustrados have demonstrated enviable track records in the profession and in the government. Four incumbent SC justices are bar placers: Antonio T. Carpio (6th place, 1975), Adolfo S. Azcuna (4th, 1962), Presbitero J. Velasco Jr. (6th, 1971) and Antonio B. Nachura (7th, 1967).

Of the 21 past Chief Justices, nine were topnotchers, including Pedro L. Yap (1st, 1946), Andres R. Narvasa (2nd, 1951) and Artemio V. Panganiban (6th, 1960).

PERFORMANCE OF THE LAW SCHOOLS

In the exams held from 1946 to 2006, a period of 60 years, the University of the Philippines produced 25 number ones; Ateneo de Manila, 17; San Beda, six; Far Eastern University, four. Based on average passing percentages for the period 1991-2005, Ateneo de Manila topped with 87.57 percent; San Beda, 80.38; UP, 76.4.

REFORMS IN THE BAR TESTS

Over the years, the SC has tried to upgrade the bar examinations. Extensive reforms were approved under Bar Matter 1161 dated June 8, 2004, including: those who have failed five or more times shall be allowed to take only one more exam after finishing a one-year refresher course; candidates who obtain a grade below 50 percent in any subject were disqualified; multiple choice questions have been adopted.

REFORMS IN LEGAL EDUCATION

The Supreme Court did not act on four proposals to improve legal education: the accreditation and supervision of law schools; the inclusion of clinical education in the law curriculum; the imposition of sanctions on sub-standard law schools; and the taking of a national admission test by those entering a law school. These four items were referred to the Legal Education Board. Sadly, although created 14 years ago, the LEB has not been operationalized.

ETHICS AND COMPETENCE

As a final note, more than knowledge of the law and competence in its practice, legal ethics should be emphasized both in basic legal education and in the bar exams. To close this address, I would like to paraphrase an oft-quoted Supreme Court decision: the doors to our legal profession must swing on reluctant hinges. Those who want to pass those doors must make a solemn commitment to adhere to an ethical compass. Quality justice always begins with a high standard of ethics. During my term as Chief Justice, I advocated the revitalization of the legal profession by breeding competent and ethical lawyers. Yes, competent and ethical attorneys, code-named EC, that is the legacy I would like to leave our profession.

Maraming salamat po.
