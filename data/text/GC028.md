1986 was a pivotal time in the Philippines\. Over twenty years of  dictatorship under President Ferdinand E\. Marcos came to a dramatic end with the People Power revolution\. It was that peaceful amassing of the citizenry on the major thoroughfare which precipitated a change of government in the country\. Also known as the EDSA revolution, it determined the change of government from Marcos’ military rule to democracy under President Corazon C\. Aquino, his political challenger in the presidential derby\.

EDSA is the acronym for Epifanio de los Santos, the name of a noted Filipino historian, writer and civil servant\. The highway, connecting several cities in Metropolitan Manila, was named after him\. When Marcos called a snap presidential elections and declared himself the winner amidst reports of widespread cheating and vote\-buying, supporters of Mrs\. Aquino amassed on EDSA in protest and threw their support behind then Defense Secretary Juan Ponce Enrile and General Fidel V\. Ramos, Art’s co\-Rotarian, who both had withdrawn their support from the dictator\.  

Epifanio de los Santos, which translates from Spanish to mean Highway of the Saints, became the stretch where people faced armed soldiers in tanks and full battle gear\. A cross section of society stood their ground for four days, sharing food and water donated by individuals, families, communities and companies who had hastily organized themselves in solidarity with the masses on the streets\. What made the situation astounding was the only arsenal of the unarmed people was prayer\. 

It was widely held that the societal change brought about by the EDSA revolution was an intervention of a power greater than human effort\. Despite the absence of reference to a divine involvement in the accounts of civil resistance building up to a nonviolent revolution, 

the astounding developments were attributed in different circles as a God\-action\. Sufficient historical accounts cover the February events of 1985 that led to the end of a 20\-year dictatorship and the restoration of democracy in the Philippines\. It is in the personal accounts of those who faced armored tanks and huddled together clutching rosaries and praying that the Presence of an Almighty God figured largely\. The Christian God is, after all, personal, possessing both supreme sovereignty over all history and nations while as much interested in the affairs of men as well as everyman\. 

It would not, therefore, be hard to imagine that what happened on the magnitude of a people’s collective life was also the experience of an individual\. His Presence, in Art’s life, though but an inkling through the years, would come to a dramatic turn which he himself did not foresee\. It was no less astonishing than a country’s bloodless revolution\. While 1986 would perpetually be inscribed in the annals of world history as the time of People Power triumph in the Philippines, it would also be forever etched in Art’s memory as the year of his own liberation from darkness to light\.  

He had been a spectator with a modicum of spiritual awareness as the events of history unfolded\. Phenomenal as it was, he viewed the happenings as the consequence of people’s actions, a confluence of a collective discontent with the system, political motives and aspirations, plus a bandwagon response\. He had not, as yet, been able to go beyond the mindset that man was the master of his own destiny\. 

__A Divine Appointment in Baguio  __	

It was a day in May on that same year when Raul and Aurora Paredes invited Art and Leni to join them for what they described as a weekend of rest and relaxation\. These friends knew about the Panganiban’s mid\-life marital problems\. The decline of Art’s law practice in the late 1970s, the slump of his Philippine businesses and the diminished circumstances of his board memberships in the Continental Bank, the Overseas Insurance Company and the Pentagon Mines, Inc\. had made him a difficult person to live with\. Preoccupied with achieving that quality of living afforded by the affluent and those deemed successful by the world’s standards, he looked for gainful options outside of the country and traveled extensively\. He stayed abroad for extended periods of time\. More than half of the year, he was either in Hong Kong, the United States or Europe\.

Initially, he met with success following investments in the Hong Kong stock market and in the rapid rise of Texas real estate\. Everything, however, came to an abrupt end when British Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher announced the cessation of Hong Kong to China and the sudden decline of the Mexican economy which resulted in the fall of Texas real estate prices\. Art lost everything\. 

Dejected and crestfallen, he returned to the Philippines and refocused efforts on his law practice and local businesses\. It was a time of mending, the scope of which would go beyond his professional life to significantly more than just financial recovery\. The prompt was that invitation from their friends for a weekend in Mirador Villa although years in search for meaning was the lead\-up to the point of their acceptance\.   

The Mirador Villa was originally a meteorological observatory where Jesuit scientists measured rainfall, observed winds and recorded the movements of the earth\. The facility on the second highest peak in Baguio City evolved over the years to become a summer resting place for Jesuit educators, then a scholasticate for young Jesuits, and later a conference and retreat venue\. It was here that Art and Leni found themselves as participants in a Marriage Encounter conducted by the Bukas Loob sa Diyos\. 

The trip up to the city of pines was not without challenges\. Less than an hour after leaving their residence in Quezon City, the car had a flat tire\. Art had to have it replaced with a spare\. He resumed driving but another tire blew out while they were going through Tarlac and it took  another hour to find a replacement\. When they finally reached their destination, they apologized profusely to their hosts who waved off their embarrassment with congratulations at having successfully overcome the obstacles\. They said that the couples attending the ME were usually beset with similar problems devised by the evil one to discourage them from getting to Mirador\. 

The ME was a program that originated in the United States in 1967\. It was developed by founder Charles Gallagher who conceptualized a weekend designed to provide married couples time away from distractions and tensions of everyday living and improve their communications as husband and wife\. The goal was to develop and enhance intimacy in their communications\.

 

__Healing the Breach__

Art and Leni were welcomed to the Marriage Encounter Class 26__ __together with 15 other couples\. Robust singing from the Praise Ministry of the BLD charismatic community welcomed them as they were ushered into the small rooms where they were to spend the next three days\. The men shared a common toilet and bath as the women similarly did\. There was a dining room and a little chapel where the Blessed Sacrament was exposed while the BLD Intercessory Ministry prayed continuously through 24 hours\. 

Members of the previous ME Class were also there, happily serving as cooks, wait staff, ushers, singers and prayer warriors\. Art and Leni were impressed by these volunteers, known as auxiliaries, who were prominent persons in their respective professions, businesses and government positions\. The auxiliaries attended to them as one would to honored guests of a King\. Also on hand were BLD couple\-elders who were unobtrusively supervising the entire weekend\.

There were no pedantic classroom lectures conducted during the ME\. Instead, Art and Leni listened to couples share about their own situations\. One had benign marital problems and another contended with serious issues that resulted in years long separation\. The latter were reconciled when they both came to a conversion to Jesus Christ during their ME\. The stories of both couples resonated with Art and Leni\. It became clear to them that having God in their life and their relationship was the only way they could resolve their difficulties\.

Fr\. Pascual Adorable, SJ, the spiritual director of the BLD, now deceased, followed through with powerful homilies\. Art described the talks of the priest, known as the lighting rod of God, as “penetrating deep into our body, soul and spirit, starting with the five senses and later gnawed into our very being overcoming us completely into the holy Presence of the Lord\. We identified with the sharers as they related their journey from pain to love and we associated their experiences with the passion and resurrection of our Lord, slowly in the beginning but overpowering in the end\.”

Antonio de los Reyes and his wife Barbara were one of the presenting couples for that ME weekend\. Their role was to share their experience in the ME and how it worked for them\.  Sonny and Bai, as they were known among friends, noticed the Panganiban couple\. 

They sat with Art and Leni during one meal and had a light and casual conversation\. They observed that the weekend seemed to have a positive effect on Art particularly\. He was receptive to the talks, interested in the activities and was participative\. By the end of the two\-day program, Bai invited them to join a Life in the Spirit Seminar conducted by the BLD\. This invitation was normally extended to those who had finished the ME\. 

Art and Leni had scheduled travels and were noncommittal\. They did agree, though, to pursue the prescribed exercise of writing to one another especially since they were making separate trips from each other\. They were enjoined to read the bible and share their reflections with one another\. When they returned from abroad, Sonny and Bai were happy to see that the notebooks of both Leni and Art were filled with handwritten notes\.  It was a good indication that they could take further steps into deepening their relationship\. 

The ME experience had convinced Art that God had truly brought Leni and him together to love each other forever despite their social, cultural, religious and economic gaps\. “We arrived on a Friday afternoon but by Sunday noon, my wife and I were miraculously transformed from a divided couple to a united whole, completely surrendering all cares and pains to our Lord,” he said\. “I was happy and confident that there was no problem so big that God could not solve\.” It was a significant development which prepared him for the follow through that was the Life in the Spirit seminar\. 

The Life in the Spirit Seminar was sponsored by the charismatic community Bukas Loob sa Diyos\. Fr\. Ado, as he was widely known, was active in the charismatic renewal movement when it first began in the Philippines\. Central to the movement was the enlivening of spiritual gifts through the power of the Holy Spirit\. Members of the movement met regularly in prayer meetings where the exercise of these spiritual gifts or charisms was encouraged\. The charisms had been part of the early church life since Pentecost and were acknowledged as a benefit to enrich the faith life of believers\. It saw a resurgence in the twentieth century through the charismatic renewal\. The worldwide movement was seen to restore the dynamism of the Christian way of life as Jesus Christ had taught it\.   

__Movement of the Holy Spirit__

The LSS was designed as an introductory course on living according to the gospel, which essentially meant becoming a follower of Christ\. The talks aimed to establish or deepen a personal relationship with Jesus\. In the course of the seminar, facilitators lay hands on the participants, similar to early church traditions, as an act of conferring a blessing\. They ask God to bestow the gift of the Holy Spirit on the person being prayed over\.

Praying for the blessing of God’s Spirit draws from the biblical account of Pentecost, when God poured out His Holy Spirit on the apostles, empowering them to live as Christ did\. The New Testament account dramatized the transformation of men cowered by fear and doubt into bold and confident followers of Jesus Christ\. It happened after the crucifixion of the Christ and His resurrection from the dead\.	

	

“When the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all together in one place\. Suddenly a sound like the blowing of a violent wind came and filled the house where they were sitting\. They saw what appeared to be tongues of fire that separated and came to rest on each of them\. All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them\. \(Acts 2:1ff, NIV \)\.”

This event on the day of Pentecost has been marked as the beginning of the Christian Church\. It was a turning point for the fledgling faith community who understood the occurrence as the fulfillment of an Old Testament prophecy\. The book of Joel declared that the Holy Spirit would be poured out on all people\. The account in the book of Acts 2:41 records that about 3,000 people joined the apostles on Pentecost as followers of Jesus Christ\. This was one of the strongest indications of the action of God’s Spirit at work in people’s lives\. 

The living reality of Jesus in the Spirit is central in the charismatic renewal, which takes its name from the Greek word charisma, meaning a gift\. This gift, described in the New Testament, involves everything God gives as grace through Jesus Christ, palpable through the ways that the Spirit manifests himself in the lives of people\.

__Personal Pentecost__

Art had sparse knowledge of the Catholic faith, much less the person of Jesus Christ\. When he and Leni joined the LSS, he listened to the talks with growing interest\. It was a two\-day immersion of teachings, reflections and prayers\. He began to confront the reality of a God who was personal and interested in Him, and, in fact, loved him\. The ideas of a distant and disinterested deity or the abstract of a force in the universe were being cancelled out by simple declarations of a loving God who cared for him\. He recognized a truth that was not determined by facts, logic or intellect\. It was ineffable but real\. 

He said, “While the ME was more cerebral and mental, the LSS was spiritual and soul\-piercing\. In the darkened LSS seminar hall, I closed my eyes but saw a clear vision of our risen Christ calling me as the choir sang ‘Here I am Lord\.’ Right there and then, I felt spiritual tongues of fire burning my sins and transforming me into a new creation in the love and image of our Lord\. And so I unconditionally answered, ‘Yes, Lord, here I am\. I will do whatever pleases you\.” This was his personal Pentecost, the starting point of a life in a totally different direction away from what he had known and valued\.

The process of conversion began with a deliberate and conscious response to an initiative extended by God\. Art heard with his heart a divine invitation to let go of control over his life and let God lead him\. What that meant in the particular was not defined but the choice to trust and believe quelled any dissension\. Yielding brought a sense of peace he had never known\. He was on a trajectory that would change the course of his life forever\.  

__Hungry for God__

Three weeks later, the Panganibans were back in Mirador, but this time as volunteers of ME class 26 attending to the next batch of couples\. What had been previously done for them, they were now doing for others, singing, serving and praying for the participants\. They shared their story of transformation in Christ\. 

They also became part of Bukas Loob sa Diyos  community, where they received help in weathering through the “cycle of  trial and triumph, of pain and pleasure, of passion and resurrection in more life\-changing, weekly prayer meetings and one\-to\-one shepherding\. Sonny and Bai took Art and Leni under their wing to help them develop and cultivate spiritual moorings\. This was called shepherding in their community’s parlance\.  

The two couples would meet every weekend in the Panganiban residence\. They normally began by having dinner together and then spent more time after in prayer and dialogue\. Sonny and Bai asked questions as a way to encourage them to share about their feelings and concerns, and talk about their differences\. Art was eager and willing to speak and listen, and sometimes the sessions extended till midnight\. 

Sonny and Bai began to notice changes in Art\. “He took to heart the shepherding sessions, the dialogues with Leni, the prayers and most of all the reading of the Word of God,” Bai observed\. “He saw the need for change in his character and his relationships\. Art acknowledged he was a sinner and needed God to save him from the things that held him captive\.” There still remained the lure of past pleasures and associations\. Bai recalled a comment that given time, Art could go back to his old life, especially his love for socializing and partying\. She conveyed the remark with the intent for him to be aware and safeguard against the possibility of backsliding\. Art smiled\. He was finding a way out his darkness and was resolute about staying the course\. 

He hungered for more of God and actively sought to immerse himself in the teachings of Jesus Christ\. It was at this time that he and Leni met Fr\. Gerry Santos, a newly\-ordained priest who would later become a monsignor\. Fr\. Gerry spent time teaching him basic catechism, sharing biblical stories and Papal Encyclicals\. “I read over 100 books on the Christian religion, mostly Catholic and some Protestant, like the 16 volumes of William Barclay’s ‘The Daily Study Bible’,” Art recounted\.

__Visible Turnaround __

Art and Leni became active in the Bukas Loob sa Diyos community and were given greater responsibilities as members of the council of servant leaders\. Besides this, they were frequently invited to speak about God’s work in their lives and had also been assigned to serve as prayer leaders\. These were expressions of gratitude to God and they did not shirk from opportunities to do more for Him\. Art and Leni were both designated to head the BLD Foundation and the BLD Treasury Ministry that raised funds for the construction of the towering Christ the Redeemer bronze statue in Mirador Hill, the place of their ME\. This and the planning and initial construction of a multi\-purpose retreat and conference facility in Dasmariñas, Cavite known as the Covenant House were two major undertakings during their watch\. These important projects would later be the subject of a coffee table book published by the BLD\.

Art’s newfound faith brought a visible metamorphosis\. Most significantly was the active sharing about God\. He explained that following the admission of transgression and a sincere turning away from what offends God, the personal encounter with Jesus necessarily leads to a profound transformation of one’s way of life to be in accord with the gospel\. 

During a prayer meeting of the BLD, he shared, “Once we are converted we begin to glow\. People begin to notice us\. People begin to see the change in our lifestyles, in our language, in our relationships, in our actions, in our habits\. Unconsciously we become witnesses of Christ\. We become evangelizers\.

“We cannot go to heaven alone\. We need to bring our family, our friends and our neighbors\. We are called to promote human development\. We are tasked to break the yoke of the poor, fight injustice, liberate man from bigotry and work for peace\.”  

Art had already been engaged in standing up for the marginalized as a student leader\. The difference between his crusade then and after his conversion was the sense of purpose had gone beyond helping others with his own limited capabilities, which fed both ego and self, to following God’s lead, putting his strength and resources at the disposal of God\. It was a surrendering of his capacities and his will to the will of God\.

__Life Ignited__

Pope Francis, in his Angelus message on March 22, 2020, reflected on the process from repentance, conversion and evangelization which Art was experiencing\. “…it is not enough to receive the light, one must become light\. Each one of us is called to receive the divine light in order to manifest it with our whole life…” 

In a talk he later delivered before bishops, priests, lay leaders and non\-Catholics, Art recounted, “The turning point of my life was when I proclaimed Jesus Christ as my Lord and Savior\. My purpose in life is to obey and glorify Him\. I realized that though I was in the world, I did not belong to this world\. I found purpose in my life and became a brand\-new creation of the Lord in the Holy Spirit\.

“It is said that life in Christ, or more precisely, life in imitation of Christ, is truly a recurring cycle of passion, death and resurrection\. I experienced my passion in my long search for the meaning and purpose of life\. I had to die to my own pride, my own private ambitions, to my own human aspirations to be able to live and to resurrect into a life on high with Jesus Christ\.”

He became a sought\-after speaker, invited to share about his faith life\. It was a powerful testimony of a life transformed from pursuit of the world to a preoccupation of things and activities spiritual\. He read the Bible cover to cover many times over, went through volumes of commentaries, researched on the encyclicals and teachings of the Holy Father\. The desire to follow Jesus was all\-encompassing, directing the practice of his law profession and his businesses, affecting his home life, leisure and lifestyle\. Scriptures became his guide to living\.

He declared in one of his talks, “Since my baptism in the Spirit in 1986, I surrendered to the Lord all my ambitions and aspirations\. I laid up before him all my man\-made plans and projects\.\.\.\.

“I was a sinner and I am still a sinner, who is thoroughly unworthy of God’s blessings\. As I contemplate my sinfulness in God’s mighty presence, I look up and see only the compassionate and merciful eyes of Jesus\. In complete faith, I lift up and abandon everything I am and I have before Him, fully confident of His unfailing love for me and asking the Son of God to enfold me\.”

There were many occasions when his faith was put to the test\. The challenges of aligning with God were part of the ongoing pruning of his character and purging of years long allegiance to a world opposed to Christian living\. Every setback, obstacle or difficulty was a point of choice to forge ahead or revert to where he had been before falling in love with Christ\. Each was crucial in the preparation for greater things to come\.
