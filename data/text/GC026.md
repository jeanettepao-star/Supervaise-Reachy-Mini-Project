__                                           Chapter 6__

*“Without counsel plans go awry, But in the multitude of counselors they are established\.” *__*Proverbs 15:22*__

The Lawyer

The world was beginning to look brighter for young Art as developments transpired towards an upward turn\. Graduating from law school with Latin honors, placing among the top 10 in the bar exams, marrying a gem of a woman–it seemed he was finally navigating towards the fulfilment of his aspirations for a better life\. Men on the trajectory to realizing their dreams, however, may find themselves racing ahead without the benefit of pause and reflection\. Pondering is necessary to understand that not everything is a result of one’s efforts\. It opens the possibility of seeing that there could be so much more beyond fulfilling personal ambitions to attain greater than what is imaginable\.

Art now owned his father’s dream, but Temi was no longer around to provide perspective or give him direction\. The newly minted lawyer, however, looked up to certain legal luminaries to whom history would later ascribe places of honor\. They became guideposts whom he sought to emulate\. Foremost among them was Dean Jovito R\. Salonga and,

fortuitously, Art had come under his mentorship while he was still a law student\.

Following graduation from the FEU, Art was heady over finishing in the top 10\. He was done with the backbreaking work of law school and could finally put the four grueling years behind him\. While he was grateful, not just for the academic education but also for the training and character formation, it had been difficult, challenging and oftentimes exhausting\. Relief and jubilation, however, were followed by apprehension over what to do next\. Tempering the initial euphoria was the uncertainty of the future\. Many opportunities for success lay before him but he was well aware that these could also spell failure\. He had to make careful and deliberate choices\.

# Aborted Dream

Dr\. Salonga recognized Art’s potential and encouraged him to prioritize his academic studies\. The Dean of the Law Institute recommended that the bar placer pursue further studies and endorsed him for a master’s degree scholarship\. Salonga had the unique distinction of having gone to both Harvard and Yale\. The former awarded him an LL\.M\. degree and the latter a doctorate in law\.

The law dean explained the difference between the approaches 

of the two top law schools\. Compared to Yale, Harvard Law School was reputed to be inhospitable\. Faculty members would tell new students to look at the persons beside them because one or the other would no longer be there by Thanksgiving\. Yale, on the other hand, kept the classes small and made every effort to make students feel at home\. “If a lawyer wants to argue a case before the court from the viewpoint of law and jurisprudence, Harvard is an excellent law school,” Salonga said\. “But from the viewpoint of the policy\-oriented approach of Yale, a lawyer is not just an advocate who specializes in rules and doctrines for the sake of his clients\. He should go beyond these and do what would serve the interests of the nation and the world community\.” Art was ignited by the Yale approach and upon the recommendation of Dean Salonga, applied and was awarded a scholarship in the Ivy League school\. The young lawyer never imagined such a door of opportunity opening up for him\. Hope and ambition surged in his heart and he was beside himself, imagining life in the third oldest institution of learning in the US\. It would be a prestigious addition to his resume\.

However, there was the matter of securing money to pay for the trip\. While board, lodging and tuition were included in the scholarship, plane fare was not part of the arrangement\. He pinned his hopes on a

Fulbright/Smith\-Mundt travel grant from the United States embassy in Manila\. The US government appropriated dollars to support educational exchange and Art hoped to avail himself of the money for a ticket to America\. It was the only thing that stood between him and the realization of a dream he hadn’t even fantasized about\. He submitted his application with eager anticipation and was devastated when the application was denied because of his activist streak as a student leader\. It was a crushing blow\. Once again, he felt the pain of being financially disadvantaged\. Frustration and anger welled up within\. A sense of helplessness replaced the elation and excitement\. He had no access to funds needed for the flight\. It seemed the world was bearing down on him\.

Once again Dr\. Salonga provided a lifeline\. He consoled the dejected lawyer by extending an invitation to be part of his highly regarded Salonga, Ordoñez , Sicat and Associates law office\. “I will thus teach you what I learned from the University of the Philippines, Yale and Harvard\.”

# The Salonga Tutelage

Art accepted the offer and came under the wing of the man he would later refer to as his guru\. He continued his education and

hands\-on training\. “Through him, I had the best of four educational worlds: FEU, UP, Harvard and Yale\.”

The law office was a powerhouse that counted the likes of Sedfrey A\. Ordonez, Pedro L\. Yap and Neptali Gonzales\. Ordoñez would later be appointed Secretary of Justice in 1987 by President Corazon C\. Aquino\. Yap would thereafter be named Chief Justice of the Supreme Court in 1988\. And Gonzales would go on to be elected President of the Philippine Senate from 1992 to 1993, 1995 to 1996, and again in 1998\. Salonga himself would hold the position of Senate President from 1987 to 1992\. With them working together, their law firm flourished and they counted distinguished names among their clientele\.

# Eminent Influencer

Art earned his spurs as a junior associate of the law firm from 1961 to 1963\. The exposure and proximity to legal luminaries served to expand his experience and depth of knowledge, but beyond the practice of law, he drew from Dr\. Salonga life lessons that would form his character\.

Dr\. Salonga effectively became his surrogate father, imparting life\-defining values of integrity, prudence and fairness\. The example of

his life offered lessons that Art consciously and subliminally absorbed\. The young man saw and described him as a dedicated husband and caring father, a faithful friend and a devoted man of God who did not smoke or drink or gamble\. A well\-regarded leader of the Cosmopolitan Church, Dr\. Salonga lived a simple, almost ascetic life\. Wealth, power, worldly allurements, titles and honors did not fascinate him\.

Art saw him as a model for how to live meaningfully\. His former dean, professor, senior partner, mentor and guru would continue to be a strong presence throughout his career and his life\. Decades later, a very accomplished Art would repeatedly speak about this man’s impact on his values, demeanor and outlook\. During the eulogy for Dr\. Salonga, which he delivered on March 14, 2016, Art said, “I can never adequately acknowledge the pervading influence of former Senate President Jovito R\. Salonga in my life\.” He mentioned the different roles and life lessons imbibed through the years\. “There were countless, undeserved favors he had given me in the course of almost six decades since he was my dean at the FEU Institute of Law and my boss when I was a struggling associate in his prestigious law firm\. He taught me not only the rudiments of the philosophy and practice of the noble profession of law but also the more life\-moving virtues of integrity, prudence, fairness and temperance\. From him I learned that law is not a mere abstract or

syllogism separate from the social milieu\. Experience, not logic, should be the life of the law\. It should be used as a brick in building the social structure and as a means of fulfilling the deepest aspirations of the people\.

“But his foremost teaching is not really about law as it is about life\.

And if my presence today in these necrological rites must have any relevance at all, I would like to sum up now the one teaching of my guru which I will always treasure, and it is this: While it is desirable to have the many necessities that money can buy, like adequate food on the table, sufficient clothing and shelter for the family, sports and recreation for the body and even a car and a house, never ever forget that it is far more important to aspire for, imbibe and live by the values that money cannot buy, like excellence, integrity, honor, dignity, and a reverential regard and love for God as the Source of all that is true, good and beautiful\.”

# The Understudy to Greatness

Another lawyer whom Art also admired was Jose W\. Diokno, regarded as the “Father of Human Rights Advocacy in the Philippines\.” Diokno became Secretary of Justice in 1962 before getting elected to the

Philippine Senate in 1963\. There was one occasion when Art, as Salonga’s briefcase carrier, had to face the formidable Diokno in court\. Salonga was unwell and telephoned Art to be at the Court of First Instance in his stead\. He instructed his junior assistant to be at the hearing presided by Judge Angel H\. Mojica, who had been Art’s professor\. His boss explicitly told him not to ask for a postponement since Salonga had promised the judge there would be no deferment of the proceedings\. It meant that Art was coming up against Diokno, counsel of the plaintiff\.

The confidence of his mentor notwithstanding, the prospect of sparring with a legendary attorney was intimidating\. While he had prepared the case for Dr\. Salonga, his knowledge of the inheritance case was limited and he felt grossly unprepared\. Still, he went to court that morning, an exercise in obedience, and picked up another significant learning\. When the case was called, he gallantly stood up and mumbled, “Ready for the defense, Your Honor\.”

Judge Mojica looked askance at him and inquired where the venerable Attorney Salonga was\. The magistrate sat behind his desk on an elevated platform, eyeing his former student quizzically\. He asked, “Young man, are you ready to continue the cross\-examination of the plaintiff?” Art pulled himself up with as much composure as he

could muster and declared he was ready for the defense\. Nervously he explained that Atty\. Salonga had called early that morning to convey he was unwell but had instructed him not to ask for a postponement because of a standing agreement not to delay the case\.

The presiding justice turned to Diokno who had been observing Salonga’s junior assistant\. “What do you say, counsel?” Mojica asked\. The petitioner’s jurisprudent–who had never completed his degree in law owing to the war but was later given permission by the Supreme Court to take the qualifying tests and went on to top the 1944 Philippine bar examinations–had already assessed an easy victory as did Art and Judge Mojica\. Nonetheless, he did something totally unexpected and declared, “Your Honor, it is true we agreed not to defer today’s hearing but in fairness to my young *compañero *and knowing that Atty\. Salonga would not tell a lie about his health, I myself am now moving to postpone the hearing\. Anyway, this case is scheduled to be heard two more times in the next two weeks\.” Mojica reverted to Art and asked, “What do you say to that motion, counsel?” Recovering quickly from his shock, the defendant’s lawyer replied, “No objections, your honor\.”

He was stunned, relieved and grateful\. Diokno now loomed even higher in his esteem\. The man, who in his mind was a heavyweight

boxer, had chosen not to take advantage of an amateur flyweight\. Art had been given a lifeline\. He felt that by requesting a postponement, the senior lawyer showed both graciousness and humility\. “He did not belittle me but instead gave me respect,” Art reflected\. “This may have been a little incident in the life of the legendary Atty\. Jose W\. Diokno, but it was a major one in mine\. It showed how a great advocate could demonstrate fairness to a young, inexperienced lawyer who hardly knew the pending case\. I would probably not have been able to cross\-examine the plaintiff adequately\.

“Atty\. Diokno earned my immediate admiration and awe, not only for his vaunted competence but also for his gallant sportsmanship in refusing to take undue advantage of a young, nervous lawyer\. I am certain that Judge Mojica was just as impressed\.”

# Libertarian Teehankee

Besides Diokno, Art hero\-worshipped another legal luminary, Atty\. Claudio Teehankee, the astute jurist who went on to become the 16th Chief Justice of the Supreme Court\. Teehankee graduated summa cum laude in his preparatory law studies at the Ateneo de Manila in 1938, repeated the feat with an LL\.B summa cum laude two years later,

also at the Ateneo, and topped the bar examinations that same year\. He applied his extensive knowledge to the technicalities of the law, its reach, consequences and limitations, not just as a legal technician serving the causes and interests of his clients but as one interpreting it to fulfill the ultimate ends of justice\. Art described him as a social engineer who saw the law as an instrument to build a just society\. “He reached for the stars to render justice via the all\-encompassing rule of law\.”

Salonga himself looked up to Teehankee\. When they had cases where they had to contend with him, Salonga would tell his young associate, “You better prepare our trial brief very well because we are going to be up against one of the best lawyers in town,” referring to Teehankee\.

Claudio Teehankee was known to stand for his convictions and uphold his beliefs against all odds\. He stood for liberty and pioneered the libertarian philosophy in the Philippines\. He strongly believed in autonomy and political freedom and maintained his position even at the cost of his personal safety and that of his family\.

For Art, the example of these legendary practitioners of the law contributed not just to setting the standard for his own practice but just as much to the formation of his belief

Independence, integrity, industry and intelligence would become the hallmarks of his judicial career\.
