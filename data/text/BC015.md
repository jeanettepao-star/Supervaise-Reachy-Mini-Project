## Be Not Afraid

In the gospel reading last Sunday, February 5, 1995, the Evangelist Luke tells us that Jesus was preaching to the crowd from a fishing boat belonging to Simon Peter. When he finished speaking, he noticed that the boat was empty of any catch. So, he told Simon Peter, “Bring the boat into deeper water and lower your nets for a catch.” To this, Peter responded, “Master, we have been hard at it all night long and caught nothing: but if you say so, I will lower the nets.

Peter’s reply must have been a mixture of frustration and weariness. He and his fellow fishermen had searched the sea all night and caught nothing. At the time, fishing was normally done at night. Peter must have told himself, “What is the point of fishing by day when we caught nothing at night?” But Peter, though tired and sleepless, had faith in the Master. So he said, “If you say so, I lower the nets.”

Although the fishermen were weary, although Jesus was not an expert in fishing, although it was obvious that daylight was the wrong time for fishing, and although he knew as a fact that there were no fish around, Peter and his fishermen - in obedience - lowered their nets. It was purely out of faith and out of trust in Jesus that they did so. Peter set aside his own human knowledge, experience, instinct and "better" judgment and simply obeyed and trusted the Master. 

**Faith and Obedience Are Indispensable **

In our own journey with Jesus, faith and obedience are indispensable. "Of what use are our efforts, our human resources, our wakeful vigils or even our mortifications if they are separated from any supernatural sense? Without obedience, everything is useless in God's eyes. It would be of no use starting to work if we did not intend to count on our Lord. Even our most worthwhile endeavors would be fruitless if we did not have the desire to carry out God's will." (Fr. Francis Fernandez, On Conversation with God, Vol. Three, p. 235) As St. Crysostom teaches, "God does not need our work. He needs our obedience." 

Because Peter obeyed, the Lord rewarded him. In fact, he and his men caught so many fish that their two boats were filled to almost sinking capacity.

The miracle related in this story tells us of our own uselessness and of the utter necessity of putting our total trust in the Lord. In Him and Him alone. "This miracle shows a great clarity: that God will make possible what for man is impossible. Like Peter, we should all throw ourselves at the feet of Jesus and tell Him, 'Depart from me, Lord, for I am a sinful man? This is our way of acknowledging the loftiness of Christ’s dignity and our own wretchedness and inability to carry out our mission without Him.” Then our Lord will take away our fear and will reveal to us, like Peter, the real purpose of our life: "Do not be afraid; from now on you will be catching me." 

In jest, may I relate a translation joke, if only to emphasize the need to banish fear. A politician actor was before a cheering crowd when he addressed them in all seriousness. "Mga kapatid, mga kababayan. Ito po ang aking samo. Huwag kayong matatakot. Tibayan ninyo ang inyong dibdib. Sa haba ng panahon, magtatagumpay rin tayo. Uulitin ko po: Huwag kayong matatakot. Tibayan ninyo ang inyong dibdib. Sa haba ng panahon magtatagumpay rin tayo. Iingglesin ko po para tayo magkaintindihan: Do not afraid. Strengthen your inside. For in the long of time, we will success!" 

**Be Not Afraid Has Biblical Roots **

Brothers and sisters, that may have been a joke. But it will serve to remind us in all seriousness to banish all fear in our lives. 

"Be not afraid." This is the same exhortation that Pope John Paul II resounded as the theme of his ministry in the See of St. Peter. He repeated this message several times during his last visit to the Philippines. Indeed, "Be not afraid" is mentioned many times in the Scriptures. When the angel Gabriel appeared to Mary to tell her of her impending pregnancy, he said to her: "Be not afraid." (Lk. 1:30) The same truth was proclaimed to Joseph when, upon learning of Mary's pregnancy, he decided to separate from her. The angel solemnly advised him, "Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary your wife into your home. For it is through the Holy Spirit that this child has been conceived in her." (Mt. 1:20) In other parts of the Scriptures, Ch ng exhorted His disciples with the same message rist various circumstances. He kept telling them, "Be not afraid." This was because the apostles were full of fear when Jesus was arrested , when he died and even more so after His resurrection. 

In his book Crossing the Threshold of Hope (1994) our Supreme Pontiff tells us to put our faith in Jesus and not to be afraid of what men may say or do. He writes (p.219):

Have no fear of that which you yourselves have created, have no fear of all that man has produced, and that every day is becoming more dangerous for him. Finally, have no fear of yourselves. Why should we have no fear? Because man was redeemed by God. From pronouncing these words from St. Peter's square, I already knew that my first encyclical and my entire papacy would be tied to the truth of the redemption. In the redemption we find the most profound basis of the words "Be not afraid." 

… The power of Christ's Cross and Resurrection is greater than any evil which man could or should fear. 

**Be Not Afraid to Be Holy **

"Be not afraid." Place your complete faith and unbending obedience in the Lord, and He will make all things possible. This is the unmistakable message of the Sacred Book. "Be not afraid." This is the resounding cry of John Paul II. "Be not afraid." This is also the Lord's special Word for us today. From this word, there are three directions to each of which I shall address the reflections I want to share with you tonight. 

First, do not be afraid to be holy, to be different, to lead a personal life of chastity, honesty and obedience to God's commandments and beatitudes. In concluding His sermon on the mount, Jesus Christ exhorted us: "Be perfect, just as Your heavenly Father is perfect." (Mt.5:48) By this message of Christ, Christians are called to be holy. The 1987 Synod of Bishops stated that through baptism "a person is called to a life of holiness." That is why in BLD (Bukas Loob sa Diyos), holiness is the first and foremost community value. 

It is easy to think of the Pope as holy. That is why over four million people came to see him. It is easy to think of a bishop, of a priest or of a nun as holy. However, is it equally as easy to call Raffy or Willy or Josie holy? You would probably say so. This is because holiness is usually associated with men and women of the cloth. 

But it should not be so. As the Bible, the Second Vatican Council and the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines proclaim, the call to holiness is universal; it is for everyone. 

Many people look at holiness in terms of piety only. Holiness is more than that. In his book, The Filipino Laity: Called to Be Holy (1988), Bishop Teodoro Bacani, Jr. (on page 97) defines holiness as "simply intimate union with God by loving Him above all things and by loving our fellowmen as ourselves. Hence, the holy person is not necessarily the one who does many acts of piety but the loving person." Bishop Bacani continues (page 98): 

The general rule is: what one is obliged by one's state of life to do is certainly God's will, and the performance of it helps us to grow in holiness. The important thing is to desist from identifying growth in holiness with the performance of acts of piety, and (instead) to identify growth in holiness with the loving accomplishment of whatever it is God wills for me here and now. . . . We grow in holiness through the loving performance of our ordinary tasks and obligations, and through seizing the many and oftentimes monotonous opportunities of doing good to others. 

Hence, our way to holiness consists in doing our assigned tasks in the way God wants us to do them: honestly, effectively and productively. "One grows in holiness as one tries to be a good father or mother, a businessman or a politician, a student or a laborer, a farmer or a fisherman." (Cf. Bacani, op.cit., p.99)

**Holiness Is Fulfilling Our Commitments**

In BLD, our way to holiness is doing what we have committed ourselves to do, like faithfully following The our eight-point covenant, or serving as auxiliaries during ME (Marriage Encounters) or as shepherds hop during LSS (Life in the Spirit Seminars) or simply faithfully attending and actively participating in our prayer meetings.

Mother Teresa was once asked whether she was embarrassed to hear people calling her a living saint. She replied, "What is so extraordinary about that? Are we not all called to holiness?" 

Yes, we are called to holiness — lay people as well as bishops, priests and nuns, charismatics or Opus Dei members, Cursillistas or Focolares. Remember that the first Filipino Catholic saint was a lay person: San Lorenzo Ruiz. 

Brothers and sisters, it is said that our transportation vehicle in heaven is determined by the depth of our holiness on earth. Three friends — Juan, Paolo and Segundo - knocked at the pearly gates one day, and they were asked by St. Peter, "How holy were you as husbands?" 

This was Juan's reply: "I was a teacher and I was a very good husband. I had eyes only for my dear wife whom I served and loved faithfully." 

St. Peter responded, "Excellent. Excellent. I will reward you with a brand-new Mercedes Benz so that you can travel around heaven in style." 

When his turn came, Paolo said, "I was a lawyer. And to be honest, I had a few flings but I always went home to my wife to whom I always denied my unfaithfulness." 

"Well," St. Peter answered, "I will give you the benefit of the doubt for your honesty this time. So I will give you a jeep.”

The third caller placed his arms around the shoulders of St. Peter, and with moist eyes said, "Pete, I was a politician. Since I had to get as many votes as I could, I kept a wife and several mistresses. But I provided abundantly for all of them and their relatives. And they were all happy."

St. Peter replied: "Pati ba naman ako binobola mo pa? Basta, bisikleta lang ang wheels mo rito." 

The three friends merrily went their separate ways traveling in their assigned vehicles. One day they met again. The last two were surprised that the Mercedes-riding Juan was sobbing. "Why are you so sad when you travel around heaven in a Chedeng? they asked. 

Juan lifted his dejected face and tearfully said, "I am not complaining about my Mercedes. I am sad because I just saw my wife merrily moving about in a bicycle!" 

**Be Not Afraid to Go into Mission**

Our second direction tonight is "Be not afraid to go into mission." During his visit, Pope John Paul II repeatedly exclaimed: "As the Father has sent me, so I send you!" And our response is Isaia's unconditional acceptance in the second reading: "Here I am Lord. Send me." 

The Vicar of Christ looks to the Philippines and the fifty-five million Catholic Filipinos to lead the drive for the "new evangelization" of Asia. The world is now populated by 5 billion people but only 1 billion are Catholics. China and India alone have about 2 billion people who do not know Christ. Add Japan, Indonesia and the rest of Asia, and you have about 2.5 billion non-Catholics, about half of the world's population. US President Bill Clinton said that the Asia-Pacific area will be the center of the highest economic growth rate in the world in the coming millennium. Pope John Paul II says that this same region will witness the most miraculous movement of the Holy Spirit in fulfilling the mission that Christ entrusted to His believers: "Go the ref ore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always until the end of the age." (Mt. 28:19-20) 

This command of the Lord is echoed by the Second Plenary Council of Philippines (PCP II). In words of the PCP II President, Archbishop Leonardo Z. Legaspi in his "Message of the Council to the People of God in the Philippines" (Feb. 17, 1991): 

The Council's thrust is toward a New Evangelization, based not on human expectations and wisdom but on the preaching of Christ crucified to today's Filipinos and Asians. 

But you might say that these are big words and big plans for Asia. What can we do in BLD? Well, I say we have already started this evangelization even ahead of the Pope's visit. For two years, from 1992 to 1993, we prayed and discerned our new mission. And in 1994 we announced this mission as follows:

**Mission **

The Lord calls us to be servants of the pilgrim Church in its efforts at renewed evangelization through the following: 

Restoring the Filipino family in Christ

Forming basic communities of faith (Basic Ecclesial Communities or BECs)

Strengthening the Filipino parish

Breaking the yokes of poverty and oppression

Raising an army of prayer warriors for the Church and for national renewal

BLD looks outward to the larger Philippine Church. And it is asking its members to participate actively in restoring the family, in strengthening parishes, in forming an army of prayer warriors and in breaking the yokes of poverty and oppression in our land.

Brothers and sisters, be not afraid to undertake this mission of BLD. For we have the Lord's promise: "I am with you always until the end of this age." 

**Be Not Afraid of Failure **

Our third direction tonight is "Be not afraid of failure." Many times we are overwhelmed by the magnitude of our mission. We are awed by the difficulties we perceive before us. 

For example, some of our members are truly impressed by our plans for the Covenant House. At the same time they are awed by its this magnitude, its size and its cost. They ask: Why are we attempting to build the largest retreat house in the country? Can we really raise enough funds — over P60 million — to construct it? 

As chairman of the BLD Foundation, I have this stock answer: "You know, BLD has been discerning this project since 1988. It has received various prophecies on it. These prophecies have been confirmed in our discernment sessions. We know in faith that our Lord wants us to build this House. His House. Since He wants it built, He will provide us with the means and funds for it. He will use our lips, our hands, our businesses, our professions as conduits to provide the funds and the means. The Lord does not directly hand out money or land. He uses His faithful ones as His instruments to implement His will. Brothers and sisters, the Lord wants us to build the Covenant House. Amen? Be not afraid! It will be built. It will be done. For our God is with us."

**If You Say So, Lord, We Will Have the Victory **

Yes, brothers and sisters, be not afraid of failure. Whatever we do, be it in our home, in our office or in our community, we first pray and discern. Then, we ask for confirmation of our discernment . Then and only then do we go forward boldly and bravely. Never mind the obstacles; never mind the problems. Truly, difficulties there will always be. Our Lord never promised us an easy life. He has sufficiently warned us of persecutions and trials. But we believe that for every problem, the Lord will provide a solution. For every trial, a victory. For every death, a resurrection. This is our faith. And on this faith, we place our trust. 

Like Peter, we may be weary, tired and frustrated. Our human experiences, instincts and wisdom may tell us otherwise. But like Peter we say, "If you say so, Lord, we will lower the nets. If You say so, Lord, we will build Your Covenant House. If You say so, Lord, we will not fear. If You say so, Lord, we will have victory." 

Praise to You, Lord Jesus Christ.
