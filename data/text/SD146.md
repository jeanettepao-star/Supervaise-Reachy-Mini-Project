LIGHTING THE WAY

Date:       2006-10-12

Publisher:  cjpanganiban.com

Source:     https://cjpanganiban.com/lighting-the-way/

Keynote address delivered by Chief Justice Artemio V. Panganiban during the Philippine Judges Association Convention on October 12, 2006, at the Holiday Inn, Mimosa Drive, Mimosa Leisure Estate in Clark, Pampanga.

---

Magandang umaga po sa inyong lahat. It seems my coming home to Pampanga has been oftener in the past few months than it has been collectively in the past two decades. I am back in Pampanga to grace your convention.

TAKING STOCK

Time flies so swiftly indeed! It seems just yesterday when I took my oath as the 21st Chief Justice, during which I vowed to lead a judiciary characterized by four Ins: independence, integrity, industry and intelligence; one that battles the ACID problems of limited access to justice by the poor, corruption, incompetence, and delay in the delivery of quality judgments; and one that strives to achieve the two lofty goals of safeguarding the liberty and nurturing the prosperity of our people.

ACCESS-TO-JUSTICE INITIATIVES

To widen access to justice by the poor, the Supreme Court has amended some portions of the Rules of Court. An important new project that has proved successful in making courts more accessible to the poor is the Justice on Wheels program. The first mobile court, which served as venue for proceedings of Family Court cases in the National Capital Region, was deployed on December 21, 2004. This large bus — staffed by a judge, a prosecutor, and a stenographer, among others — was designed to bring the courts to places where they would otherwise be inaccessible. Because of its success, the Court has decided to acquire two more buses, one for use in the Visayas and the other for Mindanao.

SOLUTIONS TO CORRUPTION AND INCOMPETENCE

The main solutions adopted to combat corruption and incompetence are strict entrance and promotion requirements in the judiciary, continuing ethical education, and improvement of the compensation of judges. The compensation of our judges will have been doubled, with the full implementation of Republic Act 9227.

DELIVERY OF QUALITY JUDGMENTS

Along with corruption, delay in the delivery of justice is the most watched area of reform. I immediately created, upon assuming office as Chief Justice, a Committee on Zero Backlog. The percentage of old cases assigned to the three Divisions of the Supreme Court has drastically gone down by 48.02 per cent.

TRIAL JUDGES IN THE FOREFRONT OF REFORMS

The foregoing projects and activities need to be transposed to the level of our trial courts. The steps taken to address the ACID problems would not amount to much without the involvement of all the members of the bench, particularly those in the front line like all of you. The Supreme Court may be the highest court of the land, but to the vast majority of our people, it is distant and far removed from their daily lives. Insofar as the great masses are concerned, justice is represented and dispensed by you, the trial judges.

CLOSING

Let me end with a little story. There was once a man with three children who built a large and profitable business empire. He gave each of his children P1,000 and tasked them to fill the room with anything they thought was proper. The first filled it with a big tree that did not quite cover the room. The second filled it with hay that also did not fill the room. The third bought candles and matches. At nightfall, he lit the candles one by one, and slowly the room was filled with light. At the sight of this, the father exclaimed: This is the one who will lead.

Indeed, sometimes it takes so little to accomplish so much of a gargantuan task. I urge all of you to take courage in lighting your own little candles of reform in your respective courts and together, we shall light up the firmament of justice in our country.

Maraming salamat po.
