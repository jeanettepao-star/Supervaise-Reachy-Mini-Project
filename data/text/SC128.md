Speech delivered by retired Chief Justice ARTEMIO V. PANGANIBAN during the launching of Secretary Jose T. Almonte's book, We Must Level the Playing Field, held at the Club Filipino, San Juan, Metro Manila on August 16, 2007.

---

Before I proceed to the main topic assigned to me today, allow me to specially greet President Fidel Valdez Ramos, our special guest today. May I publicly say maraming salamat po, President FVR, for initiating me into public service and for appointing me to the Supreme Court on a Double Ten, October 10, 1995.

PRESIDENT FVR AND THE SUPREME COURT

I first met President Ramos in 1983, when we both became new members of the Rotary Club of Manila. On June 17, 1992, after his electoral victory was already assured, he invited me to an early morning one-on-one meeting in his private office. After exchanging views on many topics, he offered me a seat in his Cabinet as justice secretary. I immediately declined, saying that I had neither campaigned for him nor supported his candidacy. I said that I had voted for my mentor, Senator Jovito R. Salonga, during the presidential elections of 1992. But because I had reservations about accepting a political office, President Ramos modified his offer to a seat in the Supreme Court. Though I owe my appointment to the Supreme Court solely and completely to him, he never spoke to me about any case or matter pending in the Court since then. Such is the gentleman that he is. Mabuhay po kayo, President FVR.

Enter National Security Adviser Jose T. Almonte

I met Secretary Jose T. Almonte, or JoeAl as he is fondly called, a few days after that encounter I had with President Ramos on June 17, 1992. Like many people who did not know JoeAl, I was initially wary of him. Because of his intelligence background, I thought of him as some sort of James Bond and Napoleon Solo combined. Behind the stony facade, however, is a very kind person, gentle of spirit and loyal to a fault to his true friends. He smiles pleasantly, laughs heartily, speaks deliberately, and listens intently.

In time, I gradually eased into his real persona as a true-blue ideologue, thinker, philosopher, strategist, reformer, visionary and patriot. When the topic was about reforms needed to propel the country to prosperity, he carefully chose his words to express the exact nuance of his thoughts. He preferred to dwell on goals and visions, principles and values, all geared towards the long-term needs of the nation.

After I joined the Supreme Court in 1995, I had to stop attending the Wednesday meetings for ethical and other reasons. But JoeAl's insights left their indelible mark in my mind. His commonsensical theories strengthened my own belief that law could not be read in a vacuum. It must be interpreted and applied in the context of pulsating contemporary social and economic realities. The rule of law must not only safeguard liberty but must help eradicate poverty and nurture prosperity.

THE CENTRAL THEME OF JOEAL****'****S BOOK

The book's title, We Must Level the Playing Field, summarizes the author's ideology. To explain the central theme, let me quote JoeAl directly: To level the playing field of enterprise, we must cut the networks of collusion that have allowed persons of influence to extract wealth without effort from the economy. And we must smooth the economic distortions that perpetuate jobless growth and uneven development. As far as possible, every one should have a fair and equal chance to pursue the possibilities of their lives.

Like most social reformers, JoeAl has a special place in his heart for the poor and the weak. However, instead of dole-outs and charity, he posits that the poor can be lifted up only by economic growth that creates and expands jobs and business opportunities for all those willing to take them.

CONCLUSION

Ladies and gentlemen, this new book entitled We Must Level the Playing Field confirms what all of us in this august gathering already knew: President Fidel V. Ramos may have been the embodiment of Philippines 2000 but National Security Adviser Jose T. Almonte was the sentient soul of the Ramos Presidency.

Maraming salamat po.
