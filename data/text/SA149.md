UNDP STUDY ON ACCESS TO JUSTICE

According to the United Nations Development Programme, access to justice is the ability of people to seek and obtain a remedy through formal or informal institutions of justice, and in conformity with human rights standards.

In the Philippines, a survey of private legal practitioners was conducted in 2004 to monitor access to justice by the disadvantaged. It reported some disturbing findings. Lawyering for the poor had an urban bias. Courts were also located mostly in urban centers. For the rural poor, the physical inaccessibility of the courts posed difficulties in access to justice. Loss of income appeared to be the strongest deterrent to lawyering for the poor, especially among young and new lawyers.

ACCESS TO JUSTICE AND POVERTY REDUCTION

The UNDP observes that access to justice is essential to poverty eradication and human development. Groups such as the poor and disadvantaged who suffer from discrimination also often fall victim to criminal and illegal acts. Crime and illegality are likely to have a greater impact on poor and disadvantaged people's lives, as it is harder for them to seek redress.

ACCESS TO JUSTICE PROJECTS

To implement the pro-poor constitutional provisions, the Supreme Court has opened the justice system to indigents through various measures. It has exempted poor litigants from the payment of docket and other fees; protected and enforced the constitutional rights of the accused; and granted free legal counsel to poor litigants.

To increase the accessibility of the judicial system, especially to the poor and the disadvantaged, the Supreme Court launched its Justice on Wheels program on December 21, 2004. The mobile courts are designed to bring the courts to places where they would otherwise be inaccessible.

The Supreme Court also conducts pro-poor, pro-labor and pro-human rights decisions. In Ang Bagong Bayani-OFW Labor Party v. Commission on Elections, it held that the Filipino-style party-list system is reserved for the poor and the marginalized.
